# Mythroot Prehistoric World — Climate Overlay v0.1

**Status:** Broad working pass. Nothing here is locked canon unless explicitly approved.

## Planetary inputs
- 32-hour rotation
- ~26° axial tilt
- ~214 Earth-day year
- ~0.08 orbital eccentricity
- Warm K-type star
- ~0.65 AU orbit
- ~70/30 land-water target
- ~1.25g gravity
- Two substantial moons
- Four major continental systems

## Global climate logic

The world keeps recognizable latitude and seasonal climate patterns, but geography heavily modifies them. Oceans redistribute heat; mountains create wet windward slopes and dry rain shadows; large continental interiors develop stronger seasonal extremes. These are well-established physical effects. citeturn0search3turn0search1turn0search2

The world should be **lush overall without being uniformly wet**. Dense low forests, thick grasslands, wetlands, seasonal woodland, scrub, dry basins, and deserts can all exist.

### Broad belts
- **Equatorial:** warm, productive, often wet; forests, wetlands, river systems.
- **Subtropical:** seasonal woodland, grassland, scrub, semi-arid basins, deserts where rain shadows reinforce dryness.
- **Mid-latitude:** strong seasons; grasslands, woodland, seasonal forests, rivers, cool coastal regions.
- **High latitude:** cold grasslands, hardy low forests, tundra-like zones at highest latitudes, seasonal wetlands and mountain ecosystems.

The ~26° tilt produces meaningful seasons. The shorter ~214-day year compresses the ecological year compared with Earth. The ~0.08 eccentricity adds another seasonal influence, so some regions will have reinforced seasons while others partially offset the two effects.

## HEARTH — connected abundance

**Climate identity:** the most internally varied, continuous continent.

- **Coasts:** ocean-moderated forests, wetlands, estuaries, coastal grasslands.
- **Interior:** broad seasonal grasslands, woodland, enormous river valleys, dry basins.
- **Mountains:** wet windward forests and drier leeward regions; major elevation zones.
- **Ecological pressure:** enormous connected habitats and long migration routes.

Hearth should support the widest continuous ecological networks.

## SHATTERED — fragmented climates

**Climate identity:** neighboring fragments can develop different ecological personalities.

- **Wet fragments:** dense forests, wetlands, productive rivers.
- **Dry fragments:** seasonal grasslands, scrub, dry woodland, interior basins.
- **Mountain fragments:** cool highlands, wet windward slopes, dry rain shadows.
- **Small surrounding islands:** strong coastal influence and isolated ecosystems.

Its key ecological pressure is **geographic isolation**. Related organisms can diverge between enormous fragments without needing the whole continent to have different climates.

## RIFT — climate shaped by active geology

**Climate identity:** the strongest environmental gradients.

- **Rift floor:** warm lowlands, lakes, wetlands, marshes, river corridors.
- **Rift shoulders:** cooler uplands, forests and grasslands.
- **Volcanic zones:** young barren terrain beside mineral-rich older soils and recovering vegetation.
- **Dry sections:** rain-shadow basins, dry grassland, scrub, seasonal waterways.

Rift is the continent where one journey can cross dramatically different environments because geology, elevation, and water are packed together.

## LOST — deep-time isolation

**Climate identity:** physically understandable climate, but operating on a separate evolutionary stage.

- **Coasts:** productive forests, wetlands, grasslands, deltas where conditions favor them.
- **Interior:** strong continental seasonality; large basins, dry grasslands, semi-arid zones, and potentially major deserts.
- **Mountains:** independent mountain systems with their own wet windward and dry leeward regions.
- **Ecological pressure:** isolation over roughly 700 million–1 billion years.

Lost should not be "alien because everything is weird." Its climates make sense. The eventual strangeness comes from what evolution did with them.

## Ocean / water framework

Oceans are a major climate engine because water stores and transports heat through circulation. citeturn0search3turn0search11

The exact ocean-current map remains open, but the eventual model should produce:
- moderated coasts;
- warm and cold current regions;
- wet and dry coastal climates;
- storm corridors;
- different climates around enclosed and semi-enclosed seas.

The two moons complicate tidal behavior, especially in narrow seas and shallow basins, but they are not the primary climate engine.

## Ecological pressures established by this pass

**Hearth:** connected ecosystems, long migrations, seasonal water, broad predator/prey networks.

**Shattered:** isolation, founder effects, local specialization, fragmented habitats.

**Rift:** geological disturbance, changing water systems, volcanic succession, sharp environmental gradients.

**Lost:** deep isolation, independent evolution, ancient ecological continuity, novel predator/prey relationships.

## Four continental identities

**Hearth:** Connected abundance.

**Shattered:** Fragmentation and specialization.

**Rift:** Transition and instability.

**Lost:** Deep-time independence.
