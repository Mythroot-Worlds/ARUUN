# Mythroot Prehistoric World — Ocean and Marine Climate Reference v0.2

**Status:** Current working reference.

## Purpose

This document provides the marine-climate layer that connects planetary parameters, continental geography, ocean circulation, coastal climate, and ecological productivity.

## Working planetary context

- ~1.25g gravity
- 32-hour rotation
- ~26° axial tilt
- ~214 Earth-day year
- ~0.08 eccentricity
- warm K-type star
- ~0.65 AU working orbital distance
- ~70/30 land-water target
- two substantial moons

## Core ocean logic

Ocean circulation must be treated as a major driver of regional climate. Large basins redistribute heat and moisture; continental geometry, prevailing winds, latitude, and bathymetry alter currents.

The world should contain:

- warm equatorial currents;
- cooler poleward currents;
- productive upwelling zones;
- nutrient-rich continental shelves;
- enclosed or semi-enclosed seas;
- strong coastal seasonal contrasts where geography supports them.

## Continental implications

### Hearth
Long coastlines and a mostly continuous continent create broad coastal climate gradients and large river-to-ocean nutrient flows.

### Shattered
Separated continental fragments create many semi-isolated marine basins. Coastal ecosystems can diverge sharply between fragments.

### Rift
The active continental split produces long inland seas, lakes, wetlands, and eventually marine incursions as the rift evolves.

### Lost
The immense surrounding ocean and deep-time isolation create a distinct marine context and limit biological exchange with the other continental systems.

## Ecological rule

Marine productivity should feed directly into coastal food webs and human population geography. Fisheries, shellfish, marine megafauna, estuaries, tidal wetlands, and seabird colonies are ecological resources before they become cultural resources.
