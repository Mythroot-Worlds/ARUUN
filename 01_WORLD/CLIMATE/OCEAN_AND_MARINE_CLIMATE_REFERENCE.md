# MYTHROOT — OCEAN & MARINE CLIMATE REFERENCE v0.2

## Purpose

This document is the detailed working reference for the planet's ocean circulation. It exists to keep coastal climates, marine ecology, navigation, and later human geography consistent.

## Scientific foundation

Surface currents are primarily wind-driven and modified by Coriolis and continental geometry. Northern subtropical gyres rotate clockwise; southern subtropical gyres rotate counterclockwise. Western boundary currents are generally stronger/narrower; eastern boundary currents broader/weaker. citeturn0search4turn0search5

Trade winds dominate roughly 5–30° latitude on either side of the equator and generally move east-to-west. The ITCZ occupies the equatorial convection belt; westerlies dominate much of the midlatitudes. citeturn0search0

## Working circulation

### Hearthward Gyre — Northern subtropical basin
**Rotation:** clockwise.

**Hearthward Western Current:** warm, strong, poleward; warmer/moister coastal climate.

**Hearthward Return Current:** cooler, broad, equatorward; cooler maritime climate and upwelling potential.

### Equatorial Drift
**Direction:** generally westward surface flow under the trade winds.

**Importance:** transports warm tropical surface water westward and creates a productive eastern-side/upwelling contrast when winds and geometry permit. citeturn0search1

### Lostward Gyre — Southern subtropical basin
**Rotation:** counterclockwise.

**Lostward Western Current:** warm, strong, poleward.

**Lostward Return Current:** cool, broad, equatorward; major upwelling candidate.

## Upwelling

Upwelling occurs when surface water is displaced away and deeper water rises. The deeper water is generally colder and nutrient-rich, often producing high biological productivity. citeturn0search2

### Shattered
Major cool-coast upwelling zone. This is one of the planet's richest marine-resource regions.

### Lost
Major cool-coast upwelling zone. This supports a continent-scale marine food web and strong land-sea nutrient transfer.

### Rift
Multiple localized productivity zones associated with rift seas, lakes, volcanic margins, irregular coastlines, and seasonal winds.

## Productivity hierarchy

**Very high:** upwelling coasts, estuaries/deltas, shallow shelves, productive inland seas.

**Moderate/high:** temperate shelves, seasonal mixing zones, reef/coastal systems.

**Lower:** strongly stratified subtropical open-ocean interiors.

Land runoff and deep-ocean nutrients both contribute to marine primary production. citeturn0search34

## Deep circulation

Temperature and salinity differences drive slower density-based deep circulation. It is a background planetary heat/nutrient transport mechanism rather than a detailed map feature at this stage. citeturn0search11turn0search16

## Continental effects

**Hearth:** warm-current coast + cool/upwelling coast + increasingly continental interior.

**Shattered:** strongest ocean influence; current contrasts drive fragment-level climate and seasonal marine-resource pulses.

**Rift:** equatorial circulation + localized productive zones + strong geographic/elevation effects.

**Lost:** enormous coastline; warm-current coast, cool/upwelling coast, productive marine margins, continental interior.
