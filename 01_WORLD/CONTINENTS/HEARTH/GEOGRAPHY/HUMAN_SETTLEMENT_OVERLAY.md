# 3. FIRST-PASS HUMAN ZONES

## A. GREAT PLAINS
**Primary human function:** grassland/woodland mosaic

**Expected population:** Very high in total, but widely dispersed.

**Approximate allocation role:** Major share of the 450,000 grassland/woodland population.

**Why people live here:**
- large herbivore biomass;
- broad plant-resource diversity;
- seasonal seed/grass productivity;
- relatively easy terrestrial movement;
- access to water corridors where rivers cross the plains.

**Settlement pattern:**
- highly mobile communities;
- seasonal aggregation points;
- herd-following populations;
- semi-mobile groups around especially productive water/plant zones.

**Important:** Do not depict the Great Plains as one continuous dense population. Its huge area means population can remain sparse locally despite a large continental total.

**Likely cultural consequence:**
High mobility + broad contact networks + large seasonal territories.

---

## B. SILVERSPINE DELTA
**Primary human functions:** river/floodplain + wetland/lake

**Expected population:** One of Hearth's highest-density human zones.

**Why:**
- reliable freshwater;
- aquatic foods;
- floodplain plants;
- game;
- seasonal productivity;
- multiple movement routes.

**Settlement pattern:**
- relatively stable seasonal camps;
- repeated-use residential sites;
- larger seasonal aggregations;
- short-distance mobility between productive patches.

**Likely cultural consequence:**
Higher population density and more persistent places of return than the Great Plains.

---

## C. GREENMARSH LOWLANDS
**Primary human functions:** wetlands/lakes + floodplain

**Expected population:** High, but geographically constrained by wet terrain.

**Why:**
- fish/aquatic fauna;
- wetland plants;
- reliable water;
- flood-cycle resources.

**Settlement pattern:**
- dry-ground islands/ridges;
- wetland-edge camps;
- seasonal movement following water levels;
- strong use of narrow terrestrial corridors.

**Likely cultural consequence:**
Water knowledge becomes central to survival and movement.

---

## D. NORTHERN WILDS
**Primary human functions:** forest/woodland + lakes/rivers

**Expected population:** Moderate.

**Why:**
- forest foods;
- freshwater;
- game;
- seasonal plant resources.

**Settlement pattern:**
- dispersed communities;
- seasonal camps;
- river/lake-oriented movement;
- lower density than major floodplains.

**Likely cultural consequence:**
Local ecological knowledge and seasonal forest movement become important.

---

## E. STONEHEARTH UPLANDS
**Primary human functions:** upland/woodland + highland transition

**Expected population:** Moderate to low.

**Why:**
- localized productive valleys;
- access to highland resources;
- terrain creates natural population pockets.

**Settlement pattern:**
- valley-focused;
- small residential communities;
- seasonal movement between elevations.

**Likely cultural consequence:**
Greater local identity and somewhat reduced exchange with plains populations.

---

## F. SUNSCOUR BASIN
**Primary human functions:** dry interior / rain-shadow

**Expected population:** Low and highly localized.

**Why:**
- scattered water;
- seasonal productivity;
- limited reliable food concentration.

**Settlement pattern:**
- highly mobile;
- water-source oriented;
- seasonal aggregation around rare productive periods.

**Likely cultural consequence:**
Strong water knowledge, route memory, and resource scheduling.

---

## G. FROSTWARD RANGE
**Primary human functions:** highland valleys

**Expected population:** Low.

**Why:**
- cold;
- elevation;
- difficult movement;
- localized productive valleys.

**Settlement pattern:**
- valley concentrations;
- seasonal elevation movement;
- small communities.

**Likely cultural consequence:**
Strong regional specialization and reduced gene flow between isolated valleys.

---

## H. AEGIR MOUNTAINS
**Primary human functions:** highland/mountain corridor

**Expected population:** Low.

**Role in human geography:**
Primarily a **barrier and corridor system**.

Population should concentrate in:
- valleys;
- passes;
- foothills;
- river headwater zones.

Avoid distributing people uniformly through mountain terrain.

---

## I. SOUTHWARD RANGE
**Primary human functions:** highland barrier / foothill transition

**Expected population:** Low in mountains; somewhat higher in accessible foothills.

**Role:**
Separates ecological zones while providing passes and headwater corridors.

---

## J. EMBERCOAST VOLCANIC ARC
**Primary human functions:** coastal + localized fertile volcanic environments

**Expected population:** Moderate where terrain permits; low in hazardous/highly broken sectors.

**Settlement pattern:**
- coastal pockets;
- volcanic-soil productive zones;
- sheltered valleys;
- seasonal movement around hazardous terrain.

**Likely cultural consequence:**
Strongly localized communities separated by difficult terrain.

---

## K. STORMCOAST PENINSULA
**Primary human functions:** coast + terrestrial resource mosaic

**Expected population:** Moderate.

**Why:**
- marine resources;
- coastal plants;
- terrestrial hunting/gathering;
- access to inland zones.

**Settlement pattern:**
- coastal camps;
- inland seasonal movement;
- resource switching by season.

---

## L. EMERALD GULF
**Primary human functions:** sheltered coastal / estuarine resource network

**Expected population:** Moderate to high locally.

**Why:**
- sheltered water;
- coastal resources;
- river/estuary interfaces;
- easier movement than exposed ocean coast.

**Likely cultural consequence:**
Potentially important contact and exchange zone.

---

## M. DAWNPOINT PENINSULA
**Primary human functions:** coastal + inland transition

**Expected population:** Moderate.

**Role:**
A transition region rather than a single ecological specialization.

Potentially supports communities with mixed coastal and terrestrial subsistence.

---

## N. COASTAL ISLES / ISLE CHAINS
**Primary human functions:** coastal/marine resources

**Expected population:** Low to moderate.

**Important constraint:**
Simple watercraft are plausible only where the technological and environmental context supports them. Open-ocean navigation and advanced ships are not assumed.

Island populations should therefore be:
- locally concentrated;
- dependent on accessible resources;
- more isolated where crossings are difficult;
- not automatically maritime specialists.

# 4. HUMAN MOVEMENT CORRIDORS

Before exact rivers are drawn, mark these as **corridor classes** rather than fixed routes.

### River corridors
Mountains/highlands → plains → wetlands/delta/coast.

### Grassland corridors
Broad movement across Great Plains following large herbivore and seasonal plant productivity.

### Wetland chains
SilverSpine Delta ↔ Greenmarsh Lowlands ↔ connected lakes/rivers where geography permits.

### Foothill corridors
Mountain margins linking highland valleys with plains and woodland.

### Coastal corridors
Stormcoast ↔ Embercoast ↔ Dawnpoint ↔ Emerald Gulf where terrain permits.

### Mountain passes
Aegir / Frostward / Southward systems should contain a limited number of important human crossings.

---

# 5. DENSITY LANGUAGE FOR MAP MARKING

Use five broad map categories:

**D1 — HIGH**
Repeatedly productive environments capable of supporting persistent communities and large seasonal aggregations.

**D2 — MODERATE**
Reliable resources but greater seasonal or geographic constraints.

**D3 — LOW**
Small populations concentrated around specific resources.

**D4 — SEASONAL**
Temporary occupation/aggregation rather than continuous residential presence.

**D5 — TRANSIT / NEAR-EMPTY**
Movement corridors, dangerous terrain, extreme environments, or areas with insufficient reliable resources for sustained occupation.

These categories describe **population density**, not importance.

---

# 6. CONTACT / ISOLATION MARKERS

Mark likely:

### High-contact zones
- major river junctions;
- delta/estuary systems;
- broad grassland corridors;
- accessible coastal resource zones;
- mountain passes.

### Intermediate-contact zones
- woodland margins;
- foothills;
- lake chains;
- mixed-resource transition zones.

### Low-contact zones
- isolated mountain valleys;
- dry interior water pockets;
- difficult volcanic terrain;
- isolated islands;
- extreme environments.

These will later help determine where distinct peoples are justified.

---

# 7. WHAT THIS OVERLAY DOES NOT DECIDE

Do **not** use this pass to determine:

- languages;
- political boundaries;
- religions/beliefs;
- clothing traditions;
- marriage systems;
- named settlements;
- exact river courses;

Those come later.

# 9. WORKING PRINCIPLE

The map should ultimately show:

**environment → water → resources → human corridors → population density → contact/isolation → peoples**

Not:

**people → arbitrary settlement → environment built around them.**

The human geography follows the world.


# 10. HYDROLOGY DECISION LAYER

Hearth's major drainage architecture is now a creative worldbuilding decision rather than an unresolved placeholder.

The major systems are:

- Great Western/Southwestern Watershed: Frostward/Aegir → Stonehearth → Great Plains → Greenmarsh/SilverSpine → coast.
- Great Plains: a broad drainage mosaic rather than a single river basin.
- Greenmarsh: low-gradient receiving basin with wetlands, lakes, backwaters, and distributaries.
- Northern Chain: northern uplands → lake systems → rivers → Greenmarsh and/or northern coast.
- Sunscour: major closed/endorheic basin draining inward to an inland lake/salt system.
- Southward Range: major watershed divide separating interior and southern coastal drainage.
- Embercoast: short, steep volcanic rivers draining to coastal plains/estuaries.
- Eastern/southeastern uplands: shorter direct-to-coast drainage systems.

The SilverSpine Delta is the terminal deltaic complex of the principal continental river network.

Exact minor channels, springs, tributaries, flood extents, and local river names remain open for regional mapping.

# HEARTH — PEOPLES GEOGRAPHY OVERLAY
## Regional Human Populations → Candidate Peoples v0.1

**Status:** WORKING CANON / PEOPLES-PHASE DECISION  
**Purpose:** Establish Hearth's first broad human cultural regions before developing individual cultural dossiers.

> This is a geographic-cultural overlay, not a final list of tribes. A "people" may contain many residential communities, seasonal camps, local branches, and overlapping identities.

---

# 1. HEARTH DEMOGRAPHIC BASE

**Total population:** ~1,500,000

Current continental allocation:

- River valleys / floodplains — 375,000
- Grassland / woodland mosaic — 450,000
- Wetlands / major lakes — 225,000
- Temperate/subtropical coasts — 180,000
- Highland valleys — 120,000
- Dry interior / rain shadows — 105,000
- Other scattered / seasonal — 45,000

Typical residential communities remain ~80–150 people, with smaller groups and larger seasonal aggregations possible. Multiple communities can share language, marriage networks, ecological knowledge, trade, and seasonal gathering sites.

---

# 2. CULTURAL-GEOGRAPHIC PRINCIPLE

Hearth is the most connected of the major continents.

Therefore:

- regional cultures should **overlap**;
- boundaries should usually be fuzzy;
- river and grassland corridors should carry language, marriage, technology, and ecological knowledge;
- mountains, difficult wetlands, dry interiors, and isolated coasts can produce stronger local identities;
- a geographic region does **not** automatically equal one people.

The first pass identifies **candidate cultural regions**. The detailed cultural dossiers decide whether each becomes one people, several related peoples, or a regional branch of a broader cultural network.

---

# 3. CANDIDATE HUMAN REGIONS

## H-01 — THE GREAT PLAINS PEOPLE-NETWORK

**Core geography:** Great Plains and connected grassland/woodland mosaic.

**Population role:** Largest human population zone on Hearth.

**Working population:** ~300,000–400,000 of the continental population, subject to final spatial allocation.

### Ecology
- large-herbivore biomass;
- Broadseed and other grassland-edge foods;
- woodland resources;
- seasonal water;
- major river corridors;
- broad terrestrial movement.

### Likely mobility
**Highly mobile → semi-mobile.**

People follow:
- herbivore migrations;
- seed/grass cycles;
- fruiting seasons;
- reliable water;
- floodplain productivity.

### Human geography
Do not make this one enormous tribe.

Expect a broad network of related communities sharing:
- seasonal routes;
- marriage exchange;
- hunting knowledge;
- plant knowledge;
- meeting places;
- stories and language continuums.

### Likely identity
The defining cultural trait is likely **mobility across a huge connected landscape**, not permanent settlement.

### Contact
Very high with riverland and woodland populations.

---

## H-02 — SILVERSPINE RIVERLANDS / DELTA

**Core geography:** Principal SilverSpine drainage, major floodplains, river junctions, and terminal delta.

**Population role:** One of Hearth's highest-density regions.

**Working population:** ~250,000–350,000.

### Ecology
- reliable freshwater;
- floodplain plants;
- aquatic foods;
- game;
- fertile floodplain soils;
- seasonal flood pulses.

### Likely mobility
**Relatively sedentary to seasonally mobile.**

Communities may return repeatedly to the same productive locations.

### Human geography
Population concentrates at:
- river junctions;
- reliable crossings;
- elevated floodplain ground;
- delta margins;
- productive oxbows and backwaters.

### Likely identity
River knowledge becomes a central cultural competency.

Potential specialties:
- flood prediction;
- fishing;
- water management;
- river crossings;
- seasonal floodplain harvesting;
- preservation of aquatic/plant foods.

### Contact
Extremely high.

This is likely one of Hearth's major cultural mixing zones.

---

## H-03 — GREENMARSH WETLAND NETWORK

**Core geography:** Greenmarsh Lowlands, marshes, shallow lakes, backwaters, seasonal channels, and floodplain forests.

**Population role:** Major wetland/lake population.

**Working population:** ~150,000–225,000.

### Ecology
- fish and aquatic fauna;
- wetland plants;
- marshgrain-equivalent future resources where appropriate;
- floodplain game;
- reliable water.

### Likely mobility
**Seasonal / wetland-edge mobility.**

People may shift between:
- dry-ground camps;
- marsh edges;
- lake margins;
- floodplain forest;
- seasonal high-water refuges.

### Likely identity
Deep water-and-wetland knowledge.

The landscape itself may be difficult for outsiders to navigate, producing strong local expertise without requiring geographic isolation from the rest of Hearth.

### Contact
High with SilverSpine and Northern Wilds; moderate with Great Plains.

---

### Ecology

- nuts;
- fruit;
- game;

- rivers;
- seasonal plant resources.

### Likely mobility
**Seasonally mobile / semi-mobile.**

Movement follows:

### Likely identity
Strong local ecological knowledge and seasonal forest movement.

### Contact
Moderate-high along rivers and lake chains; lower in interior forest zones.

---

## H-05 — STONEHEARTH UPLANDS

**Core geography:** Stonehearth Uplands and transition zones between plains, woodland, and mountain systems.

**Population role:** Moderate population concentrated in valleys and productive upland pockets.

**Working population:** ~80,000–120,000.

### Ecology

- upland grass;
- river headwaters;
- nuts and roots;
- game;
- stone resources.

### Likely mobility
**Semi-mobile.**

Seasonal vertical movement is plausible:
- lower valleys during colder periods;
- higher elevations when resources shift.

### Likely identity
A transitional people/network connecting plains and highlands.

### Contact
High with Great Plains and riverlands; moderate with mountain populations.

---

## H-06 — SUNSCOUR BASIN PEOPLE

**Core geography:** closed interior drainage basin and its reliable springs, rivers, seasonal lakes, and groundwater-fed zones.

**Population role:** Sparse but geographically broad.

**Working population:** ~75,000–105,000.

### Ecology
- scarce permanent water;
- seasonal rivers;
- springs;
- inland lake/salt basin;
- dry woodland/scrub;
- concentrated resource pockets.

### Likely mobility
**Highly mobile.**

Water-source scheduling becomes a core survival skill.

### Human geography
Most of the basin should remain sparsely occupied.

Population clusters around:
- perennial springs;
- river sources;
- reliable groundwater;
- seasonal lake margins;
- productive mountain-front fans.

### Likely identity
A culture organized around **water memory and route knowledge**.

### Contact
Moderate along mountain-front and river corridors; low across the deepest dry interior.

---

## H-07 — FROSTWARD / AEGIR HIGHLANDS

**Core geography:** highland valleys, mountain passes, headwaters, alpine/subalpine zones.

**Population role:** Low-density but locally stable.

**Working population:** ~80,000–120,000.

### Ecology
- cold-tolerant plants;
- highland browsers;
- mountain game;
- headwaters;
- steep terrain;
- localized fertile valleys.

### Likely mobility
**Valley-based seasonal movement.**

People may move vertically rather than horizontally:
- valley floor;
- foothill;
- upland;
- seasonal high pasture/hunting grounds.

### Likely identity
Strong local valley identities are likely.

The mountain system should contain several related populations rather than one universal "mountain people."

### Contact
Moderate through major passes; low between isolated valleys.

---

## H-08 — SOUTHERN FOOTHILLS / MOUNTAIN MARGINS

**Core geography:** Southward Range foothills and adjacent lowlands.

**Population role:** Low-moderate.

**Working population:** ~40,000–70,000.

### Ecology
- mixed woodland;
- foothill grass;
- river valleys;
- mountain resources;
- access to southern coastal drainage.

### Likely mobility
**Semi-mobile**, with movement between foothills and lowlands.

### Likely identity
Potentially transitional between inland and southern coastal populations.

### Contact
Moderate-high along foothill corridors.

---

## H-09 — EMBERCOAST

**Core geography:** volcanic arc, steep valleys, volcanic plains, estuaries, and coastal pockets.

**Population role:** Localized moderate population.

**Working population:** ~50,000–80,000.

### Ecology
- volcanic soils;
- coastal resources;
- short steep rivers;
- estuaries;
- localized fertile pockets;
- hazardous volcanic terrain.

### Likely mobility
**Localized / seasonal.**

People move between:
- coastal resources;
- fertile volcanic pockets;
- inland valleys.

### Likely identity
Strong local identities are plausible because productive areas are separated by difficult terrain.

### Contact
Moderate along coast and accessible valleys; lower across volcanic barriers.

---

## H-10 — STORMCOAST / EMERALD GULF / DAWNPOINT

**Core geography:** temperate/subtropical coasts, sheltered gulf, estuaries, peninsulas, and coastal-island margins.

**Population role:** Moderate coastal population.

**Working population:** ~120,000–180,000.

### Ecology
- marine foods;
- estuaries;
- coastal plants;
- terrestrial hunting;
- sheltered freshwater outlets.

### Likely mobility
**Coastal seasonal mobility → localized semi-sedentary communities.**

### Important technology constraint
Early aquatic mobility remains limited to swimming, floating, simple rafts, and later simple dugout/comparable craft as woodworking develops. Open-ocean seafaring is not assumed.

### Likely identity
Coastal populations may share a broad cultural network without all becoming "maritime peoples."

### Contact
High along sheltered coastlines and estuaries; lower across difficult crossings.

# 5. CANDIDATE PEOPLE COUNT

At this stage, the overlay proposes **10 cultural-geographic regions**, NOT 10 finalized peoples.

The eventual result may become:

- fewer broad peoples with regional branches;
- roughly one people per region;
- multiple peoples within a major region;
- overlapping identities where mobility and marriage networks are especially strong.

The Great Plains and highlands are the two regions most likely to require internal subdivision.

---

# 6. PEOPLE-CREATION TEST

A candidate becomes a distinct people only when enough of the following persist:

- shared ecological knowledge;
- repeated marriage network;
- shared language/dialect continuum;
- shared seasonal movement system;
- shared material traditions;
- shared origin/history;
- shared social customs;
- meaningful geographic boundary;
- recognizable relationship with neighboring populations.

Geography alone is insufficient.
