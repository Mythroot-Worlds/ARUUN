# HEARTH — HYDROLOGY & DRAINAGE
## Continental Geographic Layer v0.1

**Status:** WORKING CANON — CREATIVE GEOGRAPHY DECISION  
**Purpose:** Establish where Hearth's major water comes from, how it moves, where it accumulates, and where it ultimately drains.

This layer supersedes the earlier state of "exact river courses open." Rivers and major drainage architecture are now an active worldbuilding decision. Exact local channels, minor tributaries, springs, and seasonal streams remain open for later regional detail.

---

# 1. CONTINENTAL HYDROLOGIC LOGIC

Hearth's major water systems follow a conventional continental pattern:

**mountains / uplands → headwaters → tributaries → major rivers → floodplains / wetlands / lakes → deltas / estuaries → ocean**

Hearth also contains at least one major **closed (endorheic) drainage basin**, where water does not reach the ocean:

**mountains → rivers → interior basin → lake / salt basin → evaporation**

The continental map should therefore show both open and closed drainage.

---

# 2. MAJOR WATERSHED SYSTEMS

## A. GREAT WESTERN / SOUTHWESTERN WATERSHED

### Source
The Frostward and Aegir mountain systems act as major precipitation catchments.

### Flow
**Frostward / Aegir → Stonehearth Uplands → Great Plains → Greenmarsh / SilverSpine lowlands → coast**

### Character
- cold, steep headwaters;
- rocky upper valleys;
- increasingly broad tributaries through uplands;
- large meandering lowland rivers;
- extensive floodplains;
- terminal wetland / delta complexes.

This becomes one of Hearth's principal continental river networks and a major human movement corridor.

---

## B. GREAT PLAINS DRAINAGE MOSAIC

The Great Plains are **not** one flat basin drained by one river.

They contain a network of:
- major rivers;
- tributaries;
- seasonal streams;
- braided channels;
- meandering lowland rivers;
- groundwater-fed channels;
- floodplain wetlands.

Several drainage branches converge toward the lowlands and delta systems.

### Ecological consequence
Periodic flooding creates fertile floodplain patches without converting the entire Great Plains into wetland.

### Human consequence
The Plains support a large but dispersed population, with communities concentrating near reliable water and seasonal resource corridors.

---

## C. GREENMARSH RECEIVING BASIN

Greenmarsh Lowlands function as a broad, low-gradient receiving landscape.

Water arrives from:
- Northern Wilds;
- Stonehearth;
- Great Plains;
- connected lake/river systems.

### Hydrologic character
- oxbow lakes;
- backwaters;
- seasonal channels;
- marshes;
- shallow lakes;
- floodplain forests;
- distributaries;
- permanent and temporary wetlands.

The system ultimately connects with the SilverSpine lowlands/delta complex and coastal drainage.

---

## D. NORTHERN DRAINAGE / LAKE CHAIN

The Northern Wilds contain a separate northern watershed with substantial lake storage.

### Flow
**northern uplands / mountain catchments → lakes → rivers → Greenmarsh and/or northern coastal outlets**

### Character
- cooler climate;
- forested watersheds;
- snow/rain storage;
- lower evaporation;
- interconnected lakes;
- moderate-flow rivers.

The lake chain creates both ecological productivity and human movement corridors.

---

## E. SUNSCOUR CLOSED BASIN

Sunscour Basin is a major **endorheic drainage system**.

Water falls in surrounding mountains/uplands and drains inward.

### Flow
**mountain catchments → seasonal/perennial rivers → inland lake or salt basin → evaporation**

Water does not normally reach the ocean.

### Consequences
- scattered permanent water sources;
- seasonal lakes;
- springs;
- groundwater-fed oases;
- mineral/salt accumulation;
- very sparse population across the basin interior;
- population concentration around reliable water.

This explains the dry interior as a true hydrologic system rather than merely "dry land."

---

## F. SOUTHWARD RANGE WATERSHED DIVIDE

The Southward Range functions as a major drainage divide.

One side feeds interior systems.

The opposite side produces shorter southern/coastal rivers.

### Consequence
The range separates distinct drainage worlds and creates strong geographic barriers while maintaining a limited set of passes and headwater corridors.

---

## G. EMBERCOAST VOLCANIC DRAINAGE

The Embercoast Volcanic Arc produces mostly shorter, steeper coastal rivers.

### Flow
**volcanic highlands → steep valleys → coastal plains / fans → estuaries → ocean**

### Character
- rapids;
- waterfalls;
- steep channels;
- volcanic-soil floodplains;
- narrow valleys;
- estuaries;
- localized volcanic lakes/basins.

These rivers are energetic rather than continent-spanning.

---

## H. EASTERN / DIRECT COASTAL DRAINAGE

Where eastern and southeastern uplands lie close to the coast, drainage runs directly from upland catchments to the sea.

These systems are:
- shorter;
- steeper;
- more seasonal where rainfall is seasonal;
- strongly localized.

They provide additional coastal estuaries and freshwater corridors without requiring every coastal region to be fed by a continental-scale river.

---

# 3. MAJOR CONTINENTAL RIVER SYSTEMS

## SilverSpine River System

**Role:** Hearth's principal continental drainage network.

**General course:**
**Aegir / Frostward headwaters → Stonehearth Uplands → Great Plains → Greenmarsh / SilverSpine lowlands → coast**

The **SilverSpine Delta** is the terminal lowland/delta complex where major drainage branches converge and reach the coast.

The name therefore applies to both the principal river system and its major deltaic region.

---

## Northern Chain

A lake-and-river network draining the Northern Wilds.

**General course:**
**Northern uplands → major lakes → rivers → Greenmarsh and/or northern coast**

Exact lake and tributary names remain open.

---

## Sunscour Basin Rivers

Multiple streams and rivers feed the closed basin.

These should not be drawn as a single permanent river.

Expect:
- perennial channels near major springs/headwaters;
- seasonal rivers;
- disappearing channels;
- inland deltas;
- salt-lake margins.

---

## Southern River Systems

Shorter systems drain the Southward Range and adjacent highlands toward southern coasts.

They are separated from the Great Plains/Sunscour systems by watershed divides.

---

# 4. LAKES & WETLANDS

Hearth's major aquatic environments include:

### Greenmarsh Lowlands
Large wetland/floodplain complex.

### Northern Lake Chain
Connected freshwater lakes forming a major northern storage system.

### Sunscour Inland Basin
Closed drainage lake/salt-basin system with seasonal water-level changes.

### River Oxbows
Common in mature lowland river systems.

### Volcanic Lakes
Localized basins within the Embercoast volcanic terrain.

### Seasonal Wetlands
Floodplain depressions and seasonal channels across the Great Plains and Greenmarsh.

# 5. HUMAN GEOGRAPHY CONSEQUENCES

Water is now a primary determinant of population placement.

### Highest-density potential
- major floodplains;
- delta margins;
- large lake shores;
- reliable river junctions;
- productive wetland edges.

### Moderate-density potential
- grassland river corridors;
- woodland/lake systems;
- foothills;
- sheltered coasts;
- estuaries.

### Low-density potential
- dry interior;
- high mountains;
- volcanic highlands;
- isolated islands;
- regions with unreliable seasonal water.

### Seasonal occupation
- ephemeral rivers;
- seasonal lake margins;
- migratory grassland water sources;
- floodplain resource pulses.

---

# 6. HUMAN MOVEMENT CORRIDORS

The hydrology establishes natural movement routes:

1. **Major river corridors**
2. **Lake-chain shorelines**
3. **Floodplain networks**
4. **Great Plains water corridors**
5. **Foothill/headwater corridors**
6. **Mountain passes**
7. **Coastal estuaries and sheltered shores**

These corridors will later influence:
- population density;
- language contact;
- trade/exchange;
- migration;
- cultural boundaries;
- conflict;
- gene flow.

---

# 7. ECOLOGICAL CONSEQUENCES

The hydrology supports existing Hearth ecological assumptions:

- floodplain plant communities;
- wetlands and marshes;
- riverine megafauna;
- lake ecosystems;
- grassland productivity;
- coastal estuaries.

River-dependent fauna and flora should be distributed according to actual drainage rather than being treated as uniformly continental.

# 8. OPEN LOCAL DETAIL

The following remain intentionally open:

- exact minor tributaries;
- individual springs;
- precise river widths;
- exact meander patterns;
- seasonal flood extents;
- every minor lake;
- local irrigation-like human modifications;
- named river crossings;
- exact delta distributary map.

These should be resolved when individual regional cultural documents require them.

---

# 9. DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Hearth's water map is not decorative.

**Topography determines drainage.  
Drainage determines ecological productivity.  
Ecology influences population.  
Population creates movement corridors and contact zones.  
Those patterns help produce distinct peoples.**
