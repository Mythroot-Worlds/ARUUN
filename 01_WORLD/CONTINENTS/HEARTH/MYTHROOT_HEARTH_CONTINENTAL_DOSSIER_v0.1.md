# MYTHROOT — HEARTH CONTINENTAL DOSSIER
## Geography & Climate Reference — v0.1

**Purpose:** Standalone reference for mapmaking and future regional development.

## Geographic identity
Hearth is the mostly intact great continental landmass. Its defining feature is continuity: enormous interior distances, broad connected watersheds, old continental terrain, and mountain systems concentrated toward portions of its active margins.

## Major geographic structures
- broad ancient continental interior
- major mountain systems near active margins
- older, heavily eroded interior uplands
- large river basins originating in mountain regions
- broad floodplains and wetlands
- coastal plains and estuaries
- small peripheral island systems
- interior basins and plateaus
- local rain-shadow regions behind major ranges

## Climate
### Coastal
Ocean-moderated. Depending on currents and wind direction: humid forests, dense low vegetation, coastal grasslands, wetlands, storm-exposed regions.

### Mountain-facing
Wet windward slopes, forests, wet uplands, major headwaters.

### Leeward
Rain shadows produce dry woodland, grassland, scrub, semi-arid basins, and localized arid regions.

### Central interior
Strong continental seasonality: enormous seasonal grasslands, woodland mosaics, broad river plains, dry forests, interior basins.

### Cooler regions
Seasonal forests, cool grasslands, uplands, and cold-adapted ecosystems at higher elevations/latitudes.

## Water system
Mountains act as major watersheds:
mountains → precipitation → headwaters → rivers → floodplains/wetlands → coastal deltas.

## Ecological identity
**Connected abundance.** Large continuous habitats and long terrestrial migration routes.

## Artist's Map Brief
Show one enormous connected landmass, substantial interior, active-margin mountain systems, broad rivers flowing from mountains, large basins, wet/dry mountain contrasts, grassland/woodland interiors, coastal wetlands/deltas, and small peripheral islands.

## Open cartographic details
Exact coastline, mountain placement, river courses, climate boundaries, and regional names remain to be refined.
