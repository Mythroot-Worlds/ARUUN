# MYTHROOT — RIFT CONTINENTAL DOSSIER
## Geography & Climate Reference — v0.1

**Purpose:** Standalone reference for mapmaking and future regional development.

## Geographic identity
Rift is one enormous continental system actively separating into two major halves. The rift is irregular and immature rather than a clean oceanic split.

## Major geographic structures
- two enormous continental halves
- long rift system
- fault valleys
- steep escarpments
- rift lakes
- inland seas
- wetlands
- volcanic fields and cones
- highlands
- dry basins
- branching/variable rift geometry

## Climate
### Highlands
Moisture capture produces wet forests, cool uplands, dense vegetation, and river headwaters.

### Rift floor
Lower and often warmer, with sections that are substantially drier: dry grasslands, seasonal woodland, warm lowlands, seasonal waterways, localized saline basins.

### Lakes and wetlands
Permanent freshwater systems, seasonal lakes, marshes, floodplains, dense riparian vegetation, aquatic plant communities.

### Volcanic regions
Fresh barren substrate → pioneer vegetation → developing soils → recovering woodland/grassland → mature ecosystems.

### Branches
Different branches can have dramatically different moisture regimes.

## Water system
The rift creates a chain of major drainage basins, potentially freshwater, saline, seasonal, river-connected, inland-sea-connected, or intermittently isolated.

## Ecological identity
**Transition and instability.** Strong environmental gradients per unit distance.

## Artist's Map Brief
Show two enormous halves, an irregular central rift, large lakes/inland seas, wetlands, steep escarpments, highlands, volcanic zones, dry rift-floor basins, wet uplands, and sections where the rift is narrow versus wide.
