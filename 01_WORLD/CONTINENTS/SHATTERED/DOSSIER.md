# MYTHROOT — SHATTERED CONTINENTAL DOSSIER
## Geography & Climate Reference — v0.1

**Purpose:** Standalone reference for mapmaking and future regional development.

## Geographic identity
Shattered is a former continental system divided into several enormous continental fragments. These are continental-scale islands, not a field of small islands.

## Major geographic structures
- one especially large western/central fragment
- several enormous eastern/northern fragments
- smaller peripheral islands
- independent mountain systems
- deep marine basins
- major straits
- isolated interior basins

## Climate
Ocean influence is stronger than on Hearth.

### Largest fragment
Wet coasts, seasonal woodland, grasslands, dry forests, interior basins, localized semi-arid regions.

### Wetter fragments
Dense low forests, wetlands, productive rivers, foggy/stormy coasts.

### Drier eastern fragments
Dry woodland, grassland, scrub, seasonal rivers, dry basins, localized arid environments.

## Water system
The seas separating fragments are major marine systems. Some straits are narrow and potentially strongly tidal; others form deep oceanic basins.

## Established ecological seed
A future amphibious/marine-capable migratory species may migrate between major fragments. Working concept: wet-season abundance on a relatively dry eastern fragment is followed by hot/dry seasonal decline, prompting migration toward a wetter western fragment. The journey may involve roughly 40 miles of open water and carries genuine mortality risk. Species identity remains undefined.

## Ecological identity
**Fragmentation and specialization.**

## Artist's Map Brief
Show several enormous continental fragments, one especially large western/central fragment, deep seas and straits, independent mountains, wet coastal zones, drier interiors, smaller islands, and geological relationships suggesting former connection without making the fragments clean puzzle pieces.

## Open cartographic details
Exact fragment count/shape, coastline geometry, mountain placement, currents, climate boundaries, and regional names remain to be refined.
