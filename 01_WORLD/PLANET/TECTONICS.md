# Mythroot Prehistoric World — Tectonic Model v0.2

## Status

**Working scientific model — not locked canon.**

This revision is the actual tectonic reasoning pass. The earlier visual concepts should be treated only as visualizations of the previous conceptual model, not as authoritative geography.

## Scientific constraints used

The model uses real plate-tectonic mechanisms:

- divergent boundaries create new oceanic crust;
- convergent oceanic margins can subduct, producing trenches, earthquakes, volcanic arcs, and mountain building;
- transform boundaries accommodate horizontal motion and can offset otherwise continuous systems;
- continental rifts create fault-bounded basins and may either fail or mature into new ocean basins;
- oblique convergence can produce rotation and strike-slip displacement;
- continental fragments and volcanic arcs can accrete to larger continental margins.

These processes are documented by USGS sources cited below.

## Creative constraints

The present world must contain:

1. A mostly intact great continent.
2. A fragmented great continental system consisting of several enormous pieces.
3. A great continental system currently splitting into two major halves.
4. A huge, continuous Lost Continent separated from the others by an enormous ocean.
5. The three main continental systems grouped in one broad region.
6. The Lost Continent isolated for roughly 700 million–1 billion years as a working timescale.
7. A world that feels prehistoric rather than fantastical: geology should look old, active, and natural.
8. No need for an Earth-identical continental arrangement.

## The actual tectonic architecture

### Plate H — Hearth Plate

Carries most of the stable continent.

**Motion:** slow northwestward drift relative to the main oceanic system.

**Boundaries:**
- southern/eastern oceanic margins: mostly convergent/subduction;
- western margin: mixed transform and convergent segments;
- northern interior-facing margin: mostly passive/older boundary.

**Resulting geography:**
- ancient stable interior;
- old mountain systems toward active margins;
- broad interior basins and plains;
- volcanic island arcs offshore;
- small accreted continental fragments along some coasts.

### Plate S — Shatter-West Plate

Carries the western/central fragments of the Shattered Continent.

**Motion:** generally southwestward, with a rotational component.

**Boundaries:**
- eastern side: major transform/oblique boundary;
- southern/western oceanic margin: mixed subduction and transform;
- internal region: old failed rifts and continental sutures.

**Resulting geography:**
- several enormous continental fragments;
- deep marine basins between fragments;
- offset mountain ranges;
- rotated coastlines;
- long fault systems;
- volcanic arcs near active subduction margins.

### Plate SE — Shatter-East Plate

Carries the eastern fragments of the Shattered Continent.

**Motion:** generally southeastward relative to Shatter-West, with slow rotation.

**Boundaries:**
- western side: long-lived transform/oblique boundary;
- outer oceanic margins: portions of subduction;
- northern/eastern edges: mixed passive and convergent margins.

**Resulting geography:**
- large continental islands rather than tiny islands;
- mountain chains that do not line up cleanly with neighboring fragments;
- narrow seas and deep ocean channels;
- evidence of former continental connections.

The Shattered Continent is therefore not simply one continent that cracked into pieces along a single line. It is the surviving product of multiple rifts plus later translation and rotation.

### Plate RC — Rift Crown Plate

Carries the northern/one major half of the Split Continent.

**Motion:** slowly northeastward relative to Rift South.

### Plate RS — Rift South Plate

Carries the southern/other major half.

**Motion:** slowly southwestward relative to Rift Crown.

**Boundary between them:** active continental rift.

**Resulting geography:**
- a long rift valley;
- fault escarpments;
- volcanic centers;
- large freshwater basins;
- narrow inland seas;
- geothermal regions;
- regions of uplift beside subsiding basins.

The rift is geologically young enough that the two halves remain close. It is not yet a mature ocean.

A failed-rift/partial-rift component is scientifically plausible. USGS descriptions of continental rifts note that extension produces fault-bounded basins and that some rifts can remain failed rather than progressing to full ocean formation.

### Plate O — Oceanic Ring Plate

The principal oceanic plate surrounding and separating the main continental cluster.

**Motion:** generally eastward/northeastward relative to the main continental cluster, with local vector changes around spreading centers.

**Function:** this is the principal "moving floor" around the continents.

**Boundaries:**
- spreading ridges generate new oceanic crust;
- subduction zones consume older oceanic crust;
- transform faults offset spreading segments and connect some ridges to trenches.

**Resulting geography:**
- major deep-ocean basins;
- trenches;
- volcanic arcs;
- earthquake belts;
- scattered hotspot/island chains.

### Plate L — Lost Plate

Carries the Lost Continent.

**Motion:** generally southward relative to the main continental cluster, with slow independent rotation.

**Isolation mechanism:** the Lost Plate separated from the ancestral continental complex when a major continental rift matured into a spreading ocean basin. It has remained on the far side of that ocean for roughly 700 million–1 billion years as a working history.

**Important:** the Lost Plate is not geologically dead.

It has:
- active margins in some regions;
- old passive margins in others;
- internal ancient mountain belts;
- younger volcanic provinces;
- long-lived erosion and uplift.

Its biological isolation is therefore accompanied by a genuine geological history of its own.

## How the four continents were produced

### Hearth — why it stayed together

Hearth sits on the oldest and thickest continental core.

When the ancestral supercontinental system fragmented, the surrounding crust was more readily pulled apart than this core.

It therefore survived mostly intact.

Its later mountain belts form where oceanic plates repeatedly approached and subducted beneath its margins.

**Visual result:** one enormous coherent landmass with old mountains, broad interior lowlands, and smaller peripheral island systems.

### Shattered — why it broke so badly

The Shattered region sat in a tectonically complicated part of the ancestral continent where several rifts intersected.

Some rifts succeeded and became ocean basins.

Some failed.

Later transform motion shifted the resulting blocks sideways and rotated them.

Some fragments also encountered volcanic arcs or other continental fragments and were accreted onto neighboring margins.

**Visual result:** several enormous continental islands separated by deep seas, with coastlines and mountain belts that often look like pieces of a former whole but do not fit together perfectly.

### Split/Rift — why it is still one system

The Split Continent sits over an active continental rift.

The crust is being stretched and thinned, but the process has not yet progressed to a mature ocean basin across the whole rift.

Some areas have already subsided enough to form lakes or narrow seas; other portions remain dry land.

Volcanism concentrates along the hotter, more strongly stretched sections.

**Visual result:** two enormous land halves divided by a long, irregular rift containing lakes, inland seas, wetlands, fault escarpments, and volcanic fields.

### Lost — why it is so different

The Lost Continent separated early enough to receive an enormous independent evolutionary history but late enough to share deep ancestry with life on the main continents.

Its geological isolation is produced by a broad oceanic basin, not magical separation.

Its internal geology continued evolving after separation.

**Visual result:** a single enormous landmass with its own mountain systems, basins, climates, and ancient geological scars.

## Plate boundary logic

The model intentionally avoids putting every boundary around a continent into the same category.

A real continental margin can transition between:

**subduction → transform → passive margin → rift → subduction**

over geological time.

Likewise, a present-day transform fault may be the remnant of an older spreading or subduction system.

This lets the world feel geologically old rather than mechanically simple.

## Mountains

Major mountain ranges are derived from:

1. continental collision;
2. oceanic subduction beneath continental margins;
3. accreted volcanic arcs/terranes;
4. uplift along major rift shoulders;
5. long-lived transform deformation.

Therefore:

- Hearth's oldest mountains are concentrated around ancient collision zones and older active margins.
- Shattered fragments have separate mountain systems that were once connected but later displaced.
- Rift lands have long escarpments and uplifted shoulders rather than one continuous collision-built range through the entire center.
- The Lost Continent contains mountain systems unrelated to the present mountains of the main cluster except where very old ancestry can be traced.

## Volcanoes

Volcanic activity should cluster primarily around:

- active subduction zones;
- continental rift zones;
- spreading centers where appropriate;
- a small number of long-lived hotspot provinces.

Volcanoes should not be scattered uniformly across the world.

## Earthquakes

Earthquakes should cluster around:

- subduction zones;
- transform faults;
- active continental rifts;
- major plate-boundary zones.

The Split Continent therefore has frequent local earthquakes. The Shattered Continent has significant earthquake belts along its transform margins. Hearth's stable interior is comparatively quiet.

## Moons and tectonics

The moons should **not** be treated as the primary engines moving the continents.

The planet's internal heat and mantle dynamics remain the fundamental tectonic driver.

The two moons can, however, contribute a secondary cyclic stress/tidal influence that interacts with an already active tectonic system.

That preserves the user's desired connection between the moons and geological behavior without requiring impossible lunar gravity to shove continents around.

## What is now established vs. still open

### Working-established
- Four continental systems.
- Lost Continent is very old and geographically isolated.
- Hearth is the stable continental core.
- Shattered is a multi-fragment continental system.
- Split/Rift is an actively separating continental system.
- Lost Plate is independent.
- Shattered fragments experienced rifting plus transform/rotational movement.
- Split Continent is an incomplete continental rift.
- Main oceanic system contains spreading, subduction, and transform boundaries.
- Tectonics remain active in the present world.
- Exact plate motion is slow and geological, not catastrophic.
- Lunar effects are secondary rather than the fundamental tectonic engine.

## Bottom line

The continents are not four shapes that happened to be placed on a map.

They are the accumulated result of:

**ancient continental assembly → supercontinental organization → major rifting → Lost Continent separation → multiple secondary rifts → transform displacement and rotation → subduction and accretion → ongoing continental rifting → present-day tectonic activity.**
