**006 — Marshtread**
CANONICAL EXEMPLAR

- **Continent / Environment:** Hearth / wetland-floodplain
- **Primary function:** Amphibious grazer (contrast solution to Broadback's dry-grassland grazing)
- **Secondary functions:** Prey base for floodplain predators; seasonal vegetation control in shallows
- **Body plan:** Barrel-bodied quadruped, splayed broad hooves/pads for mud and soft substrate, low center of gravity, thick waterproofed hide
- **Scale:** ~3.5–4m long, 800–1200kg
- **Locomotion:** Slow, stable wading walker; competent but unhurried swimmer
- **Diet:** Submerged and emergent aquatic vegetation, floodplain grasses
- **Primary sense:** Smell (tracking vegetation blooms and water depth changes)
- **Defense:** Herd numbers, retreat into deep water where land predators struggle
- **Social behavior:** Loose mixed-age herds that expand/contract with flood cycles
- **Reproduction:** Calves born in the dry season on firmer ground, mobile within days
- **Migration:** Follows the flood line — moves toward drying floodplain edges as water recedes, back toward the interior as rains return
- **Ecological relationships:** Opens grazing lanes in dense floodplain vegetation for smaller herbivores; a preferred prey item for Longjaw (007) at water's edge
- **Evolutionary rationale:** Broadback's lineage solved grazing on dry, low-quality forage with broad weight distribution; Marshtread solves a wetter version of the same problem by trading speed for buoyancy and mud tolerance
- **Major tradeoff:** Excellent in floodplain terrain / very poor sprint speed and stamina on firm dry ground
- **Why Hearth:** Hearth's vast interior floodplains and river plains give this niche real ecological weight; broad ranges keep populations connected across wet seasons
- **Prehistoric identity:** Reads as a slow, ancient wallowing giant — closer to a hippo-adjacent silhouette than anything reptilian, but heavy and primitive rather than sleek
- **Writer-facing description:** Marshtread moves through the shallows like a submerged boulder learning to walk, its passage marked by trails of disturbed mud and stripped waterweed. Herds gather at the flood margins in numbers that can stretch for miles, an easy target were it not for the water always within reach behind them.

**007 — Longjaw**
CANONICAL EXEMPLAR — SIGNATURE

- **Continent / Environment:** Hearth / forest-woodland
- **Primary function:** Apex terrestrial predator
- **Secondary functions:** Population control on Broadback and Marshtread herds; carcass provider for Rift-lineage scavengers where ranges overlap
- **Body plan:** Bipedal, deep narrow skull, serrated recurved teeth, long counterbalancing tail, reduced forelimbs used for grip rather than manipulation
- **Scale:** ~9–11m long, 4–5 tonnes
- **Locomotion:** Powerful walking gait with short-burst pursuit speed; not a long-distance runner
- **Diet:** Large-bodied herbivores, opportunistic carrion
- **Primary sense:** Binocular vision tuned for depth judgment in forest cover, secondary smell for tracking
- **Defense:** Size and bite force are its own deterrent; no natural predators as an adult
- **Predation strategy:** Ambush from cover in mixed woodland, short explosive charge, single disabling bite to the flank or neck
- **Social behavior:** Solitary outside of mating and cub-rearing
- **Reproduction:** Small litters, extended maternal care given the energy cost of raising young to a viable hunting size
- **Migration:** Territorial; follows herd migration routes rather than fixed seasonal movement
- **Ecological relationships:** Apex regulator of Hearth's large-herbivore biomass; its kills sustain Rift-scavenger-lineage opportunists at range overlaps
- **Evolutionary rationale:** Hearth's enormous connected herbivore populations create sustained selective pressure for a large-bodied ambush-and-burst predator; convergent with Earth's large bipedal predators because the physics of hunting very large prey favor the same solution
- **Major tradeoff:** Devastating short-range power / poor stamina, cannot sustain pursuit past a few hundred meters
- **Why Hearth:** Only a continent with broad, connected megaherbivore populations can support an apex predator this large on a sustainable prey base
- **Prehistoric identity:** The "T-Rex but not T-Rex" case — the silhouette is familiar because the ecological problem (killing very large prey efficiently) has a narrow set of good solutions, not because the world is imitating Earth
- **Writer-facing description:** Longjaw doesn't chase — it waits. A Broadback herd grazing the woodland edge might pass within thirty meters of one and never know, until the ground shakes once, hard, and doesn't again.

**008 — Rootsnout**
CANONICAL EXEMPLAR

- **Continent / Environment:** Hearth / dry forest-scrub
- **Primary function:** Omnivore / ground rooter
- **Secondary functions:** Soil turnover, seed dispersal, prey for juvenile Longjaw
- **Body plan:** Low, dense, tusked quadruped with a reinforced snout and thick shoulder hide
- **Scale:** ~2m long, 150–250kg
- **Locomotion:** Fast for its bulk over short distances; strong digging forelimbs
- **Diet:** Roots, tubers, fallen fruit, insects, carrion opportunistically
- **Primary sense:** Smell, used both for foraging underground and for early predator detection
- **Defense:** Tusks, thick hide over vitals, aggressive group response to threats against young
- **Social behavior:** Small family groups, matriarch-led
- **Reproduction:** Litters of 2–4, precocial young able to move with the group within a day
- **Migration:** Non-migratory; defends a stable home range
- **Ecological relationships:** Rooting behavior aerates dry-forest soil and exposes food for smaller scavengers following behind
- **Evolutionary rationale:** A dry, patchy resource base rewards flexible omnivory over specialization; tusks and digging are the direct answer to buried food that grazers can't access
- **Major tradeoff:** Broad diet flexibility / limited defense against a committed apex predator, relies on group aggression rather than size
- **Why Hearth:** Hearth's dry forest-scrub belt has patchy, seasonal resources that favor a generalist digger over a specialist
- **Prehistoric identity:** Convergent with Earth's tusked rooting mammals, but scaled and hided for a world with heavier gravity and larger regional predators
- **Writer-facing description:** A Rootsnout family working a dry hillside sounds like a small excavation crew — snorting, digging, tusks scraping stone — right up until the matriarch's head comes up and the whole group goes dead silent.

**009 — Duststriker**
CANONICAL EXEMPLAR

- **Continent / Environment:** Hearth / arid-desert
- **Primary function:** Burrowing ambush predator (small-prey specialist)
- **Secondary functions:** Population control on desert burrowers and ground-nesting prey
- **Body plan:** Low, sinuous, short-limbed with an armored dorsal ridge and a wide flattened skull for burrow work
- **Scale:** ~1.5m long, 15–25kg
- **Locomotion:** Fast digger, ambushes from a shallow burrow entrance rather than pursuing
- **Diet:** Small burrowing vertebrates, ground-nesting prey, insects
- **Primary sense:** Ground vibration detection, secondary heat/thermal sensitivity for locating buried prey
- **Defense:** Retreats into burrow network at first sign of larger predators; armored ridge protects the spine during retreat
- **Predation strategy:** Wait at burrow mouth, strike-and-drag prey underground
- **Social behavior:** Solitary, territorial burrow systems
- **Reproduction:** Small clutches of leathery eggs laid in a side-chamber of the burrow
- **Migration:** None; burrow systems are multi-generational
- **Ecological relationships:** Burrow networks are sometimes reused by smaller desert species once abandoned
- **Evolutionary rationale:** Extreme heat and water scarcity favor minimizing surface activity; ambush-from-shelter uses far less water and energy than pursuit hunting
- **Major tradeoff:** Extremely efficient in its burrow territory / nearly helpless in open pursuit and vulnerable if caught away from a burrow entrance
- **Why Hearth:** Hearth's rain-shadow interior basins produce true arid desert with the resource scarcity that favors this ambush-from-shelter strategy
- **Prehistoric identity:** Reads as an old, low, armored thing that has outlasted the desert around it rather than fighting it
- **Writer-facing description:** The desert looks empty until a patch of packed sand inhales and a Duststriker is gone again, prey already pulled below.

**010 — Ridgehorn**
CANONICAL EXEMPLAR — SIGNATURE

- **Continent / Environment:** Hearth / highland-mountain
- **Primary function:** Horned browser
- **Secondary functions:** Prey base for highland predators; erosion-resistant grazing pressure on alpine scrub
- **Body plan:** Stocky, short-legged quadruped with a heavy curved horn pair and thickened frontal skull plating
- **Scale:** ~2.5m at the shoulder-equivalent, 600–900kg
- **Locomotion:** Sure-footed climber on steep, broken terrain; slow on flat ground
- **Diet:** Tough alpine scrub, lichen-analogues, woody browse
- **Primary sense:** Vision, tuned for long sightlines across open highland terrain
- **Defense:** Horns and skull plating used in head-on defense and dominance contests
- **Social behavior:** Small bachelor and matriarchal groups; seasonal aggregation during rut
- **Reproduction:** Single calves, born on defensible high ground
- **Migration:** Elevational — moves to lower slopes in the harshest winter months, back up as snow (or its Hearth-climate equivalent seasonal cover) recedes
- **Ecological relationships:** Its grazing pressure keeps highland scrub from choking narrow mountain passes used by other species
- **Evolutionary rationale:** Old, eroded highland terrain rewards stability and defense over speed — there's nowhere to outrun a predator on a ridgeline, so the answer is armor and horn instead
- **Major tradeoff:** Formidable defensive weaponry and footing / slow and easy to corner on level ground
- **Why Hearth:** Hearth's major, long-eroded mountain systems provide the stable, large-scale highland range this species needs
- **Prehistoric identity:** Heavy horn, plated skull, and a build meant for standing its ground rather than running — the defining "big and dangerous but not fast" prehistoric silhouette
- **Writer-facing description:** Ridgehorn doesn't flee uphill so much as dare you to follow. Two males meeting on a narrow ledge in rutting season is a sound you hear well before you see it.

**011 — Farwader**
CANONICAL EXEMPLAR — SIGNATURE

- **Continent / Environment:** Shattered / coastal, cross-strait migratory
- **Primary function:** Migratory amphibious grazer
- **Secondary functions:** Genetic/ecological connector between isolated continental fragments
- **Body plan:** Elongated buoyant torso, webbed broad limbs, layered fat and dense fur for open-water insulation
- **Scale:** ~4m long, 700kg
- **Locomotion:** Strong sustained open-water swimmer; competent but slow walker on land
- **Diet:** Coastal and shallow-marine vegetation, supplemented by terrestrial forage during land phases
- **Primary sense:** Smell for detecting distant vegetation blooms across water; secondary vision for coastline navigation
- **Defense:** Group crossing behavior dilutes individual predation risk; land-phase group vigilance
- **Social behavior:** Forms large aggregations before a crossing, dispersing again once inland
- **Reproduction:** Timed so calves are strong enough to swim before the next seasonal crossing
- **Migration:** **[WORKING INFERENCE, built directly from the world-doc's stated concept]** — undertakes open-water crossings on the order of dozens of miles between continental fragments when an eastern range turns seasonally dry and resource-poor, moving toward wetter western fragments. Crossings carry real predation risk from Straitrunner (025) and significant mortality
- **Ecological relationships:** Its crossing corridor is the primary reason Straitrunner (025) patrols the strait seasonally; also a key prey source for coastal predators on both fragments it connects
- **Evolutionary rationale:** Fragmentation without full isolation creates a payoff for a species that can treat open water as a viable, if costly, migration route rather than a hard barrier
- **Major tradeoff:** Uniquely able to exploit resources across otherwise-isolated fragments / the crossing itself is dangerous and energetically brutal, and animals die on every attempt
- **Why Shattered:** This is the signature ecological concept for the continent — isolation that is real but not absolute, with a species built specifically to bridge it
- **Prehistoric identity:** A heavy, primitive-looking swimming grazer undertaking a genuinely dangerous open-ocean migration reads as ancient large-scale animal behavior rather than a modern one
- **Writer-facing description:** When the eastern range starts to fail, the Farwader herds gather on the shoreline for days, restless, before the first ones commit to the water. Not all of them reach the far coast; the ones that do arrive gaunt, exhausted, and ravenous.

**012 — Thicketback**
CANONICAL EXEMPLAR

- **Continent / Environment:** Shattered / forest-woodland
- **Primary function:** Herd grazer (regional variant demonstrating continental divergence from Broadback lineage)
- **Secondary functions:** Prey base for Nightclaw (013)
- **Body plan:** Smaller, more compact build than Broadback, with denser hide for forest thorn cover
- **Scale:** ~2.5m long, 300–400kg (notably smaller than mainland Hearth relatives)
- **Locomotion:** Agile in dense understory, better maneuverability than open-plains relatives
- **Diet:** Woodland browse, low shrubs, fallen fruit
- **Primary sense:** Hearing, adapted to detect predators through dense vegetation where sightlines are short
- **Defense:** Scatter-and-freeze response, relies on cover and stillness more than group defense
- **Social behavior:** Small family bands rather than large herds — forest cover doesn't support the huge aggregations Hearth's open grassland does
- **Reproduction:** Single or twin calves, hidden in dense cover for the first weeks
- **Migration:** Minimal; forest resources are more stable year-round than open floodplain
- **Ecological relationships:** Its smaller herd size and browsing pattern shape understory density in ways the larger Broadback lineage does not
- **Evolutionary rationale:** Classic island/fragment-rule body size shift — isolated forest populations descended from a larger open-range ancestor trend smaller where resources are patchier and predators ambush rather than course-hunt
- **Major tradeoff:** Excellent at evading ambush predators in dense cover / cannot sustain the large defensive herds that protect its mainland relatives
- **Why Shattered: fragmentation and regional divergence are exactly what should produce a smaller, more cover-adapted regional variant of a Hearth-type lineage
- **Prehistoric identity:** Same broad prehistoric grazer silhouette as its mainland relative, just built for a shrunken, forest-locked world
- **Writer-facing description:** Thicketback moves through undergrowth the way its Hearth cousins never could — quiet, low, gone into the thorns before you're sure it was there at all.

**013 — Nightclaw**
CANONICAL EXEMPLAR

- **Continent / Environment:** Shattered / forest-woodland
- **Primary function:** Ambush predator
- **Secondary functions:** Regulates Thicketback and smaller forest fauna populations
- **Body plan:** Compact, low-slung, short-limbed predator with retractable grasping claws and a short powerful bite; smaller overall than Hearth's Longjaw
- **Scale:** ~3.5m long, 150–200kg
- **Locomotion:** Climbs partway up leaning trunks and broken canopy for ambush vantage; fast over short ground distances
- **Diet:** Thicketback, smaller forest vertebrates
- **Primary sense:** Low-light vision, tuned for dusk and night hunting under forest canopy
- **Defense:** Avoidance and retreat into dense cover; not built to fight larger threats
- **Predation strategy:** Drop-and-grapple ambush from elevated cover, claws to pin, bite to finish
- **Social behavior:** Solitary
- **Reproduction:** Small litters denned in hollow logs or root cavities
- **Migration:** Territorial, non-migratory
- **Ecological relationships:** Keeps Thicketback herds small and dispersed rather than allowing large open-forest aggregations
- **Evolutionary rationale:** Island-fragment predators trend smaller alongside their prey base; dense forest also rewards climbing ambush over open pursuit
- **Major tradeoff:** Superior ambush precision in dense cover / cannot take large prey and cannot compete directly with a mainland-scale apex predator
- **Why Shattered:** A smaller, denser forest fragment with a smaller prey base produces a proportionally smaller apex predator — direct application of the continent's isolation/divergence logic
- **Prehistoric identity:** A compact, claw-first ambush predator reads as an old forest-floor lineage rather than a scaled-down copy of a mainland giant
- **Writer-facing description:** Nightclaw doesn't announce itself. The first sign is usually a Thicketback's alarm bark cutting off mid-sound.

**014 — Saltgrazer**
CANONICAL EXEMPLAR

- **Continent / Environment:** Shattered / coastal
- **Primary function:** Armored coastal browser
- **Secondary functions:** Controls tidal-zone vegetation, provides a defended prey option along vulnerable coastline
- **Body plan:** Heavy dorsal plating over a low quadruped frame, salt-tolerant skin glands
- **Scale:** ~3m long, 500kg
- **Locomotion:** Slow, deliberate walker across tidal flats and rocky shoreline
- **Diet:** Tidal-zone vegetation, coastal scrub
- **Primary sense:** Smell, tracks tide cycles via changing vegetation exposure
- **Defense:** Dorsal plating deters coastal predators; curls low to protect the underside when threatened
- **Social behavior:** Small loose groups along a shared stretch of coastline
- **Reproduction:** Nests laid above the high-tide line
- **Migration:** Local tidal-cycle movement only, not seasonal
- **Ecological relationships:** Heavy coastal dependence typical of Shattered fauna; shares range with Reefgrazer (026) at the waterline
- **Evolutionary rationale:** Strong ocean influence throughout Shattered pushes many lineages toward coastal dependence; armor answers the exposure risk of feeding in the open on a narrow tidal strip
- **Major tradeoff:** Well-defended and stable food source / very slow, cannot retreat quickly if caught away from the plating's effective defensive posture
- **Why Shattered:** Coastal dependence and strong ocean influence are core to this continent's ecological identity
- **Prehistoric identity:** A slow armored grazer working a tideline reads as ancient and deliberate — a species that has never needed to be fast
- **Writer-facing description:** Saltgrazer works the tideline like it has all the time in the world, because for the most part, it does.

**015 — Steamhide**
CANONICAL EXEMPLAR

- **Continent / Environment:** Rift / volcanic-disturbed terrain
- **Primary function:** Heat-tolerant opportunist / scavenger
- **Secondary functions:** Early exploiter of disturbance events (minor eruptions, ash-falls, flash floods) that other species avoid
- **Body plan:** Low, wide-footed quadruped with thick insulating hide and heat-resistant foot pads
- **Scale:** ~1.8m long, 60–90kg
- **Locomotion:** Steady walker across unstable, heat-affected ground others can't cross comfortably
- **Diet:** Carrion, disturbance-exposed vegetation and invertebrates, opportunistic small prey
- **Primary sense:** Smell, tuned to volatile compounds released by geothermal activity and carrion alike
- **Defense:** Retreats onto ground too hot or unstable for most predators to follow
- **Social behavior:** Loose, opportunistic aggregations around a resource event; otherwise solitary
- **Reproduction:** Nests dug near warm ground for natural incubation
- **Migration:** Follows volcanic/disturbance activity across the region rather than a fixed seasonal route
- **Ecological relationships:** First responder to volcanic disturbance events, clearing carrion before it can spread disease through less heat-tolerant fauna
- **Evolutionary rationale:** Rift's volcanic disturbance zones are a real, recurring resource nobody else can safely exploit — heat tolerance turns danger into a private feeding ground
- **Major tradeoff:** Access to an entire resource category no competitor can touch / heat-adapted physiology costs it thermoregulatory efficiency everywhere else, tires fast in cooler terrain
- **Why Rift:** Only a continent with genuine active volcanic disturbance produces the selective pressure for true heat-tolerance specialization
- **Prehistoric identity:** A low, heat-scarred opportunist walking through ash reads as primal survivalism rather than spectacle
- **Writer-facing description:** Where the ground cracks and steams and everything else backs away, Steamhide walks in, because it knows exactly what that means for dinner.

**016 — Highvault**
CANONICAL EXEMPLAR — SIGNATURE

- **Continent / Environment:** Rift / highland-mountain
- **Primary function:** Horned climbing browser
- **Secondary functions:** Connects highland and escarpment ecosystems; prey base for Thermalglider (033)
- **Body plan:** Long-limbed, lighter-framed than Hearth's Ridgehorn, with a single curved horn and exceptional grip pads
- **Scale:** ~2m long, 300kg
- **Locomotion:** Superb climber across escarpments and fault-line cliffs; the most vertically capable large herbivore in the library
- **Diet:** Cliffside scrub, hardy highland vegetation
- **Primary sense:** Vision, precise depth perception for cliff navigation
- **Defense:** Escape onto terrain predators can't follow, secondary horn defense if cornered
- **Social behavior:** Small family groups holding a stretch of escarpment
- **Reproduction:** Kids born able to stand and climb within hours — a hard requirement on cliff terrain
- **Migration:** Moves between highland and lowland Rift terrain seasonally, using the extreme elevation gradients unique to Rift geography
- **Ecological relationships:** Its cliff-edge grazing keeps escarpment vegetation from destabilizing loose slopes
- **Evolutionary rationale:** Rift's fault valleys and escarpments put contrasting habitats close together — this species solves the highland half by prioritizing vertical agility over the bulk-and-armor strategy Hearth's highland species use
- **Major tradeoff:** Unmatched climbing ability and escape terrain / thin build offers little defense if actually caught on level ground
- **Why Rift:** Rift's dramatic short-distance elevation gradients are the reason this kind of extreme vertical specialist exists here rather than on Hearth's older, more gradual mountains
- **Prehistoric identity:** A horned climber moving across a sheer fault escarpment reads as ancient mountain-adapted megafauna, distinct from Hearth's slower armored highland strategy
- **Writer-facing description:** Highvault crosses terrain that looks like it shouldn't hold anything larger than a bird, and does it without slowing down.

**017 — Reedstalker**
CANONICAL EXEMPLAR

- **Continent / Environment:** Rift / wetland
- **Primary function:** Wading ambush predator (contrast to the fully aquatic Rift river ambush predator, 004)
- **Secondary functions:** Controls wetland-margin prey populations
- **Body plan:** Tall, long-legged wader with a spear-like beak-jaw and long flexible neck
- **Scale:** ~2.5m tall, 80kg
- **Locomotion:** Slow, deliberate wading stalk; fast strike speed only in the neck and head
- **Diet:** Fish, amphibious prey, wetland invertebrates
- **Primary sense:** Vision, exceptional at detecting movement through disturbed water
- **Defense:** Retreats into dense reed cover; long legs allow it to outdistance most wetland predators through deep mud
- **Predation strategy:** Motionless stalk followed by a single explosive neck-strike
- **Social behavior:** Solitary, defends a stretch of wetland margin
- **Reproduction:** Platform nests built in dense reed stands above flood level
- **Migration:** Follows wetland water-level changes locally rather than long-distance movement
- **Ecological relationships:** Occupies the wading-predator niche that Rift's wetlands support alongside the fully submerged ambush strategy of Candidate 004 — direct demonstration of multiple evolutionary solutions to a similar environment
- **Evolutionary rationale:** Rift's extensive wetland systems support more than one predatory strategy at once; a wading, strike-from-above hunter avoids direct competition with the submerged ambush specialist sharing its habitat
- **Major tradeoff:** Precise, low-energy strike-hunting / limited to shallow water, cannot pursue prey into deep channels the way Candidate 004 can
- **Why Rift:** Rift's wetland systems are extensive enough to support two distinct ambush strategies without direct competition
- **Prehistoric identity:** A tall, spear-jawed wading hunter reads as an old wetland lineage, convergent with wading hunters on Earth for the same functional reasons
- **Writer-facing description:** Reedstalker can stand in one place long enough that the wetland forgets it's there — until the water breaks once, and something that was swimming a second ago isn't anymore.

**018 — Basingrazer**
CANONICAL EXEMPLAR — SIGNATURE

- **Continent / Environment:** Rift / grassland (valley floor)
- **Primary function:** Major grazer
- **Secondary functions:** Primary prey base sustaining Rift's valley-floor predator guild
- **Body plan:** Long-necked, broad-chested quadruped built for reaching both ground-level and mid-height woody browse
- **Scale:** ~7m long (including neck), 3 tonnes
- **Locomotion:** Steady long-stride walker, herd movement across open valley floor
- **Diet:** Mixed grazing and browsing depending on season
- **Primary sense:** Vision, elevated head height gives long sightlines across the valley floor for predator detection
- **Defense:** Herd numbers and size; young protected at herd center
- **Social behavior:** Large mixed herds, the biggest aggregations Rift's terrain supports
- **Reproduction:** Seasonal calving synchronized to peak vegetation availability
- **Migration:** Moves the length of the rift valley floor tracking seasonal rainfall bands
- **Ecological relationships:** Anchors the entire valley-floor food web; its migration timing shapes predator movement patterns across the whole region
- **Evolutionary rationale:** Rift valley floors are long, resource-rich corridors between extreme terrain types — a large-bodied, long-necked mixed feeder can exploit both grass and woody browse across that whole corridor without needing to specialize
- **Major tradeoff:** Extremely flexible diet and impressive reach / an enormous body with correspondingly enormous resource needs, vulnerable during lean seasons if migration timing fails
- **Why Rift:** Rift's long valley floor is a distinct enough resource corridor to justify its own dedicated major grazer, separate from Hearth's Broadback
- **Prehistoric identity:** A long-necked mixed-feeding giant crossing an open valley floor is one of the library's clearest "old world" anchor images
- **Writer-facing description:** A Basingrazer herd crossing the valley floor at dusk is visible from the ridgeline for hours before it arrives and hours after it's gone — a slow-moving horizon of its own.

**019 — Plateauhorn**
CANONICAL EXEMPLAR — SIGNATURE

- **Continent / Environment:** Lost / grassland-plateau
- **Primary function:** Major herbivore
- **Secondary functions:** Anchors Lost's open-plateau food web independently of any Hearth/Shattered/Rift lineage

- **Migration:** None; fixed canopy territory
- **Ecological relationships:** A minor but real check on Lost's forest insect-analogue population, filling a niche that on the other continents is occupied by true powered fliers
- **Evolutionary rationale:** True powered flight is one evolutionary answer to the aerial niche, but it is not the only one — Lost's deep isolation preserved a gliding lineage that never crossed into powered flight, filling the same general niche through an earlier-stage aerial solution
- **Major tradeoff:** Low energy cost, quiet, effective in dense canopy / no true powered flight, entirely dependent on height and canopy structure to move at all
- **Why Lost:** A perfect example of the continent's mandate to feel different due to independent history rather than invented physics — glide-only aerial locomotion is a real, primitive evolutionary stage that Lost's isolation simply never pushed past
- **Prehistoric identity:** Explicitly primitive by design — a pre-powered-flight aerial form is about as "deep time" as the aerial category gets
- **Writer-facing description:** Duskveil doesn't fly so much as fall on purpose, from one tree to the next, all night, and it is astonishingly good at it.
