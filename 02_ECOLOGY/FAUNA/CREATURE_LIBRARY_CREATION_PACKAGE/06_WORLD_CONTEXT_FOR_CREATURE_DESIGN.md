# MYTHROOT WORLDS — WORLD CONTEXT FOR CREATURE DESIGN
## Prehistoric World Creature-Building Reference v0.1

**Purpose:** Give the creature-building process enough world context to extrapolate responsibly without repeatedly needing the entire worldbuilding archive.

**Important:** This is a working design summary. The full Master World Bible and continental dossiers remain the higher-detail references. Do not silently turn an inference into established canon.

---

# 1. PROJECT IDENTITY

**Mythroot** is the project/company umbrella.

The prehistoric world being developed here is **currently unnamed**.

Do not name the world unless the project explicitly names it later.

The world is intended to feel like an **alternate evolutionary history of a prehistoric planet**, not a science-fiction alien world and not a generic fantasy setting.

The guiding principle is:

> **Different because evolution took a different path, not different because the planet ignores biology.**

Science is the default grounding system, but the broader Mythroot project may deliberately create other worlds where science is not the governing rule. This particular world is the scientifically grounded prehistoric experiment.

---

# 2. PLANETARY FOUNDATION

The current working world parameters include:

- approximately **1.25g surface gravity**;
- **32-hour planetary days**;
- a **K-type star** with a somewhat warmer/oranger spectral character than Earth's Sun;
- **two moons**;
- a large moon farther away and a smaller moon closer to the planet, intended to appear relatively similar in the sky;
- the moons have meaningful interaction with the planet's tides and geological systems, but they do **not** simply push continents around;
- day/night is not assumed to be a simple 16-hour-light/16-hour-dark split;
- moonlight can make portions of the night visibly illuminated;
- the world has active tectonic/geological systems and large continental movement over deep time.

These parameters are constraints and pressures, not automatic explanations for every biological trait.

---

# 3. VISUAL WORLD IDENTITY

The planet should look like an **old, robust, prehistoric world**.

Vegetation trends toward:
- short rather than towering growth;
- broad, thick, sturdy forms;
- dense vegetation;
- robust low forests/woodlands;
- thick bushes and vegetation swaths rather than isolated delicate plants;
- extensive grasses/grass-like vegetation where climate permits;
- regional variation in vegetation color and pigmentation.

The world should not be covered uniformly in purple plants.

Some regions may have unusual coloration due to climate, geography, light environment, pigments, or evolutionary history, while other regions can look much more familiar.

The overall visual principle is:

> **Things grow thick rather than impossibly tall.**

This is partly aesthetic, but it is also compatible with the project's interest in mechanical constraints, gravity, climate, and robust body plans.

---

# 4. CONTINENTAL SYSTEM

There are four major continental systems.

## HEARTH

Hearth is the mostly intact major continental landmass.

General identity:
- enormous connected terrestrial ecosystems;
- broad interior;
- major mountain systems;
- older eroded terrain;
- large watersheds;
- river plains;
- wetlands;
- coastal plains;
- interior basins;
- rain-shadow regions.

Ecological implication:
- broad ranges;
- large populations;
- long terrestrial migrations;
- strong competition;
- relatively strong gene flow;
- less isolation-driven divergence than Shattered or Lost.

Hearth is useful as the **baseline continental ecosystem**.

---

## SHATTERED

Shattered is a fragmented continental system made of several enormous continental-scale pieces rather than a field of tiny islands.

General identity:
- major continental fragments;
- deep marine basins;
- major straits;
- independent mountain systems;
- isolated interior basins;
- wet and dry regional contrasts;
- strong ocean influence.

Ecological implication:
- geographic isolation;
- regional divergence;
- local specialization;
- altered body size in some lineages;
- coastal/marine dependence;
- island/fragment migration.

A major future/working ecological concept is an **amphibious migratory species** that travels between major fragments when seasonal food shortages make migration worthwhile. One working scenario involves an eastern region becoming extremely dry and resource-poor seasonally, prompting migration toward wetter western regions. The open-water journey may be on the order of dozens of miles and carries real predation/mortality risk.

This is an example of the desired design philosophy:

> **The animal migrates because its ecological situation makes migration worthwhile, not because "amphibious creature" sounds cool.**

---

## RIFT

Rift is an enormous continental system actively separating into two major halves.

General identity:
- long irregular rift system;
- fault valleys;
- escarpments;
- rift lakes;
- inland seas;
- wetlands;
- volcanic regions;
- highlands;
- dry basins;
- extreme environmental gradients.

Ecological implication:
- highland specialization;
- lowland specialization;
- freshwater specialization;
- wetland specialization;
- volcanic/disturbance adaptation;
- rapid divergence across barriers;
- organisms moving between sharply different environments.

Rift is an especially useful setting for specialized creatures because many contrasting habitats occur relatively close together.

---

## LOST

Lost is one enormous continuous continent isolated from the main continental cluster by an immense ocean.

Working history:
- isolation on the order of hundreds of millions of years, with a current working estimate around **700 million–1 billion years**;
- geology and life continued evolving independently during that isolation.

General identity:
- enormous interior;
- independent mountain systems;
- ancient rounded ranges;
- younger mountain systems;
- plateaus;
- major basins;
- inland lakes/wetlands;
- long ocean margins;
- regional volcanic/active areas.

Ecological implication:
- deep-time evolutionary independence;
- adaptive radiation;
- unusual body plans;
- ancient lineage persistence;
- convergence with Earth-like functions;
- unrelated organisms filling ecological roles familiar from Earth.

**Lost should not be weird just to be weird.**

Its organisms should be different because they have had an extraordinarily long independent evolutionary history.

# 5. CLIMATE LOGIC

Climate is expected to emerge from:
- latitude;
- planetary circulation;
- ocean currents;
- continentality;
- mountain ranges;
- elevation;
- water distribution;
- seasonal patterns;
- tectonic geography.

The project does not want arbitrary biome placement when physical geography provides a better explanation.

Important working environments include:
- grassland;
- wetland/floodplain;
- forest/woodland;
- dry forest/scrub;
- arid/semi-arid basin;
- highland/mountain;
- volcanic/disturbed terrain;
- coastal environments;
- rivers;
- lakes/inland seas;
- open ocean;
- deep water.

Regional climate should strongly influence:
- resource availability;
- water dependence;
- migration;
- reproduction;
- thermoregulation;
- body size;
- locomotion;
- predator/prey relationships.

# 6. EVOLUTIONARY GROUNDING

The project is specifically interested in **evolutionary plausibility**.

Important principles:

### Convergent evolution
Unrelated organisms can evolve similar solutions to similar ecological problems.

Therefore, a creature may legitimately resemble an Earth crocodilian, snake, tyrannosaur, boar, rodent, bird, etc., if the ecological function and physical constraints make the resemblance plausible.

### Tradeoffs
Evolution does not give an organism every advantage.

A strong adaptation should have costs.

Examples:
- powerful swimming / poor land mobility;
- heavy armor / reduced speed;
- huge body / high resource demand;
- extreme specialization / narrow diet;
- powerful sensory system / energetic cost.

### Function over taxonomy
Do not begin with:
> "We need a crocodile."

Begin with:
> "We need a shallow-water ambush predator."

Then determine what evolutionary solutions could perform that job.

### Predator/prey arms races
Predation can favor:
- speed;
- acceleration;
- maneuverability;
- armor;
- camouflage;
- sensory detection;
- group behavior;
- chemical defenses;
- defensive weapons;
- behavioral flexibility.

### Seasonality
Seasonal resource changes can favor:
- migration;
- dormancy;
- reproductive timing;
- food storage;
- seasonal body/behavior changes;
- concentration around water.

---

# 7. PREHISTORIC SCALE

The project wants **large, dangerous, impressive animals**, but not routine kaiju.

Desired feeling:
> "A group of people with primitive weapons could potentially kill this, but holy shit, it would be a terrible idea."

The world can contain:
- large lizards;
- big snakes;
- heavy herbivores;
- dangerous predators;
- massive-but-plausible beasts;
- robust burrowers;
- amphibious predators;
- large aerial organisms where physics allows;
- large arthropods where atmospheric/ecological conditions justify them.

Do not routinely produce 30–60 foot monsters.

"Big" should usually mean **ecologically substantial**, not fantastically gigantic.

# 8. CURRENT CANONICAL EXAMPLES

These are early exemplar concepts that demonstrate the intended design language.

## Broadback — Candidate #001
**Hearth / seasonal grassland-woodland / major grazer**

Large, low-slung herd grazer with a broad robust body, broad weight-distributing feet, efficient fibrous digestion, seasonal movement, and strong predator vigilance.

Ecological principle:
**large low-quality vegetation → large herbivore biomass → predator/scavenger support.**

## Rift scavenger — Candidate #002
**Rift / herd-associated terrestrial environments / scavenger**

Turkey-sized, two-legged, furred opportunist that follows large herds and exploits carcasses and scraps.

Primary sense:
**smell.**

It competes with its own species over valuable food but retreats from larger predators.

Its habitat follows large-herd ecosystems rather than a single fixed biome:
- grassland;
- seasonal woodland/grassland;
- wetland margins;
- floodplains;
- dry-season water concentrations.

## Shattered burrower — Candidate #003
**Shattered / wetland-floodplain context / ecosystem engineer**

Social herbivore living in groups of roughly 50.

Key traits:
- extensive interconnected burrow systems;
- long front claws for excavation;
- chemical alarm/deterrent secretion;
- group behavior.

The chemical defense is intended as a **deterrent**, not a toxin or lethal weapon. It may temporarily irritate exposed sensory tissues of nearby predators, buying the group time to retreat underground.

Its burrowing can alter:
- soil;
- water movement;
- shelter availability;
- vegetation;
- habitat for other species.

## Rift river ambush predator — Candidate #004
**Rift / warm riverine-wetland / freshwater ambush predator**

Approximately 8–10 feet in working concept.

Low, heavily muscled semi-aquatic body with:
- four sturdy legs;
- powerful laterally flattened tail;
- long narrow jaw;
- backward-pointing teeth;
- eyes/nostrils positioned for partial submergence;
- explosive aquatic acceleration.

Juveniles occupy a smaller aquatic predator niche.

Adults lay eggs in elevated mud nests and specialize in larger prey near water.

Key tradeoff:
**excellent aquatic ambush ability / mediocre terrestrial mobility.**

## Lost serpent predator — Candidate #005
**Lost / warm arid-semi-arid basin / ambush predator**

Long flexible serpent-like body with four functional limbs.

Front limbs are specialized for grappling prey; rear limbs provide leverage/anchoring.

Primary hunting sense:
**ground-transmitted vibration detection.**

Secondary senses remain functional:
- smell;
- vision.

Hunting sequence:
**detect vibration → orient → launch → grapple → coil → bite/constrict.**

Nocturnal/crepuscular activity and water conservation are favored in the working concept.

Key tradeoff:
**excellent close-range ambush/grappling predator / poor long-distance terrestrial pursuit.**

These are examples, not a complete locked taxonomy. Their exact names and final details can change during development.

# 9. ECOLOGICAL DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

The world should contain **many implied species** beyond the canonical library.

The canonical library exists to show:
- what functions exist;
- what body plans are possible;
- what evolutionary solutions are common;
- what makes each continent different;
- what "normal" looks like;
- what "unusual" looks like.

The separate creator framework allows future writers/designers to extrapolate additional organisms.

Do not feel pressure to make every plausible creature explicit.

---

# 10. CREATURE CREATION RULE

When inventing a creature, ask:

1. **Where does it live?**
2. **What climate/environment does it occupy?**
3. **What ecological job does it perform?**
4. **What evolutionary pressures act on that job?**
5. **What solutions are favored?**
6. **What tradeoffs come with those solutions?**
7. **Why does this lineage exist here rather than somewhere else?**
8. **What makes it visually/ecologically prehistoric-esque?**
9. **Is it distinct enough to deserve inclusion?**

If the answer is coherent, build the animal.

---

# 11. WHAT NOT TO DO

Do not:
- create arbitrary monsters;
- add anatomy without function;
- make every organism gigantic;
- make every organism reptilian;
- avoid familiar silhouettes simply to appear original;
- make Lost alien for the sake of alienness;
- make every continent's fauna completely unique;
- force every climate to have the same number of species;
- fill every ecological niche with a separate fully documented creature;
- name the world.

The goal is:

> **A believable prehistoric biosphere that evolved differently from Earth.**
