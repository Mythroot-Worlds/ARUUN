# MYTHROOT WORLDS
# Prehistoric World — Creature Necessity Sheet v0.1

**Purpose:** Determine which creatures need to be explicitly designed during the initial worldbuilding pass.

**Core rule:** We are not trying to catalog an entire planet's biodiversity. We are creating enough representative organisms to make the ecology understandable, establish the world's biological identity, and provide examples from which future creators can extrapolate.

**Primary priority:** Ecological function.

**Secondary organization:** Environment — Ground, Freshwater, Marine, Air.

---

# 1. PRODUCTION LIMIT

## Initial target

Aim for approximately **30–40 fully developed exemplar creatures across the entire world**, not 30–40 per continent.

This is a production target, not a biological claim.

The exact number may move slightly if ecological coverage requires it.

### Rough working distribution

- **Ground:** ~15–20
- **Freshwater / Marine:** ~7–10
- **Air:** ~5–8
- **Specialized / cross-environment organisms:** several, as needed

Some organisms can satisfy multiple functions and therefore count toward more than one category.

## What we are NOT doing

We are not creating:
- every species;
- every genus;
- complete taxonomic families;
- dozens of visually similar variants;
- a complete food web for every region;
- a representative animal for every possible niche.

The unknown ecology creation framework exists specifically so the world can support additional creatures later without requiring us to design them all now.

---

# 2. THE THREE-TIER SYSTEM

## TIER 1 — ESSENTIAL FUNCTIONS

These functions **must be represented** somewhere in the initial creature set.

If a major ecological function has no example, the world may feel incomplete.

### Ground
- major grazer
- major browser
- small prey/consumer
- omnivore
- small predator
- large predator
- scavenger
- ecosystem engineer
- burrower/shelter maker
- dangerous specialist

### Freshwater
- aquatic primary consumer
- filter feeder
- bottom feeder
- aquatic prey species
- freshwater predator
- amphibious/transitional organism

### Marine
- schooling prey
- marine grazer/filter feeder
- marine predator
- large marine consumer
- scavenger
- coastal/intertidal organism

### Air
- aerial consumer
- pollinator/nectar or equivalent specialist
- seed/fruit disperser
- aerial predator
- scavenger
- major migratory flyer

Not every bullet requires its own species. One organism may satisfy multiple functions.

---

# 3. TIER 2 — SIGNATURE EVOLUTIONARY SOLUTIONS

These are not necessarily required to close an ecological hole.

They exist to demonstrate **how evolution solves problems in this world** and to establish the setting's biological identity.

Examples of desired possibilities:

- amphibious long-distance island migrant;
- giant lizard or other large reptilian analogue;
- unusually large rodent-like consumer;
- massive boar-like omnivore;
- unusual defensive herbivore;
- specialized volcanic-environment organism;
- unusual burrower;
- extreme wetland specialist;
- strange but plausible aerial predator;
- unusual Lost-continent lineage;
- convergent organism that resembles an Earth animal without sharing its ancestry.

These are the creatures people should remember.

---

# 4. TIER 3 — BACKGROUND DIVERSITY

Do not fully design these unless they become important.

Instead, establish them at the ecological-family/archetype level.

Example:

> Small ground herbivores are diverse throughout the main continental systems, ranging from solitary burrowers to social grazing species.

That statement can establish biodiversity without requiring ten separate creatures.

The creator framework can later generate individual organisms within that established ecological space.

---

# 5. CONTINENTAL COVERAGE

We should **not** distribute creatures evenly.

The continents have different ecological identities.

## Hearth

Role:
**Baseline connected ecosystem.**

Need enough organisms to establish:
- broad connected food webs;
- major grazing/browsing systems;
- large predators;
- scavengers;
- river/wetland relationships;
- long terrestrial migrations.

Hearth should provide several of the world's baseline ancestral ecological forms.

## Shattered

Role:
**Regional variation and fragmentation.**

Need examples demonstrating:
- island/fragment specialization;
- coastal and marine dependence;
- regional variants of shared ancestral lineages;
- seasonal migration between fragments;
- isolation-driven divergence.

The amphibious island migrant belongs here as a major candidate.

## Rift

Role:
**Environmental specialization and transition.**

Need examples demonstrating:
- wetland/lake specialization;
- dry rift-floor adaptation;
- highland adaptation;
- volcanic/disturbance adaptation;
- organisms crossing sharply different environments.

## Lost

Role:
**Deep-time independent evolution.**

Need fewer examples, but they should be more evolutionarily divergent.

Need examples demonstrating:
- familiar ecological function with unfamiliar body plan;
- convergent evolution;
- ancient lineage persistence;
- replacement of familiar functions by unrelated lineages;
- radically different predator/prey relationships.

Lost should not simply have "weirder-looking animals."

---

# 6. FUNCTION-FIRST CREATURE DESIGN

For every creature candidate, start here:

## What job does it perform?

Examples:
- converts grass into large-animal biomass;
- controls small herbivores;
- processes carcasses;
- modifies soil;
- pollinates plants;
- disperses seeds;
- filters aquatic nutrients;
- controls shoreline populations;
- moves nutrients between ecosystems;
- migrates between seasonal resource zones.

Only after the function is established do we decide:

**Ground / Freshwater / Marine / Air**
→ body plan
→ size
→ behavior
→ defenses
→ reproduction
→ appearance
→ regional distribution.

---

# 7. COVERAGE TEST

Before approving a new creature, ask:

### A. Is there an ecological need?
Does the creature fill a function we need represented?

### B. Is that function already adequately represented?
If yes, does the new creature demonstrate a meaningfully different solution?

### C. Does it teach us something?
Does it establish:
- a new body plan;
- a new behavior;
- a new predator/prey relationship;
- a new environmental adaptation;
- a new continental identity?

### D. Is it visually or conceptually distinct?
If it is essentially "Creature #4 but slightly bigger," it probably does not need full development.

### E. Does it belong here?
Its environment, resources, anatomy, and behavior must fit the world.

---

# 8. THE DUPLICATION TEST

We stop designing a category when additional creatures primarily add:

- another color;
- another size;
- another set of horns;
- another coat/scale pattern;
- another nearly identical predator;
- another version of an existing ecological role without a meaningful difference.

Regional variants can still exist, but they do not automatically deserve individual design.

---

# 9. THE "ENOUGH" TEST

We are done with the initial creature pass when:

1. Every Tier 1 essential function has at least one convincing representative.
2. Major environmental systems have enough fauna to feel functional.
3. Each continent has several biologically meaningful examples.
4. The world demonstrates multiple evolutionary solutions to the same ecological problem.
5. We have enough memorable signature organisms to establish biological identity.
6. The creator framework can plausibly generate the missing background diversity.
7. New creatures would mostly be elaborations rather than foundational additions.

At that point:

**STOP.**

Do not continue merely because Earth has more species.

---

# 10. CREATURE COUNT IS NOT SPECIES COUNT

The 30–40 target means:

> **30–40 organisms we deliberately understand well enough to use as canonical examples.**

The world itself can contain:
- hundreds;
- thousands;
- millions of species.

Those implied organisms do not need individual documentation.

The setting should feel larger than the creature list.

# 11. REQUIRED CREATURE DESIGN TEMPLATE

Every fully developed exemplar should eventually receive:

### Identity
- Working name
- Scientific/descriptive designation if useful
- Continental distribution
- Environment

### Function
- Primary ecological role
- Secondary ecological roles
- What ecological problem it solves

### Ecology
- Diet
- Predators
- Competitors
- Symbiotic/beneficial relationships
- Ecosystem effects

### Biology
- Body plan
- Approximate size
- Locomotion
- Senses
- Defense
- Reproduction
- Lifespan/general life history

### Behavior
- Solitary/social
- Territoriality
- Migration
- Activity pattern
- Response to humans

### Geography
- Climate requirements
- Habitat requirements
- Range
- Regional variants

### Human relevance
- Prey/resource
- Threat
- Cultural significance
- Possible domestication relationship
- Useful materials

### Design rationale
A short explanation of **why this organism makes sense in this world.**

---

# 12. CROSS-ENVIRONMENT CREATURES

Some creatures should deliberately cross environmental categories.

Examples:
- amphibious species;
- coastal hunters;
- aquatic animals that travel over land;
- flying animals that feed primarily in water;
- terrestrial animals that migrate through water;
- organisms whose life cycle uses multiple environments.

These are valuable because they connect otherwise separate ecological systems.

---

# 13. SHARED LINEAGES VS. UNIQUE CREATURES

The three primary continental systems should often share ancestral lineages.

A creature does not automatically need four continental versions.

Instead:

**Shared lineage**
→ regional population
→ environmental pressure
→ adaptation
→ possible regional variant.

Only develop the variant as its own exemplar when the difference is ecologically or narratively meaningful.

Lost has much more freedom because its evolutionary history is independent.

---

# 14. CURRENT PRIORITY ORDER

We will work through:

## GROUND
1. Essential functions
2. Signature solutions
3. Continental distribution
4. Stop when coverage is sufficient

## FRESHWATER
1. Essential functions
2. Signature solutions
3. Rift-specific opportunities
4. Stop when coverage is sufficient

## MARINE
1. Essential functions
2. Signature solutions
3. Shattered migration/coastal opportunities
4. Stop when coverage is sufficient

## AIR
1. Essential functions
2. Signature solutions
3. Continental/migratory opportunities
4. Stop when coverage is sufficient

The order is **Ground → Freshwater → Marine → Air**, while ecological function remains the primary organizing principle.

---

# 15. FIRST CREATURE-BUILDING QUESTION

Before inventing the first organism, ask:

> **What is the first ecological function that this world absolutely needs us to represent?**

Do not name the animal yet.

Define the job first.

Then determine:
**where it performs that job → what resources it uses → what pressures act on it → what evolutionary solution could plausibly arise.**

Only then do we build the creature.
