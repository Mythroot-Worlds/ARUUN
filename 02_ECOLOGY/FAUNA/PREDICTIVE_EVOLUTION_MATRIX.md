# MYTHROOT WORLDS — PREHISTORIC WORLD
# Predictive Evolution Matrix v0.1

**Purpose:** Compact reference for creating canonical exemplar creatures.

## Core rule
The matrix does not decide what a creature must become. It identifies plausible or favored evolutionary solutions from four inputs:

**CONTINENT → CLIMATE → FUNCTION → EVOLUTIONARY POSSIBILITIES**

The final creature remains a creative decision.

---

## 1. CONTINENT

### HEARTH
**Primary pressure:** connected ecosystems and large continuous ranges.
- broad geographic ranges
- long migrations
- large populations
- strong competition
- relatively strong gene flow
- large-bodied niches where resources permit
- less isolation-driven divergence

### SHATTERED
**Primary pressure:** fragmentation and isolation.
- regional divergence
- fragment-specific adaptations
- founder effects/bottlenecks
- altered body size
- resource specialization
- coastal/marine dependence
- migration between fragments where possible

### RIFT
**Primary pressure:** extreme environmental transitions.
- highland/lowland specialization
- freshwater and wetland specialization
- volcanic/disturbance adaptation
- divergence across geographic barriers
- organisms crossing sharply contrasting environments

### LOST
**Primary pressure:** deep-time evolutionary independence.
- adaptive radiation
- divergent body plans
- ancient lineage persistence
- convergent solutions
- unrelated lineages replacing familiar functions
- highly specialized regional organisms

Lost should be biologically unfamiliar because of evolutionary history, not because its physics are inherently alien.

## 3. FUNCTION

Function is the primary design priority.

### Ground
grazer; browser; mixed feeder; root/fruit consumer; small prey; omnivore; burrower; small predator; pursuit predator; ambush predator; pack/social predator; scavenger; ecosystem engineer; dangerous specialist; migratory connector.

### Freshwater
filter feeder; aquatic grazer; bottom feeder; schooling prey; aquatic omnivore; aquatic predator; ambush predator; scavenger; ecosystem engineer; amphibious/transitional organism.

### Marine
filter feeder; grazer; schooling prey; benthic feeder; marine omnivore; pursuit predator; ambush predator; apex/near-apex predator; scavenger; coastal/intertidal specialist; migratory connector.

### Air
aerial consumer; pollinator/nectar specialist; seed/fruit disperser; aerial insect/arthropod consumer; aerial predator; scavenger; aquatic-prey hunter; long-distance migrant.

One organism may perform multiple functions.

## 5. DERIVED REGIONAL PRESSURES

These are consequences of the selected climate/location, not additional required inputs.

### Wetland
saturated ground; flooding; standing water; dense vegetation; seasonal water-level changes.

### Dry forest
seasonal drought; patchy resources; disturbance/fire potential; shade competition.

### Grassland
open visibility; abundant low vegetation; grazing pressure; pursuit predation.

### Highland
lower temperatures; elevation; steep terrain; strong precipitation gradients.

### Coastal
salt exposure; tides; storms; marine food access.

### Volcanic/disturbed
unstable substrate; young soils; repeated disturbance; rapid succession.

---

## 6. CONTINENTAL BIASES

### Hearth
Favor broad ranges, large connected populations, long migrations, strong competition, less isolation, large connected food webs.

### Shattered
Favor isolation, regional specialization, altered body size, coastal/marine access, island migration, local resource specialization.

### Rift
Favor wet/dry specialization, elevation, freshwater dependence, volcanic disturbance, rapid habitat transitions, lake/river specialization.

### Lost
Favor deep-time divergence, adaptive radiation, convergent evolution, unrelated lineages filling familiar functions, unusual body plans, highly specialized ecosystems.

---

## 7. POSSIBILITY RATINGS

**FAVORED:** strongly supported by the combination of conditions.

**PLAUSIBLE:** reasonable, but not especially demanded.

**DIFFICULT:** possible, but requires strong compensating advantages.

**POOR FIT:** conflicts with the requirements and should generally be rejected unless a strong explanation exists.

---

## 8. EXAMPLE

**Request:** Large grazer — seasonal lowland wetland — Shattered.

**Inputs**
- Continent: Shattered
- Climate: seasonal wetland
- Function: large grazer

**Pressures**
- abundant wet-season vegetation
- saturated ground
- periodic drying/flooding
- predators
- fragmented geography
- seasonal resource collapse

**Favored possibilities**
- broad weight-distributing feet
- strong legs/joints
- efficient fibrous-plant digestion
- wading ability
- seasonal movement
- strong predator detection
- large body size for predator resistance

**Plausible possibilities**
- semi-aquatic lifestyle
- aquatic-plant feeding
- social herding
- seasonal reproduction
- short-distance fragment migration

**Difficult possibilities**
- extremely tall, lightly built body
- extreme dependence on a single dry-season food
- high water requirements where permanent water is absent

Then we choose the combination that produces the actual creature.

## 10. PHILOSOPHY

The matrix answers:

> **What could evolution plausibly do here?**

It does not answer:

> **What should the creature be?**

Science creates boundaries. Ecology creates pressures. Function creates the job. Continent and climate create context.

**Creative selection creates the organism.**

---

## 11. WORKFLOW

1. Choose **Continent**.
2. Choose **Climate**.
3. Choose **Function**.
4. Generate plausible evolutionary possibilities.
5. Select the solutions that best fit the world.
6. Build the creature.
7. Check it against the world.
8. Add it to the canonical creature set.
9. Stop when the Creature Necessity Sheet says coverage is sufficient.
