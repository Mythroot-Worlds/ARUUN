# MYTHROOT WORLDS — CANONICAL FLORA LIBRARY
## Prehistoric-esque World, v0.1 (working draft)

**Status:** Working canon, built per `01_FLORA_FUNCTION_MATRIX.md` and `02_FLORA_CREATION_MATRIX.md`, cross-referenced against the existing `creature_library.md` (v0.2). No world-name assigned; none used here.

**Approach:** Every plant below exists because a named creature (or an implied background layer, e.g. small burrowing rodent-analogues) needs it — not for botanical completeness. Where a plant covers a function/climate combo not yet claimed by any creature entry, that's noted as filling a background gap rather than inventing new canon.

**Target:** ~30 canonical flora. This library contains **30 entries**, of which **13 are flagged signature** (★) — tied to signature-tier fauna or serving as a defining regional resource.

## HEARTH

---

**F01 — Broadgrass**
*Hearth / grassland — grazing resource*
- Growth form: tall, dense, fibrous grass-like stands; forms continuous cover across Hearth's open interior
- Key strategy: rapid regrowth from protected basal/root tissue after heavy grazing; high silica content discourages anything but a broad, fibrous-adapted digestive system
- Fauna link: the primary food source sustaining Broadback herds; herd grazing pressure keeps it from choking out over large ranges
- Line: Broadgrass doesn't fight back — it just grows faster than anything can eat it, which is the whole point.

---

**F02 — Marshreed**
*Hearth / wetland-floodplain — grazing + wetland engineering*
- Growth form: tall emergent reed stands rooted in shallow water, transitioning to floating mats in deeper flood pools
- Key strategy: rhizome spread lets it recolonize instantly after seasonal flood/drought cycles; slows water flow and traps sediment
- Fauna link: submerged base and emergent shoots are Marshtread's primary forage; dense stands are Marshwing's colonial nesting cover
- Line: A flood-season stand of Marshreed is as much architecture as vegetation — half of what lives in the shallows is only there because the reeds are.

---

**F03 — Riverweed**
*Hearth / river — aquatic grazing resource*
- Growth form: fully submerged, current-anchored producer forming dense river-bottom beds
- Key strategy: flexible stems bend with current rather than fighting it; anchors deep enough to survive seasonal flow surges
- Fauna link: Rivergrazer's primary food source; its grazing keeps channels navigable for smaller river fauna
- Line: Where the current runs slow, the riverbed disappears under Riverweed — until a Rivergrazer surfaces through it like the bottom just breathed.

---

**F04 — Duskroot**
*Hearth / dry forest-scrub — root/tuber resource*
- Growth form: low, spreading shrub with a shallow visible canopy hiding a disproportionately large underground tuber system
- Key strategy: stores water and carbohydrate underground through the dry season, safe from surface fire and grazing pressure
- Fauna link: the tubers are Rootsnout's primary dig-target; Rootsnout's excavation is the main reason Duskroot populations stay patchy rather than solid
- Line: Nothing about Duskroot looks worth digging for, until a Rootsnout family proves otherwise every dry season.

---

**F05 — Stonemat**
*Hearth / highland-mountain — browse resource*
- Growth form: low, dense, mat-forming scrub and lichen-analogue growth hugging exposed rock and thin soil
- Key strategy: extremely slow growth, wind- and cold-adapted low profile; recovers slowly from grazing, so it can only support a low-density browser
- Fauna link: Ridgehorn's primary forage on the ridgelines; Ridgehorn's browsing pressure keeps it from overrunning narrow mountain passes
- Line: Stonemat looks like the mountain's own skin until a Ridgehorn herd works a slope bare in a single season.

---

**F06 — Bloomthorn**
*Hearth / arid-desert — water-storage/desert specialist*
- Growth form: low succulent-like growth with a spined, water-storing body
- Key strategy: rain-triggered rapid growth and flowering, followed by long dormancy; spines deter surface browsers, protecting the water store
- Fauna link: not eaten directly by any canonical exemplar, but the seed flush after rare rain sustains the small burrowing rodent-analogues that make up Duststriker's underground prey base
- Line: Bloomthorn spends most of its life looking dead, and that's exactly when it's winning.

---

**F07 — Hollowfruit**
*Hearth / forest-woodland — fruit/seed resource + canopy*
- Growth form: mid-height woody tree with a broken, uneven canopy and heavy seasonal fruit drop
- Key strategy: fruit timed to seasonal fruiting pulses to swamp seed predators; canopy gaps create the broken sightlines Longjaw's ambush strategy depends on
- Fauna link: fallen fruit supplements Rootsnout's diet; canopy structure incidentally shapes Longjaw's preferred ambush terrain
- Line: A Hollowfruit grove drops its whole season's fruit in a matter of weeks — long enough for a Rootsnout family to get comfortable, which is exactly when the woodland gets dangerous.

---

**F08 — Deepshoal weed**
*Hearth / lake-inland sea — aquatic producer*
- Growth form: dense, low submerged meadow across shallow lake margins and inland-sea shoals
- Key strategy: forms the base of the inland sea's productivity; tolerates still, low-oxygen water better than a fast-current specialist could
- Fauna link: sustains the fish-analogue populations that make up Deepjaw's prey base, and the shallow cover it provides is where Deepjaw's hatchlings shelter before dispersing
- Line: The inland seas look empty from the shore — everything worth eating is hiding in the Deepshoal weed, and everything hunting it knows exactly where to look.

## SHATTERED

---

**F09 — Tideshrub**
*Shattered / coastal — salt-tolerant browse*
- Growth form: low, dense woody shrub rooted above the regular tideline with salt-excretion glands on its leaves
- Key strategy: salt exclusion at the root paired with active excretion keeps tissue viable in a zone most vegetation can't tolerate
- Fauna link: Saltgrazer's primary browse; Saltgrazer's slow grazing pressure keeps Tideshrub from encroaching on open tidal flats
- Line: Tideshrub grows where the ocean leaves salt behind twice a day, which is exactly why nothing but a Saltgrazer bothers competing for it.

---

**F10 — Crustbloom**
*Shattered / coastal-shallow marine — aquatic grazing resource*
- Growth form: hard, encrusting algae-analogue growth over reef-analogue structure
- Key strategy: mineral-hardened surface tissue resists wave action and casual grazing; regrows from protected basal layers
- Fauna link: Reefgrazer's specialized grinding beak-jaw exists specifically to exploit Crustbloom's tough surface; its removal opens channels other reef-associated species use
- Line: Crustbloom looks like part of the reef until a Reefgrazer school works a patch down to bare structure in an afternoon.

---

**F11 — Shoregrass**
*Shattered / coastal, seasonal — pre-migration fattening forage*
- Growth form: dense seasonal coastal grass, growth surging ahead of the crossing season
- Key strategy: rapid seasonal growth cued to the same conditions that trigger Farwader migration, so peak forage and peak departure-fattening line up
- Fauna link: the shoreline aggregation forage Farwader herds rely on to build fat reserves before committing to open water
- Line: When Shoregrass comes in thick along the eastern shoreline, it's less a season than a countdown.

---

**F12 — Thornbrush**
*Shattered / forest-woodland — browse + cover*
- Growth form: dense, low, thorned woodland shrub layer
- Key strategy: physical thorn defense against unrestrained browsing, balanced against a forest fragment's smaller herbivore population
- Fauna link: Thicketback's primary browse; the same dense cover Thicketback hides in is what Nightclaw uses for ambush
- Line: Thornbrush cover hides Thicketback from Nightclaw and hides Nightclaw from Thicketback — which is, unfortunately for one of them, the entire arrangement.

---

**F13 — Fragmentreed**
*Shattered / wetland-floodplain — wetland engineering + food*
- Growth form: dense rhizome-networked reed stand adapted to a fragment-scale floodplain rather than Hearth's continental one
- Key strategy: extensive rhizome mat stabilizes the burrow-riddled ground Shattered burrower depends on
- Fauna link: both a direct food source and the structural vegetation whose root mat keeps Shattered burrower's tunnel networks from collapsing
- Line: Pull up a stand of Fragmentreed and you'll usually find a burrow entrance had been using its roots as a ceiling.

---

**F14 — Ashbloom**
*Shattered / coastal disturbance — pioneer species*
- Growth form: fast, low, wind-dispersed colonizer
- Key strategy: high seed output and rapid germination on exposed, storm- or quake-disturbed coastal ground, common on a continent defined by fragmentation and instability
- Fauna link: no direct canonical consumer yet; establishes cover and stabilizes exposed ground ahead of Tideshrub and Thornbrush recolonization — a background gap-filler rather than an invented food source
- Line: Ashbloom is usually the first green on ground the last storm tore open, and usually gone again once something slower-growing crowds it out.

---

**F15 — Driftweed**
*Shattered / open ocean — floating aquatic producer*
- Growth form: buoyant, mat-forming floating vegetation drifting with open-ocean currents between fragments
- Key strategy: no fixed anchor point at all — reproduces and disperses entirely by drifting, well suited to a continent defined by water gaps between landmasses
- Fauna link: shelters and feeds small open-water fish-analogues, forming part of the prey base Straitrunner hunts through during the crossing season
- Line: A Driftweed mat crossing the strait carries its own small ecosystem with it — and Straitrunner has learned exactly what usually swims underneath one.

## RIFT

---

**F16 — Valleymix**
*Rift / grassland (rift floor) — mixed grazing/browse*
- Growth form: mixed-height stand combining grass-like groundcover with scattered woody browse plants along the valley floor
- Key strategy: two growth forms sharing one corridor lets a single long resource-rich strip support both grazing and browsing pressure without either outcompeting the other
- Fauna link: Basingrazer's mixed diet is built directly around Valleymix's dual structure — grass at the base, woody browse at reach height
- Line: A Basingrazer herd works a Valleymix corridor top to bottom, and somehow neither layer ever quite runs out.

---

**F17 — Faultscrub**
*Rift / highland-mountain (escarpment) — cliffside browse*
- Growth form: wiry, low, deep-anchored scrub rooted directly into narrow cliff ledges and fault-line cracks
- Key strategy: root system wedges into unstable rock rather than soil; lighter overall biomass than Hearth's Stonemat, favoring cliffs too steep for a bulkier plant to hold
- Fauna link: Highvault's primary forage, reachable only because Highvault's climbing ability matches the terrain Faultscrub actually grows on
- Line: Faultscrub grows where almost nothing can reach it, which is precisely why Highvault bothered evolving the ability to.

---

**F18 — Ashcolonizer**
*Rift / volcanic-disturbed — pioneer/disturbance specialist*
- Growth form: fast, low, mineral-tolerant colonizer that establishes directly on fresh ash-fall and cooling volcanic ground
- Key strategy: rapid germination and mineral-rich soil tolerance let it claim ground within days of a disturbance event, well ahead of slower-growing competitors
- Fauna link: the disturbance-exposed vegetation Steamhide forages on immediately after an eruption or ash-fall, before anything less heat-tolerant can move in
- Line: The first green after an eruption on Rift is almost always Ashcolonizer, and the first thing walking through it is almost always Steamhide.

---

**F19 — Rift reed-stand**
*Rift / wetland — cover/nesting + wetland engineering*
- Growth form: dense reed stand rooted in Rift's wetland systems, similar in role to Hearth's Marshreed but shorter and denser to suit smaller wetland basins
- Key strategy: dense growth above flood level provides stable nesting platforms; root mat slows water movement through the wetland
- Fauna link: Reedstalker builds its platform nests directly in dense stands; the same cover shelters the Rift river ambush predator's (004) elevated mud nests nearby
- Line: A Rift wetland's reed-stands do double duty — one predator nests above the waterline in them, another just below it.

---

**F20 — Basinweed**
*Rift / lake-inland sea — aquatic producer*
- Growth form: low submerged meadow adapted to Rift's individually isolated lake basins
- Key strategy: population-level variation from lake to lake, mirroring the same fault-barrier isolation that produces Riftlurker's per-lake specialization
- Fauna link: sustains the fish-analogue populations each regional Riftlurker variant specializes on
- Line: No two Rift lakes grow quite the same Basinweed, and no two Riftlurkers hunt quite the same way because of it.

---

**F21 — Floodroot**
*Rift / wetland-floodplain — root/tuber + soil stabilization*
- Growth form: low, spreading plant with a shallow visible mat and dense underground tuber storage
- Key strategy: underground storage survives seasonal flood/drought swings on the rift valley floor; root mat helps stabilize floodplain soil against erosion
- Fauna link: exposed tubers and disturbed ground left behind by larger grazers make up part of the scavenging forage Rift scavenger (002) works over
- Line: Floodroot rarely gets dug up on purpose — it's usually whatever a Rift scavenger finds after something bigger has already torn the ground open.

---

**F22 — Galleryhang**
*Rift / river (bank) — canopy/bank stabilization*
- Growth form: low, spreading tree with a wide root plate that grips unstable riverbank soil
- Key strategy: root structure resists bank erosion from Rift's seasonal flow surges; low canopy shades the bank rather than reaching for height
- Fauna link: overhanging bank cover doubles as concealment for the Rift river ambush predator's (004) elevated mud nests and juvenile ambush sites
- Line: Nothing about a Galleryhang stand along a Rift riverbank looks dangerous — right up until the shade under it moves.

## LOST

---

**F23 — Plateautuft**
*Lost / grassland-plateau — grazing resource*
- Growth form: tough, dense, independently evolved grass-analogue covering Lost's open plateau
- Key strategy: convergent solution to open-plateau grazing pressure, arrived at through an unrelated lineage from Hearth's Broadgrass
- Fauna link: Plateauhorn's primary forage across the plateau's herd ranges
- Line: Plateautuft looks like ordinary grazing country from a distance — until you remember nothing that grows here is related to anything on Hearth.

---

**F24 — Deeplichen**
*Lost / highland-mountain — ancient slow-growth browse*
- Growth form: extremely slow-growing lichen-analogue crust over ancient highland rock
- Key strategy: growth measured in decades rather than seasons; only viable where predation and browsing pressure have stayed low and steady across deep time
- Fauna link: Shellback's primary forage — the only browser slow enough, and long-lived enough, to rely on something that grows this slowly without wiping it out
- Line: A patch of Deeplichen a Shellback is working over has probably been growing since before that Shellback hatched.

---

**F25 — Mudmat**
*Lost / wetland — engineering substrate + food*
- Growth form: dense, low, detritus-accumulating wetland plant with a body plan unrelated to any Hearth/Shattered/Rift wetland vegetation
- Key strategy: rapid decomposition and dense root mat give Mudweaver colonies stable material to shape into mounds and channels
- Fauna link: both a food source and the literal building material for Mudweaver's engineered mud-channel networks
- Line: A Mudweaver colony doesn't just eat Mudmat — it builds its whole wetland out of it.

---

**F26 — Driftplankton mat**
*Lost / river — filter-feed source*
- Growth form: fine, suspended, plankton-analogue producer distributed through the water column rather than rooted
- Key strategy: reproduces fast enough in nutrient-rich stretches to sustain continuous filtering pressure from a very large consumer
- Fauna link: the suspended organic base of Rootfin's entire diet — Rootfin positions itself wherever this producer concentrates most densely
- Line: Rootfin never seems to be searching for food, because Driftplankton mat has already told it exactly where to be.

---

**F27 — Canopyveil**
*Lost / forest-woodland — canopy + insect-analogue support*
- Growth form: broad, low-canopied tree forming Lost's forest-woodland cover, structurally distinct from Hearth's Hollowfruit
- Key strategy: dense mid-height canopy creates the humid, insect-rich understory layer Lost's forest fauna depends on
- Fauna link: sustains the insect-analogue population Duskveil hunts through the canopy at night; canopy cover also shapes the forest-margin terrain Packrender uses when pursuing Plateauhorn near the plateau edge
- Line: By day a Canopyveil stand looks quiet. By night it's the reason Duskveil never has to glide far to find dinner.

---

**F28 — Basinsucculent**
*Lost / arid basin — water-storage/desert specialist*
- Growth form: low, water-storing succulent-like growth adapted to Lost's major interior basins
- Key strategy: independent convergent solution to arid water scarcity, structurally distinct from Hearth's spined Bloomthorn
- Fauna link: rain-triggered seed flush sustains Lost's small burrowing rodent-analogue base layer, the same background role Bloomthorn fills on Hearth
- Line: Basinsucculent solved the same problem Bloomthorn did on the other side of the ocean, and solved it without ever knowing Bloomthorn existed.

---

**F29 — Marginbloom**
*Lost / coastal-ocean margin — coastal stabilization + producer*
- Growth form: low, salt-tolerant coastal plant stabilizing Lost's long ocean margins
- Key strategy: root mat traps sediment along the shoreline, and seasonal die-back exports organic material into deeper water
- Fauna link: sustains shorebird-analogue prey populations along the coast, and its exported detritus is part of the sinking-carrion supply Abysshunter scavenges in deep water offshore
- Line: Nothing about Marginbloom looks like it reaches the deep ocean — but a surprising amount of what feeds Abysshunter started as a leaf here.

---

**F30 — Rootveil**
*All four continents / cross-climate groundcover — soil stabilization + base forage*
- Growth form: low, dense, fast-spreading groundcover; the specific species differs by continent, but the ecological role is consistent
- Key strategy: shallow fibrous roots stabilize disturbed and open soil almost anywhere climate allows it; tolerates heavy, repeated cropping
- Fauna link: the default forage layer for the small burrowing rodent-analogues implied as a base prey species on every continent — the resource that makes that background layer plausible in the first place
- Line: Rootveil is the plant nobody in the world notices and almost everything in the world is quietly eating.

## OPEN GAPS / NOT ADDRESSED IN THIS PASS

Flagged rather than silently left out:

- No dedicated flora entry exists for Hearth's or Shattered's **volcanic/disturbed** terrain, since neither continent is established as having active volcanism (Rift's F18 covers the only confirmed volcanic case).
- **Deep water** flora is unaddressed on all continents — Abysshunter (Lost) and any future deep-water fauna are assumed to run on sinking organic material (partly covered by F29) rather than local deep-water producers.
- Lost's **arid basin** (F28) and Hearth's **desert** (F06) are the only two true arid entries; Shattered and Rift have no dedicated true-desert flora, consistent with neither continent's dossier calling out true desert as a defining feature.
- No flora entry directly claims the **open-ocean** niche on Lost or Rift the way F15 (Driftweed) does for Shattered — a reasonable gap if those continents' open-ocean food webs are meant to run through Hearth/Shattered-style producers rather than a Lost-specific one, but flagged rather than assumed.
