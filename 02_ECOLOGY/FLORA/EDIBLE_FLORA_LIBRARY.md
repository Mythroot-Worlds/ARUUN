# ARUUN — CANONICAL EDIBLE FLORA LIBRARY
## Aruunite Food Ecology, v0.1 (working draft)

**Status:** Working canon, built per `ARUUNITE_EDIBLE_FLORA_EXPANSION_HANDOFF.md`, cross-referenced against `MYTHROOT_FLORA_LIBRARY_v0.1.md` and `creature_library.md`. World is Aruun; the peoples are Aruunites.

**Approach:** Every plant below exists because an Aruunite population would plausibly notice, harvest, process, or remember it — not for botanical completeness. Where a food role overlaps an existing megafauna-forage plant (e.g. Duskroot, Hollowfruit), that overlap is called out rather than duplicated into a new species.

**Target:** enough staples, seasonal foods, fallbacks, and processed/fermented foods that every region has a credible answer to "what do these people eat." This library contains **30 canonical edible plants**.

**Domestication scale:** 0 = gathered only · 1 = managed wild resource · 2 = proto-domestication candidate · 3 = strong domestication candidate · 4 = likely future staple.

## 1. STARCHY GRAINS / GRASS SEEDS

---

**EF01 — Broadseed**
- **Identity:** Large-seeded wild grass · Hearth · grassland / floodplain edge
- **Food:** Seed head, ground or pounded into flour; contributes the bulk of a gathering group's calories in season; nutty, slightly sweet raw, mellows on cooking; stores well dry
- **Ecology:** Tall grass-like producer, shares open grassland range with Broadback; seasonal seed-set timed to the same growing pulse Broadback herds track; fibrous, drought-tolerant root
- **Human use:** Managed wild resource trending toward true domestication (3) — stands near seasonal camps are already selectively favored; requires threshing and grinding before use; flour keeps for a full season if kept dry; flatbread and porridge are the two main preparations
- **Safety:** Fully edible once threshed; no toxicity; no serious look-alikes
- **Cultural potential:** Staple grain for both mobile and settling groups; the plant most likely to anchor eventual agriculture on Hearth
- **Rationale:** Hearth's enormous open grassland is the same resource base that supports Broadback — an obvious, abundant, storable calorie source for any group willing to do the processing work.

---

**EF02 — Coldhusk**
- **Identity:** Upland cold-tolerant grain · Hearth · highland-mountain
- **Food:** Small, tough-husked seed; requires hulling before grinding; dense, low-sugar, calorie-heavy; keeps almost indefinitely once hulled and dried
- **Ecology:** Low, wind-hardy grass growing above Stonemat's browse line; short growing season timed to Ridgehorn's summer range shift
- **Human use:** Proto-domestication candidate (2) — viable but harvest windows are short and access is difficult; hulling is labor-intensive, favoring groups that overwinter near the highlands rather than pure nomads
- **Safety:** Edible once hulled; raw husk is unpalatable but not toxic
- **Cultural potential:** Emergency/seasonal staple for highland-dwelling or highland-adjacent groups; valuable trade good given its storage life
- **Rationale:** A short highland growing season with a hardy, storable seed is exactly the kind of resource that rewards a group willing to stay put through the cold months instead of following easier lowland forage.

---

**EF03 — Marshgrain**
- **Identity:** Wetland grain · Rift · wetland / lakeshore
- **Food:** Seed harvested from standing water at the edge of Rift reed-stands; cooks into a dense porridge; poor raw palatability
- **Ecology:** Emergent grass rooted in shallow water alongside Rift reed-stand; seed-set follows the same wet-season cycle that floods the wetland margin
- **Human use:** Proto-domestication candidate (2) — harvest requires wading and hand-stripping seed heads, awkward but not skill-intensive; grain stores moderately well if dried fully before storage, otherwise molds quickly in Rift's humidity
- **Safety:** Edible cooked; raw seed is gritty and largely indigestible rather than dangerous
- **Cultural potential:** Wet-season staple for groups working Rift's lake basins; drying racks near the wetland edge are a likely early piece of material culture
- **Rationale:** Rift's wetland systems are extensive and predictable enough to support a grain harvest without competing directly with Reedstalker or the wetland's larger fauna, which don't touch the seed heads.

---

**EF04 — Duneseed**
- **Identity:** Drought-tolerant grain · Rift · dry basin
- **Food:** Small, hard seed produced in a brief post-rain flush; ground into a coarse meal; bitter unless roasted first
- **Ecology:** Low, deep-rooted grass triggered into rapid seed production by the same rare rain events that green Rift's dry basin floor
- **Human use:** Managed wild resource (1) — harvest windows are unpredictable and brief, so this functions as an opportunistic fallback rather than a planned staple; roasting before grinding is required to make the meal palatable
- **Safety:** Edible once roasted; unroasted seed is bitter and mildly irritating rather than seriously toxic
- **Cultural potential:** Fallback/emergency food for groups moving through or near Rift's dry basin; its unpredictability keeps it from becoming a dependable staple
- **Rationale:** A grain that only fruits after rare rain is a real fallback resource in Rift's dry basin country, where predictable food is otherwise scarce between wet cycles.

---

**EF05 — Saltseed grass**
- **Identity:** Coastal salt-tolerant grain · Shattered · coastal
- **Food:** Small seed head growing just above the tideline, alongside Tideshrub; mild, slightly salty flavor; usable ground or whole in a boiled porridge
- **Ecology:** Salt-excreting grass rooted above the regular tide zone; tolerant of the same salt exposure Tideshrub is built for
- **Human use:** Proto-domestication candidate (2) — grows in dense enough stands along stable stretches of coastline to reward repeated harvest by groups that return to the same shoreline seasonally
- **Safety:** Fully edible; no toxicity
- **Cultural potential:** Coastal staple, particularly valuable to groups whose mobility is built around Shattered's fragment-hopping lifestyle, since it requires no processing beyond grinding
- **Rationale:** Shattered's coastal dependence extends naturally to its human populations — a salt-tolerant grain growing in the same narrow strip Tideshrub and Saltgrazer already exploit is a low-effort, low-competition food source.

---

**EF06 — Plateauseed**
- **Identity:** Independent-lineage plateau grain · Lost · grassland-plateau
- **Food:** Large seed head, structurally unlike any Hearth/Rift/Shattered grain — husk splits open rather than shattering, an evolutionary quirk of an unrelated lineage; ground into a dense, oily-tasting flour
- **Ecology:** Tall grass-analogue growing alongside Plateautuft on Lost's open plateau; shares Plateautuft's grazing-tolerant regrowth strategy
- **Human use:** Strong domestication candidate (3) — the self-opening husk actually simplifies threshing compared to Hearth's grains, an accidental head start toward domestication
- **Safety:** Fully edible; no toxicity; husk fragments are the only minor hazard if not winnowed
- **Cultural potential:** Likely the anchor staple grain for any future Lost agriculture, given how little processing it demands
- **Rationale:** Lost's deep-time independent evolution produced a grain that solves the same seed-dispersal problem Hearth's grasses solve, but through a mechanically different husk — genuinely useful to Aruunites for reasons that have nothing to do with looking familiar.

## 2. SEEDS AND NUTS

---

**EF07 — Ironshell nut**
- **Identity:** Hard-shelled tree nut · Hearth · forest-woodland
- **Food:** Dense, oily kernel inside an extremely hard shell; high in fat and calories; requires cracking with a heavy stone or tool
- **Ecology:** Produced by a slow-growing woodland tree occupying the same canopy layer as Hollowfruit; masts irregularly, producing a heavy crop only every few years
- **Human use:** Managed wild resource (1) — the shell's hardness limits exploitation to groups with the right tools and patience; mast years likely drive short-term aggregation of otherwise mobile groups
- **Safety:** Edible once shelled; no toxicity
- **Cultural potential:** High-value fat/calorie source, especially valuable heading into lean seasons; mast years plausibly become remembered, anticipated events
- **Rationale:** A masting nut this calorie-dense is worth real tool investment, and the unpredictable mast cycle is exactly the kind of seasonal gap that would shape group movement and memory.

---

**EF08 — Oilpod**
- **Identity:** Oily seed pod · Rift · highland / lakeshore
- **Food:** Cluster of small oily seeds inside a papery pod; primary regional oil source once crushed and pressed; seeds themselves are also edible roasted
- **Ecology:** Low shrub growing at the boundary between Faultscrub's escarpment range and the lakeshore basin below it
- **Human use:** Proto-domestication candidate (2) — extracting oil requires crushing and a water- or heat-assisted separation step; roasted whole seeds are a simpler, lower-yield alternative requiring no processing beyond fire
- **Safety:** Edible roasted; raw seed has a mildly bitter, faintly irritating coating that cooking neutralizes
- **Cultural potential:** Regional oil staple — likely traded toward groups without direct access to the highland-lakeshore boundary zone
- **Rationale:** Rift's sharp elevation gradients put a highland shrub and a lakeshore camp within easy reach of each other, making a labor-intensive oil source worth the trip.

---

**EF09 — Saltnut**
- **Identity:** Coastal tree nut · Shattered · coastal
- **Food:** Medium nut with a thin, easily cracked shell and a mild, faintly salty kernel; good raw or roasted
- **Ecology:** Small salt-tolerant tree rooted just inland of Tideshrub, benefiting from the same coastal exposure
- **Human use:** Managed wild resource (1) — easy to harvest and process, but individual trees are scattered rather than forming dense stands
- **Safety:** Fully edible; no toxicity
- **Cultural potential:** A reliable, low-effort snack/travel food for Shattered's mobile fragment-hopping populations
- **Rationale:** Low processing cost matters most for a population that moves often between fragments — Saltnut asks almost nothing of a group in exchange for real calories.

---

**EF10 — Deepnut**
- **Identity:** Underground-fruiting ground nut · Lost · forest-woodland
- **Food:** Nut-analogue structure that develops entirely underground at the base of Canopyveil trees rather than on a branch — an independent evolutionary solution to seed protection; rich, dense kernel once the tough outer casing is removed
- **Ecology:** Symbiotic root-associated structure beneath Canopyveil canopy; development timed to the same humid understory conditions that support Canopyveil's insect-analogue population
- **Human use:** Proto-domestication candidate (2) — requires digging to locate and a firm strike to open the casing; the underground habit means it's invisible without knowledge of where to look, making this a learned, taught skill rather than an obvious resource
- **Safety:** Edible once opened; no toxicity
- **Cultural potential:** A distinctly Lost food-knowledge marker — the kind of resource that separates groups who know the forest from groups who don't
- **Rationale:** Lost's independent evolutionary history produced a genuinely different structural answer to the same "protect the seed" problem Hearth's Ironshell solves in the canopy — different enough that finding it at all requires real, transmitted forest knowledge.

## 3. ROOTS, TUBERS, BULBS, CORMS

---

**EF11 — Sweetroot**
- **Identity:** Dependable staple tuber · Hearth · woodland / floodplain
- **Food:** Large, starchy, mildly sweet tuber; edible raw in a pinch, far better roasted; stores for months in a cool pit
- **Ecology:** Low spreading plant growing along the same woodland-floodplain transition zone Marshreed and Hollowfruit occupy; storage organ built to survive both dry season and flood
- **Human use:** Strong domestication candidate (3) — easy to locate, dig, and store; a strong early candidate for deliberate replanting of harvested crowns
- **Safety:** Edible raw or cooked; no toxicity
- **Cultural potential:** A true staple — dependable enough to anchor a settling group's diet across multiple seasons
- **Rationale:** Hearth's stable, broad floodplain-woodland zone is exactly the kind of dependable habitat that lets a storage-organ plant become a true year-round staple rather than a fallback.

---

**EF12 — Bitterbulb**
- **Identity:** Wetland-margin bulb · Rift · wetland
- **Food:** Starchy bulb, toxic in its raw state; becomes safe and calorie-dense after extended soaking and leaching in running water
- **Ecology:** Grows at the drier edge of Rift's wetland, just beyond Rift reed-stand's usual waterline
- **Human use:** Managed wild resource (1) — the leaching process (multiple days of soaking, changing the water) is real processing overhead, so this functions best as a dependable fallback rather than a first-choice food
- **Safety:** **Requires processing** — raw bulb contains a bitter, gut-irritating compound that leaching removes; a real hazard for anyone who skips or shortens the process
- **Cultural potential:** Fallback/emergency staple; the leaching technique itself is a transmitted piece of survival knowledge, likely tied to specific known soaking sites
- **Rationale:** A toxic-until-processed tuber is a believable and valuable fallback specifically because the processing barrier keeps it from being over-harvested by anything that can't learn the technique.

---

**EF13 — Driftcorm**
- **Identity:** Storable coastal corm · Shattered · coastal
- **Food:** Firm, starchy corm growing in sandy coastal soil; mild flavor, good boiled or roasted; stores well dry
- **Ecology:** Low coastal plant rooted just above Tideshrub's zone, in the same stabilized dune soil Ashbloom colonizes after storms
- **Human use:** Proto-domestication candidate (2) — corms can be dug, dried, and carried between fragments, making this a strong food for Shattered's mobile lifestyle
- **Safety:** Fully edible once dried or cooked; minimal toxicity concerns
- **Cultural potential:** A portable staple specifically suited to a population that regularly crosses or moves between fragments
- **Rationale:** Shattered's fragmented geography rewards a storable, portable calorie source more than a fresh-eaten one — Driftcorm travels well, which matters more here than almost anywhere else.

---

**EF14 — Deepcorm**
- **Identity:** Unusual underground storage organ · Lost · forest-woodland / highland margin
- **Food:** A segmented, multi-chambered underground storage structure unlike a simple tuber or bulb — each chamber matures on a slightly different cycle, so a single plant can be partially harvested repeatedly rather than dug up all at once
- **Ecology:** Found at the boundary between Canopyveil forest and Deeplichen highland margin; independent structural solution to underground storage
- **Human use:** Proto-domestication candidate (2) — partial, repeated harvesting from the same plant is a genuinely different management strategy than Hearth's dig-and-replace approach, and would reward groups that return to known plants rather than harvesting anonymously
- **Safety:** Edible once cooked; raw chambers are mildly unpalatable rather than dangerous
- **Cultural potential:** Likely tied to specific remembered plant locations rather than general habitat knowledge — a "our family's root patch" resource rather than an anonymous staple
- **Rationale:** Lost's long independent history produced a storage strategy that rewards a completely different human harvesting behavior — return-and-partial-harvest rather than dig-and-move-on — which is a distinctly Lost cultural seed.

## 4. FRUITS

---

**EF15 — Honeydrop fruit**
- **Identity:** High-sugar fleshy fruit · Hearth · woodland / floodplain
- **Food:** Small, thin-skinned, very sweet fruit; excellent fresh, dries into a chewy preserve; overripe fruit ferments naturally on the ground
- **Ecology:** Produced by a shrub growing in Hollowfruit's understory shade; seed dispersal depends on megafauna passage — seeds only germinate reliably after passing through a large herbivore's gut
- **Human use:** Proto-domestication candidate (2) — easy to gather in season; drying is simple and effective; naturally fermented windfall fruit is a plausible early, accidental fermentation substrate
- **Safety:** Fully edible fresh or dried; overripe/fermented fruit is safe but mildly intoxicating
- **Cultural potential:** Seasonal treat/luxury food, and a likely candidate for the earliest deliberate fruit-based fermentation
- **Rationale:** A fruit that depends on megafauna gut passage to germinate ties Aruunites directly into the same seed-dispersal web as Hearth's grazers — humans following herbivore trails would find the richest Honeydrop stands exactly where the animals have already been.

---

**EF16 — Firefruit**
- **Identity:** Small, abundant, dry fruit · Rift · lakeshore / highland margin
- **Food:** Small, low-moisture fruit with a tart, intense flavor; naturally dry enough to store without further processing
- **Ecology:** Shrub growing in disturbed ground near Rift's volcanic-adjacent terrain, alongside Ashcolonizer's recovery zones
- **Human use:** Managed wild resource (1) — no processing required beyond picking; abundant in disturbed-ground flushes following volcanic activity
- **Safety:** Fully edible; no toxicity
- **Cultural potential:** A dependable, low-effort seasonal food, and possibly one of the few good things Aruunites associate with Rift's volcanic instability
- **Rationale:** A fruit that thrives on the same disturbed ground Ashcolonizer colonizes turns a hazard zone into a recurring, low-effort food source — worth the risk of returning to recently active ground.

---

**EF17 — Tideberry**
- **Identity:** Water-rich coastal fruit · Shattered · coastal
- **Food:** Juicy, mildly sweet fruit with high water content; primarily valuable as a hydration supplement in dry coastal stretches, not a calorie staple
- **Ecology:** Low vine growing among Tideshrub and Driftcorm in the coastal transition zone
- **Human use:** Gathered only (0) — abundant enough that management isn't necessary; picked and eaten fresh, doesn't store
- **Safety:** Fully edible; no toxicity
- **Cultural potential:** Minor but genuinely useful — a supplement to, never a replacement for, dependable water sources on dry stretches of coastline
- **Rationale:** Shattered's coastline includes dry stretches where a water-rich fruit growing right at hand meaningfully extends how far a group can travel between reliable water.

---

**EF18 — Veilfruit**
- **Identity:** Independent-lineage forest fruit · Lost · forest-woodland
- **Food:** Fruit with a tough, papery outer casing that must be peeled away in strips (structurally unlike Hearth or Shattered fruit skins) to reach a soft, custardy interior; rich, high-fat flesh rather than high-sugar
- **Ecology:** Grows on Canopyveil in a shaded mid-canopy layer; fat-rich flesh is unusual among fruiting strategies elsewhere on Aruun, but plausible as an independent solution to seed provisioning in a low-light understory where sugar production is harder to sustain
- **Human use:** Proto-domestication candidate (2) — peeling technique must be learned; interior doesn't store well and is best eaten fresh
- **Safety:** Fully edible once peeled; outer casing is unpalatable but not toxic
- **Cultural potential:** A genuinely distinctive Lost delicacy — fat-rich fruit is unusual enough to carry cultural weight beyond simple nutrition
- **Rationale:** A fat-rich rather than sugar-rich fruit is a real, functional consequence of Lost's shaded understory conditions, and gives Lost a fruit that couldn't be mistaken for any Hearth/Rift/Shattered equivalent.

---

**EF19 — Marshfig**
- **Identity:** Seasonal wetland fruit · Hearth · wetland-floodplain
- **Food:** Soft, seed-heavy fruit growing directly from Marshreed's upper stem nodes; mild flavor, best fresh
- **Ecology:** Epiphytic-adjacent growth on mature Marshreed stands, fruiting in the weeks after peak flood recedes
- **Human use:** Gathered only (0) — abundant during its short window, doesn't store
- **Safety:** Fully edible; no toxicity
- **Cultural potential:** A reliable short-season treat tightly linked to the flood cycle, likely marking a specific remembered point in the yearly round for floodplain-dwelling groups
- **Rationale:** Growing directly on Marshreed ties this fruit's harvest window to the same flood-recession signal that already governs Marshtread's movement — a resource easy to predict once the flood pattern is known.

## 5. LEGUME / PULSE ANALOGUES

---

**EF20 — Podvine**
- **Identity:** Climbing legume analogue · Hearth · woodland margin
- **Food:** Protein-rich seed inside a tough pod; requires extended boiling to break down a mild toxin and make the seed digestible
- **Ecology:** Vine climbing through Hollowfruit's broken canopy, using the tree's structure for light access
- **Human use:** Proto-domestication candidate (2) — the boiling requirement is real but manageable overhead for a settling or semi-settled group with reliable fire and water access
- **Safety:** **Requires cooking** — raw or undercooked seed causes gut upset; a real but well-understood hazard once the preparation is known
- **Cultural potential:** A key protein supplement, especially valuable to groups without regular access to hunted meat
- **Rationale:** A protein-dense seed that needs boiling to be safe is a believable reason for groups to settle near reliable water and firewood rather than treating this as a food to eat on the move.

---

**EF21 — Basin lentil**
- **Identity:** Small dry-season fallback pulse · Rift · dry basin margin
- **Food:** Small, hard seed; boils down into a dense, protein-rich mash; keeps well dry
- **Ecology:** Low shrub growing at the edge of Rift's dry basin, tolerant of the same rare-rain growth pulse Duneseed depends on
- **Human use:** Proto-domestication candidate (2) — simple to harvest and store once dried; boiling is the only preparation needed
- **Safety:** Fully edible cooked; raw seed is merely hard, not toxic
- **Cultural potential:** A dependable dry-season protein source pairing naturally with Duneseed's carbohydrate as a fallback meal
- **Rationale:** Pairing with Duneseed in the same dry-basin fallback window gives Rift groups a believable complete meal precisely when other food is scarcest.

---

**EF22 — Saltpod**
- **Identity:** Salt-tolerant coastal legume · Shattered · coastal
- **Food:** Small, mild pod eaten young and whole, or dried and shelled if mature; salty flavor from the same tolerance mechanism as Saltseed grass
- **Ecology:** Low shrub rooted alongside Saltseed grass above the tideline
- **Human use:** Managed wild resource (1) — simple to harvest, minimal processing
- **Safety:** Fully edible; no toxicity
- **Cultural potential:** A minor but easy protein supplement for coastal-dwelling groups
- **Rationale:** Shares Saltseed grass's niche and salt-tolerance mechanism, giving Shattered's coastal foragers a second easy food from the same narrow strip of ground.

## 6. LEAFY GREENS

---

**EF23 — Springshoot green**
- **Identity:** Seasonal floodplain green · Hearth · wetland-floodplain
- **Food:** Tender young shoots, gathered briefly in early spring before Broadgrass and Marshreed reach full height; mild, slightly bitter flavor; primarily a micronutrient and flavor source rather than a calorie source
- **Ecology:** Fast-growing early shoots of the same general floodplain vegetation community as Broadgrass and Marshreed, just harvested at an earlier growth stage
- **Human use:** Gathered only (0) — brief seasonal window, no storage value
- **Safety:** Fully edible; no toxicity
- **Cultural potential:** A seasonal marker food — the "first greens" of the year for floodplain-dwelling groups, likely carrying some ritual weight as a signal that the growing season has begun
- **Rationale:** A short, predictable early-season window tied to the same growth cycle that governs the region's grain staples gives Aruunites a believable seasonal-calendar marker plant.

---

**EF24 — Frondgreen**
- **Identity:** Fern-analogue forest green · Lost · forest-woodland
- **Food:** Young, tightly coiled fronds gathered before they unfurl; mild, slightly nutty flavor, best lightly cooked
- **Ecology:** Grows in the humid understory beneath Canopyveil, alongside Deepnut's root zone
- **Human use:** Gathered only (0) — brief harvest window before fronds unfurl and toughen
- **Safety:** Edible cooked; unfurled mature fronds are tough and unpalatable rather than toxic
- **Cultural potential:** A minor seasonal green valued mainly for dietary variety in an otherwise starch-and-fat-heavy Lost forest diet
- **Rationale:** Lost's shaded, humid forest floor supports a genuinely different green than Hearth's open-floodplain shoots, giving forest-dwelling Aruunites their own seasonal variety food.

## 7. EDIBLE FLOWERS / SHOOTS / STEMS

---

**EF25 — Reedshoot**
- **Identity:** Wetland shoot/stem · Rift · wetland
- **Food:** Tender base of young Rift reed-stand shoots, eaten raw or lightly roasted; mild, slightly sweet
- **Ecology:** Harvested from the same reed-stands Reedstalker nests in and the Rift river ambush predator (004) uses for cover, taken only from young, non-flowering growth
- **Human use:** Gathered only (0) — minor secondary food, harvested opportunistically while working the wetland edge for other resources
- **Safety:** Fully edible; no toxicity
- **Cultural potential:** A minor flavoring/snack food; harvesting it means working close to Reedstalker and river-predator territory, which may carry some risk-awareness or ritual caution in practice
- **Rationale:** A minor, easy food available anywhere Rift reed-stand already grows costs nothing extra to notice while gathering Marshgrain or hunting the same wetland margin.

---

**EF26 — Saltbloom**
- **Identity:** Coastal edible flower · Shattered · coastal
- **Food:** Small, salty-sweet flower, eaten fresh as a flavoring or minor snack; not a calorie source
- **Ecology:** Flowering structure of Tideshrub itself, available only during its brief flowering window
- **Human use:** Gathered only (0) — purely opportunistic, tied to Tideshrub's flowering season
- **Safety:** Fully edible; no toxicity
- **Cultural potential:** Minor ritual/flavor food, plausibly tied to seasonal gatherings timed around Tideshrub's bloom
- **Rationale:** Costs nothing to notice on a plant Shattered groups are already harvesting for Tideshrub's browse-adjacent uses, and gives coastal populations a small seasonal marker of their own.

## 8. MUSHROOMS / FUNGAL FOODS

---

**EF27 — Woodcap**
- **Identity:** Traditional seasonal mushroom · Hearth · forest-woodland
- **Food:** Firm, mild-flavored mushroom fruiting reliably after the wet season in Hollowfruit's leaf litter; good fresh or dried for storage
- **Ecology:** Decomposer fruiting body drawing on Hollowfruit's seasonal leaf-fall
- **Human use:** Managed wild resource (1) — reliable, recognizable, and predictable enough that groups likely return to known productive patches year after year
- **Safety:** Fully edible; the main hazard is confusing it with unrelated toxic fungi in the same leaf litter, making correct identification a genuinely important taught skill
- **Cultural potential:** A recognizable, dependable, seasonally anticipated traditional food — the kind of plant with a real name and real identification lore passed between generations
- **Rationale:** A dependable, well-known fungus with a real (if learnable) identification risk is exactly the kind of food that generates transmitted knowledge and tradition rather than pure opportunism.

---

**EF28 — Deepcap**
- **Identity:** Lost forest/highland-margin fungus, with a dangerous close relative · Lost · forest-woodland / highland margin
- **Food:** Edible variety has a pale, faintly ridged cap and a mild, earthy flavor, fruiting in the same humid zone as Frondgreen; the dangerous relative is nearly identical in casual appearance but has a smoother cap surface and grows slightly higher on decaying wood rather than at ground level
- **Ecology:** Decomposer fruiting body in Canopyveil's leaf litter and fallen deadwood
- **Human use:** Gathered only (0) — the identification risk keeps this from becoming a casually harvested staple even where it's abundant
- **Safety:** **Serious look-alike hazard** — the toxic relative causes severe illness and is common enough in the same habitat that confident identification requires real, specific taught knowledge; not a plant a group would let a child gather alone
- **Cultural potential:** High-stakes knowledge food — likely tied to specific trusted foragers or elders whose identification skill is trusted, rather than general-purpose harvesting
- **Rationale:** A genuinely dangerous look-alike pair gives Lost's forest-dwelling Aruunites a believable reason for specialized, trust-based foraging knowledge rather than universal casual gathering.

## 9. HONEY-LIKE SWEETENER SYSTEM

---

**EF29 — Goldbloom**
- **Identity:** Nectar-producing tree · Hearth · woodland margin
- **Food (indirect):** Does not directly feed humans — its dense, seasonal nectar flow supports a nectar-collecting insect-analogue (**flagged for coordination with fauna creation**) that concentrates and stores the nectar as a thick, preservable sugar resource Aruunites can raid from wild nest sites
- **Ecology:** Mid-height flowering tree with an unusually long, staggered bloom period compared to Hollowfruit, timed to keep the nectar-insect colonies fed across most of the growing season
- **Human use:** Managed wild resource (1) — wild nest raiding only at this stage; concentrated stored sugar has real preservation and fermentation value once obtained, but nests are neither abundant nor easy to safely access
- **Safety:** The nectar-insect defends its stored sugar; raiding a nest carries real physical risk, not a plant toxicity risk
- **Cultural potential:** High cultural/ritual value precisely because it is not universally abundant — likely a prestige or ceremonial resource, and a plausible fermentation substrate for an early alcoholic beverage
- **Rationale:** A concentrated, storable sugar source this valuable and this hard to get plausibly becomes culturally significant out of proportion to its actual caloric contribution — exactly the "not universally abundant" honey-analogue the handoff calls for. **Open item:** the nectar-collecting insect itself still needs a matching fauna entry; this plant is built to support one but does not invent it.

**EF30 — Basin fallback tuber**
- **Identity:** Arid-basin emergency staple · Lost · arid basin
- **Food:** Small, tough, low-moisture tuber; bitter and barely palatable raw, improves substantially with slow roasting; primarily valued for water content and modest calories in a genuinely resource-poor environment
- **Ecology:** Grows alongside Basinsucculent in Lost's interior basin, tapping deep groundwater most surface vegetation can't reach
- **Human use:** Managed wild resource (1) — a true emergency food rather than a preferred one; groups likely know its locations precisely because they'd only turn to it when better options have failed
- **Safety:** Edible once roasted; raw tuber is unpleasantly bitter but not seriously toxic
- **Cultural potential:** Emergency/survival food only — unlikely to carry positive cultural weight, more likely remembered as "the hard season food"
- **Rationale:** Lost's arid interior basins need at least one true last-resort food to make survival there credible, distinct from Hearth's more palatable Duskroot fallback.
