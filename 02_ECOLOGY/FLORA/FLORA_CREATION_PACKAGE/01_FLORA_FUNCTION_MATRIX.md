# MYTHROOT WORLDS — FLORA FUNCTION MATRIX v0.1

## Purpose

This matrix identifies what plants need to **do** in the ecosystem before we decide what they look like.

Core rule:

> **CONTINENT → CLIMATE → FUNCTION → EVOLUTIONARY POSSIBILITIES → PLANT**

The matrix is a design tool, not a complete botanical catalogue.

---

# 1. PRIMARY PRODUCERS

The basic biomass foundation.

Possible forms:
- low groundcover
- grasses/grass-like plants
- herbaceous mats
- shrubs
- woody plants
- trees
- aquatic producers
- floating plants

Questions:
- How much biomass does the environment support?
- Is growth continuous or seasonal?
- Is the plant adapted to disturbance?
- Does it form dense stands or scattered individuals?

---

# 2. GRAZING RESOURCES

Plants primarily supporting large herbivores.

Possible strategies:
- fast-growing soft vegetation
- tough fibrous vegetation
- nutrient-rich seasonal growth
- dense low mats
- tall grass-like stands
- aquatic vegetation
- salt-tolerant coastal forage

Possible defenses:
- silica/fiber
- spines
- toxins
- rapid regrowth
- growth from protected roots/rhizomes

---

# 3. BROWSE RESOURCES

Woody or semi-woody vegetation supporting browsers.

Possible forms:
- shrubs
- low trees
- thorny growth
- broad-leaved woody plants
- high-canopy foliage
- seasonal leaf producers

---

# 4. FRUIT / SEED RESOURCES

Concentrated food resources and dispersal relationships.

Possible strategies:
- fleshy fruit
- hard seeds
- nutrient-rich seeds
- seasonal fruiting
- continuous low-level fruiting
- mast-like synchronized production
- animal-dispersed seeds
- wind-dispersed seeds
- water-dispersed seeds

---

# 5. ROOT / TUBER RESOURCES

Underground food stores.

Useful in:
- seasonal climates
- drought-prone regions
- fire-prone regions
- environments with predictable above-ground dieback

Possible strategies:
- deep roots
- bulbs
- tubers
- rhizomes
- water storage
- carbohydrate storage

---

# 6. GROUND COVER / SOIL STABILIZATION

Plants that hold soil and shape the surface environment.

Functions:
- erosion control
- moisture retention
- soil stabilization
- nutrient capture
- microbial habitat
- protection from intense rainfall
- recovery after disturbance

---

# 7. WETLAND / AQUATIC ENGINEERING

Plants that physically alter water and saturated soils.

Possible functions:
- slow water
- trap sediment
- stabilize banks
- create floating mats
- provide submerged habitat
- create shallow-water refuges
- oxygenate local water
- accumulate organic matter

---

# 8. NUTRIENT CYCLING / SOIL BUILDING

Plants that help make difficult soils biologically productive.

Possible strategies:
- nutrient fixation
- deep mineral access
- symbiosis with fungi/microbes
- rapid decomposition
- nutrient-rich leaf litter
- disturbance colonization

---

# 9. PIONEER / DISTURBANCE SPECIES

First colonizers after:
- volcanic activity
- landslides
- floods
- fire
- exposed soil
- major animal disturbance

Possible traits:
- rapid reproduction
- wind-dispersed seeds
- hardy roots
- fast growth
- tolerance of poor soils
- high seed output

---

# 10. FIRE / DISTURBANCE ADAPTATION

Possible strategies:
- protected underground growth
- thick bark
- rapid post-fire regrowth
- fire-triggered reproduction
- seed banks
- resprouting
- fire-resistant tissues

---

# 11. WATER CONSERVATION

Especially important in arid and semi-arid regions.

Possible strategies:
- deep roots
- water storage
- reduced leaf area
- waxy surfaces
- seasonal dormancy
- nocturnal gas exchange
- rapid life cycles after rain
- protected growing points

---

# 12. SHADE / CANOPY ENGINEERING

Plants that create vertical structure.

Functions:
- shade
- humidity retention
- nesting habitat
- climbing surfaces
- wind reduction
- temperature moderation
- vertical food resources

Do not assume every climate supports tall forests. Height must make ecological and mechanical sense.

---

# 13. POLLINATION SUPPORT

Plants can create relationships with animals through:
- nectar
- pollen
- scent
- specialized flowers/reproductive structures
- seasonal mass flowering
- night flowering
- water/wind pollination

---

# 14. SEED DISPERSAL

Possible mechanisms:
- animals
- wind
- water
- explosive release
- gravity
- attachment to fur/skin
- ingestion and defecation

The mechanism should fit the environment and available animal communities.

---

# 15. TOXIC / DEFENSIVE PLANTS

Possible defenses:
- chemical deterrents
- irritants
- bitter compounds
- toxins
- spines
- tough tissues
- rapid wound sealing

Defense should impose an ecological cost on herbivores without automatically becoming a fantasy poison.

---

# 16. COASTAL / SALT-TOLERANT PLANTS

Possible functions:
- stabilize shorelines
- trap sediment
- provide nursery habitat
- tolerate salt exposure
- withstand tides/storms
- create transitional habitat between land and sea

---

# 17. DESERT / ARID SPECIALISTS

Possible strategies:
- water storage
- deep-rooted growth
- brief rain-triggered growth
- seed dormancy
- reduced leaf surface
- protected stomata
- underground storage organs
- nocturnal physiology

---

# 18. VOLCANIC / MINERAL-RICH SPECIALISTS

Possible strategies:
- rapid colonization
- tolerance of unstable soils
- mineral accumulation
- symbiotic nutrient acquisition
- deep anchoring
- heat/drought tolerance

---

# 19. ECOLOGICAL CONNECTORS

Plants can connect multiple functions.

Examples:
- fruit producer + canopy
- wetland stabilizer + aquatic habitat
- grass + soil stabilization
- shrub + browse + nesting cover
- pioneer + soil building
- coastal plant + nursery habitat

Prefer multi-function plants when biologically reasonable. They make ecosystems feel less artificially compartmentalized.

---

# 20. FLORA DESIGN RULES

A plant earns inclusion when it:
- fills a needed ecological function;
- demonstrates a meaningful evolutionary solution;
- defines a region;
- supports important fauna;
- creates distinctive habitat;
- or establishes a recurring resource.

Do not create a new species merely because two plants have different colors.

The goal is ecological coverage, not botanical completeness.
