# HEARTH CULTURAL MATRIX — FAMILY, PARTNERSHIP & KINSHIP

**Document class:** ANALYTICAL REFERENCE — NOT CANON  
**Purpose:** Cross-regional comparison of existing Hearth family, household, partnership, and kinship material.  
**Authority:** The regional cultural documents remain authoritative. This matrix does not reconcile contradictions, promote proposals, or create canon.

---

## Status Key

- **ESTABLISHED / LOCKED** — explicitly established in the source material.
- **WORKING / PROPOSED** — explicitly described as a working proposal or working profile.
- **OPEN / UNKNOWN** — the available source material does not establish a final answer.

## 1. CORE SOCIAL UNIT

| Region | Status | Source-derived model |
|---|---|---|
| Plains | WORKING / PROPOSED | Flexible household plus mobile work/travel groups; recurring anchor communities support people and resources that cannot always travel. |
| Mountains | ESTABLISHED / LOCKED | Extended specialist household/lineage; family is simultaneously household, teaching institution, and repository of specialist knowledge. |
| River | WORKING | Extended households of roughly 10–20 people within a larger river-corridor social network. |
| Wetlands | WORKING | 12–15-person functional group as the primary daily household/survival unit within a cooperative village. |
| Desert | WORKING | Extended family households within oasis communities; Lifekeeper network connects multiple oases. |
| Coast | WORKING | Community is the practical household; communal living rather than separate nuclear-family households. |

## 2. PARTNERSHIP / PARTNER SELECTION

| Region | Status | Source-derived model |
|---|---|---|
| Plains | WORKING / PROPOSED | Flexible and need-oriented; partners may meet through home communities, traveling groups, gatherings, or kin networks. |
| Mountains | ESTABLISHED / LOCKED | Generally within the same specialist occupation/lineage; relevant house heads arrange partnerships according to population and community need. |
| River | WORKING | Individuals have meaningful input; families/community consider age, health, kinship, relationships, community needs, and river connections. Both prospective partners can refuse. |
| Wetlands | WORKING / PROPOSED | Partners generally drawn from broader village/nearby groups rather than restricted to one's own 12–15-person group; exact mechanics remain open. |
| Desert | WORKING / PROPOSED | Partnerships commonly connect different oasis communities; not restricted to the same specialist lineage. |
| Coast | WORKING / PROPOSED | Partnerships exist within the communal structure and do not automatically create separate households. |

## 3. RESIDENCE AFTER PARTNERSHIP

| Region | Status | Source-derived model |
|---|---|---|
| Plains | WORKING / PROPOSED | Partners may remain with an existing community, join another mobile group, or move toward an anchor community according to skills and family responsibilities. |
| Mountains | ESTABLISHED / LOCKED | Couple normally remains with the specialist group in the village where their shared skill is most needed. |
| River | WORKING | Couples may remain in either partner's community or establish a new household where practical; moving does not sever original family ties. |
| Wetlands | WORKING / PROPOSED | Couple may remain in one partner's group or move to another according to population, skill balance, kinship, and group needs. |
| Desert | WORKING / PROPOSED | Couple may remain in either partner's oasis or move according to family/community needs, resources, and travel obligations. |
| Coast | WORKING / PROPOSED | Partnership does not normally create a separate household; community remains primary domestic unit. |

## 4. KINSHIP

| Region | Status | Source-derived model |
|---|---|---|
| Plains | WORKING | Kin can be distributed across communities because of mobility; broader network preserves family ties, hospitality, stories, routes, and ecological knowledge. |
| Mountains | ESTABLISHED / LOCKED | Specialist lineage is the central kin/knowledge structure; marriage exchanges cycle among villages and families to avoid repeatedly pairing close groups. |
| River | WORKING | Bilateral kinship; children belong to both parents' families. Kin relationships can extend upstream and downstream. A broader category of river kin creates reciprocal hospitality and assistance. |
| Wetlands | WORKING | Daily identity centers on the functional group, while village cooperation and seasonal exchanges connect people beyond the immediate group. Exact broader kinship mechanics remain open. |
| Desert | WORKING | Children belong to family and oasis community; Lifekeeper travel naturally creates connections between families and oases. |
| Coast | WORKING | Biological parentage is recognized, but parentage does not define exclusive responsibility; community adults can regard children as their own in the social sense. |

| Region | Status | Source-derived model |
|---|---|---|
| Plains | ESTABLISHED / DEVELOPED | Children remain attached to family while being raised within the broader community; young children may remain near anchor communities while older children increasingly accompany mobile groups. |
| Mountains | ESTABLISHED / LOCKED | Children grow within the extended specialist lineage and learn from many adults possessing the same specialist skill. |

| Coast | WORKING | Children are raised communally; adults collectively supervise, teach, discipline, feed, protect, train, and support them while biological parentage remains recognized. |

# 8. WHAT IS ACTUALLY DIFFERENT

The current source material supports these distinct organizing principles:

- **Plains:** family embedded within mobility and flexible group life.
- **Mountains:** family and lineage are inseparable from specialist knowledge and survival specialization.
- **River:** family extends along a connected geographic/social corridor.
- **Wetlands:** the functional group is the daily household while the village remains a cooperative survival system.
- **Desert:** family and oasis life are connected by a wider Lifekeeper network.
- **Coast:** community itself functions as the practical household, while biological parentage remains recognized.

These differences should be preserved rather than normalized into one generic Hearth family structure.

# 9. CURRENT OPEN DECISIONS

The source material identifies or leaves open several areas for future creative development:

1. Exact partnership mechanics for Plains, Wetlands, Desert, and Coast.
2. Complete separation/divorce mechanics outside the developed River model.
3. Reproduction and birth details as a family/kinship institution.
4. Childhood stages and formal coming-of-age structures beyond the existing developmental layer.
5. Leadership and authority within family/community structures where not yet established.
6. Elders and death/continuing kin obligations.
7. Cross-cultural marriage and kin networks between Hearth regions.
8. How partnership affects inheritance, household property, or specialist knowledge where applicable.
9. Settlement architecture as an expression of family and household structure.

These are **design questions, not missing facts to be guessed**.
