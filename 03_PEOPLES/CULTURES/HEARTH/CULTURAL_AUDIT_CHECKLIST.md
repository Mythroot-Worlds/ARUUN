**Rule:** This checklist identifies whether a topic is established. It does not decide the answer. Missing material must be developed with the culture's geography, ecology, history, and existing canon in mind.

# III. CROSS-CULTURAL RELATIONSHIP MATRIX

These should be deliberately developed rather than assumed.

## Plains ↔ Wetlands
- Shared cooperation
- Hospitality
- Flexible work groups
- Distributed knowledge
- Need-driven training
- Wetlands adapt these to water and seasonal flooding

## Wetlands ↔ River
- Ancient aquatic relationship
- Shared water vocabulary
- Shared ecological knowledge
- Possible overlapping stories/traditions
- Shared technologies
- Wetlands should absorb some River cultural history rather than being treated as only a Plains offshoot

## Mountains ↔ Desert
- Isolation pressures
- Specialized knowledge
- Strong internal cohesion
- Potentially lineage-linked expertise
- Different source of isolation:
  - Mountains = terrain
  - Desert = distance/water scarcity

## River ↔ Coast
- Ancient aquatic cultural relationship
- Shared water traditions
- Shared ecological vocabulary
- Exchange corridors
- Uncertain cultural precedence / shared ancestry

## Plains ↔ River
- Major contact through rivers crossing plains
- Shared food/resource exchange
- Possible influence on travel and water knowledge

## Coast ↔ All
- Shoreline as movement/information corridor
- Salt and preserved foods as potential trade resources
- High cultural connectivity

## Desert ↔ All
- Oasis/travel routes as contact points
- Lifekeepers as information carriers
- Water knowledge as culturally important
- Strong internal identity despite external geographic isolation

# IV. MARRIAGE / PARTNERSHIP DESIGN POOL

This is a **toolbox**, not a menu that every culture must use.

The goal is to develop a finite set of internally coherent mechanisms that can be reused across cultures and modified to fit local ecology.

Possible mechanisms:

1. **Village exogamy**
   - Marriage normally outside one's home community.

2. **Regional exchange cycles**
   - Communities pair with different partner communities across generations/seasons.

3. **Lineage exchange**
   - Specific family/skill lineages exchange partners with corresponding lineages elsewhere.

4. **Need-based residence**
   - Newly partnered people settle where their skills are most needed.

5. **Flexible residence**
   - Couple chooses or negotiates which community they join.

6. **Community matching**
   - Elders/families help identify compatible partners.

7. **Individual courtship**
   - Individuals choose partners with community approval.

8. **Kin-network matching**
   - Existing family relationships guide partner selection.

9. **Rotational inter-community gatherings**
   - Marriage opportunities occur during scheduled regional gatherings.

10. **Seasonal partner exchange**
    - Communities maintain formal reciprocal marriage relationships.

11. **Skill-lineage pairing**
    - Partner selection intentionally connects different specialist lineages.

12. **Broad endogamy**
    - Marriage remains mostly within the cultural region but avoids close kin.

13. **Clan exogamy**
    - Marriage within the broader people but outside one's clan.

14. **Household absorption**
    - One partner joins the other's existing household/community.

15. **Mutual household formation**
    - Couple forms a new household independent of either family.

16. **Foster/kinship-based adoption**
    - Children can be raised by extended kin without losing cultural belonging.

17. **Partner redistribution after community loss**
    - In demographic emergencies, communities actively help maintain viable family structures.

18. **Traveling-network partnerships**
    - Mobile specialists create relationships across otherwise isolated communities.

19. **Multiple-community ancestry**
    - A person's recognized kin network may span several settlements.

20. **Mixed system**
    - Any combination above where ecology and social structure support it.

### Important rule

We should **not randomly assign these**.

For each culture, ask:

> What does this society need marriage to accomplish?

Then choose the mechanism that solves that problem.

Example:
- Mountain isolation → inter-village lineage exchange makes sense.
- Desert oasis network → mobile specialists can create cross-oasis kinship.
- River connectivity → broad regional partner networks may work.
- Coast → high-contact communities may support broader local endogamy with careful lineage tracking.

# VI. FUTURE CONTINENTS

This same checklist should be reused for every continental Peoples project.

However:

**Do not copy Hearth cultures onto other continents.**

Each new continent begins with:
**geography → climate → ecology → water → population → survival pressures**

Then the same checklist is applied.

The marriage/kinship design pool can be reused as a creative toolbox, but each implementation must be justified by the new culture's environment, population structure, history, and social needs.

---

# VII. CANON RULE

> **Documents record our decisions. They do not make our decisions.**

If an existing document conflicts with a better-developed cultural idea, flag the conflict, decide what makes the world work best, then update the record.

Do not preserve an old statement merely because it was written first.
