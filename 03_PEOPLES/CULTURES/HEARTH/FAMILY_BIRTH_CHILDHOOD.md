# MYTHROOT WORLDS — HEARTH PEOPLES
## Birth, Childhood & Coming-of-Age — Working Canon v0.1

**Status:** SAVED WORKING CANON — approved framework; later refinements remain possible.

This document records the approved Hearth developmental framework for Plains, Mountains, River, Wetlands, Desert, and Coast.

## PLAINS
- Birth is supported by experienced women; anchor communities or slower groups provide stability when needed.
- Infants are carried while adults work and learn through constant observation.
- Children gradually copy useful adult tasks; aptitude is observed, but community need can override preference.
- Adulthood is demonstrated by reliable contribution to survival.
**Principle:** Carry your share.

## MOUNTAINS
- Birth occurs within the extended specialist lineage.
- Infants remain immersed in their specialist family's environment.
- Training begins early through simple lineage-specific games and tasks.
- Adolescents perform real work and may be redirected if their aptitude differs from the expected specialty.
- Adulthood requires sustained competence recognized by the specialist lineage.
**Principle:** Master your lineage's knowledge.

## RIVER
- Birth is supported by the extended household and community.
- Infants are gradually and safely introduced to water.
- Children learn fishing, gathering, swimming, currents, plants, animals, boats/rafts, and weather.
- Adolescents accompany experienced adults on supervised journeys to other river settlements.
- Adulthood means being able to travel the river safely enough to be useful.
**Principle:** Read the water and know the network.

## WETLANDS
- Birth is primarily supported by the 12–15-person functional group.
- Infants are cared for by many adults within the group.
- Children rotate through gathering, fishing, hunting, weaving, food processing, scouting, and terrain/water awareness.
- Adolescents can spend seasons with other groups to discover aptitude and spread knowledge.
- Adulthood means being trusted to carry an independent portion of the group's survival burden.
**Principle:** Become useful to your group and village.

## DESERT
- Birth occurs within the oasis community with experienced support.
- Infants remain close to family and learn early that water must not be wasted.
- Education deliberately has two stages: **theory and practice**.
- Earlier in the day, elders teach plants, animals, weather, terrain, water conservation, tracks, resources, survival rules, stories, and accumulated knowledge.
- Later, experienced adults take children into the environment and demonstrate those lessons.
- Children rotate through elders, gatherers, hunters, Water Watchers, animal handlers, Lifekeepers, and other experienced adults rather than learning only from parents.
- Parents remain deeply involved but are not the child's entire educational system.
- Adolescents begin supervised travel between nearby settlements; exceptional candidates may later attempt the Lifekeeper Trials.
- Ordinary adulthood is demonstrated by responsible handling of scarce resources, especially water.
**Principle:** The elders teach the knowledge. The experienced show the practice. The child learns by doing.

## COAST
- Birth is a community event; biological parentage remains recognized.
- Infants live within the communal environment and learn by observing everyone.
- Children rotate through fishing, gathering, hunting, land reading, guarding, weaving, tools, food processing, and preservation.
- Everyone learns broad survival skills rather than inheriting a narrow role from parents.
- Adolescents increasingly participate fully in communal work cycles.
- Adulthood means being able to care for oneself and contribute reliably to the community.
**Principle:** Become broadly capable and dependable.

## COMPARATIVE MODEL

| People | Developmental emphasis |
|---|---|
| Plains | Carry your share |
| Mountains | Master your lineage's knowledge |
| River | Read the water and know the network |
| Wetlands | Become useful to your group and village |
| Desert | Learn knowledge from elders, then prove it in practice |
| Coast | Become broadly capable and dependable |

## CANON NOTE
These systems are distinct responses to the same survival problem: turning children into adults capable of keeping their communities alive.

Documents record our decisions; they do not override future creative decisions.
