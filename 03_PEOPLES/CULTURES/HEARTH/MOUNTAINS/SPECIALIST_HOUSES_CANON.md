# HEARTH MOUNTAINS — SPECIALIST HOUSES

**Status: CANON**

## Settlement
Mountain villages contain approximately **60–80 people**.

This is a normal population range, not a rigid target. Local ecological conditions may produce variation.

## Four Houses
Each Mountain village is organized into **four broad specialist Houses**, generally containing approximately **15–20 people each**.

The Houses are knowledge lineages, not narrow occupations. They maintain deep expertise across related survival competencies while all members of the village learn foundational survival skills.

### Food House
Hunting, tracking, gathering, animal behavior, butchery and food preparation, wild cultivation, locally appropriate fishing, preservation, and related food-resource knowledge.

### Craft House
Stone, wood, bone and antler, fiber and weaving, cordage, hide and leather, tools, weapons, clothing, containers, repair, and maintenance.

### Shelter & Terrain House
Shelter construction, site selection, mountain travel and routes, terrain hazards, weather and environmental adaptation, use of natural formations, and maintenance of the physical spaces the community depends upon. This does not imply large-scale engineering or infrastructure.

### Knowledge & Care House
Elders, teaching, history, oral tradition, medicine, birth, child development, cultural memory, and preservation/transmission of specialist knowledge.

## Universal knowledge
House membership does not restrict basic survival knowledge. Every Mountain person learns foundational skills such as food acquisition, fire, cooking, shelter, tool use, navigation/environmental awareness, and care for injured or vulnerable community members.

A House represents **depth of knowledge and inherited/maintained specialization**, not exclusive ownership of a skill.

## Environmental specialization
Skills strongly dependent on local conditions remain regional or cross-house competencies. Fishing and specialized water knowledge are examples. Fire and other fundamental survival skills are universal rather than specialist Houses.

## Existing Mountain social structure
The established same-house/same-specialization partnership system remains in force. The annual Mountain Conclave continues to serve marriage exchange, specialist population balancing, knowledge exchange, trade, and regional coordination.

## Supersession
This document formally supersedes the previously open questions concerning Mountain settlement size, number of Houses, House structure, and population quotas. The earlier working document remains in the repository as historical development material; this document is the current canon statement.
