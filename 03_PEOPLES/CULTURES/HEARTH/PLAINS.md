# HEARTH — GREAT PLAINS PEOPLE
## Cultural Base v0.1

**Status:** WORKING CANON / PEOPLES PHASE

## Geographic foundation
The Great Plains are Hearth's largest human landscape, within the continent's ~1.5 million people. The Hearth population layer allocates 450,000 people (30%) to grassland/woodland environments. Residential communities generally run ~80–150 people, with smaller groups and larger seasonal aggregations possible. Hearth has strong connectivity and gene flow.

## Core character
The Plains people are highly mobile, cooperative, environmentally alert, and broadly welcoming to peaceful outsiders.

They are **not universally nomadic**. Mobility ranges from highly mobile to semi-mobile, with repeatedly used anchor communities.

## Mobility
Movement responds to:
- herbivore migrations;
- predator movements;
- seasonal plants, seeds, grasses and fruit;
- water;
- floodplain productivity;
- weather and local danger.

A practical cultural rule is **don't get caught slipping**: environmental awareness is continuous because predator and prey movements can change where it is safe and productive to remain.

## Anchor communities
Semi-permanent/repeatedly used locations support:
- elders who can no longer travel;
- very young children;
- injured/recovering people;
- childcare and teaching;
- food storage/processing;
- craft and repair;
- animal management;
- knowledge keeping.

Elders are not considered useless; their responsibilities change when long-distance travel is no longer possible.

## Traveling groups
The practical mobile unit is generally **10–25 people**, with **10–15** a common small group.

Groups are functional and flexible rather than permanently familial. A group may contain hunters, gatherers, scouts/lookouts, carriers, animal handlers, generalists, and children able to help. Groups split, merge, and change membership according to need.

Household sleeping/family arrangements do not determine daytime work-group membership.

## Daily/nightly rhythm
Aruun's 32-hour day does not mean Aruunites remain awake for 32 hours. Communities use overlapping periods of work, rest, and watch.

Day-oriented work includes gathering, protective hunting escorts, water collection, toolmaking, hide work, food processing, childcare, teaching, and camp maintenance.

Night-oriented activity is especially hunter-heavy, with scouts and lookouts supporting it.

**Someone is always watching.** Watch can overlap with other work; dedicated lookouts/scouts are used when danger warrants it.

## Hunting
Hunting operates at multiple scales:
- local short-duration hunts;
- coordinated hunts for large/dangerous prey;
- long-range tracking of herds.

Large kills can require field processing, temporary processing locations, runners, retrieval groups, preservation, and coordinated transport. The Plains diet remains diversified rather than relying exclusively on megafauna.

## Gathering
Gatherers follow plant seasonality, fruiting, seeds, roots, greens, wetland resources, insects and other small foods where appropriate. Gathering groups may separate from hunters and reconnect later.

## Social organization
Four useful layers:
1. **Household** — intimate/family life and sleeping arrangements.
2. **Work/travel group** — current practical work and movement.
3. **Community** — recurring social obligations and anchor locations.
4. **Plains cultural network** — the broad connected world of related communities sharing language continuums, stories, routes, ecological knowledge, marriage exchange and hospitality.

A person can change work/travel groups without necessarily changing wider identity.

## Skill training
Children are not expected to know what they have not been taught.

Learning progresses through observation, imitation, play, simple useful tasks, and increasingly serious training.

**Need determines training.** If more hunters, gatherers, toolmakers, scouts, weavers, animal handlers, builders, etc. are needed, children are trained accordingly. People can learn multiple disciplines.

Critical knowledge is deliberately redundant: skills belong to the people rather than being dependent on one individual.

## Leadership
Leadership is functional and responsibility-based.

Potential roles include hunt, gathering, craft, teaching/knowledge, watch/scouting, animal handling, and anchor-community coordination.

Authority is situational: competence and community trust in one responsibility do not automatically confer authority over everything else.

## Trust and accountability
An honest mistake is distinguished from negligence or deliberate recklessness.

When serious harm follows a decision:
- affected people are heard;
- the relevant work group is questioned;
- the responsible person explains the decision;
- experienced people provide context;
- the community evaluates whether trust remains.

The key question is:

> **Do we still trust this person to make decisions that affect people who depend on them?**

Loss of trust can remove someone from leadership or dangerous responsibility. Exact disciplinary law remains open.

## Hospitality
Peaceful outsiders are generally welcomed with food, water, temporary shelter, and practical assistance.

Hospitality is a survival adaptation based on reciprocity: anyone may someday be the traveler who needs another community.

Hospitality does not mean instant trust. A welcomed stranger can still be observed and evaluated.

## Environmental knowledge
Plains people continuously monitor:
- predator territory;
- prey movement;
- weather;
- water;
- tracks;
- scents;
- sounds;
- unusual animal behavior;
- absence of expected wildlife;
- seasonal timing.

Stories and conversation transmit this information.

## Daily social life
When work groups return, people reconnect through eating, processing food, repairing tools, teaching children, sharing news, telling stories, joking, discussing weather and animal movement, and planning future travel.

They are people, not abstract survival machines.

## Working cultural principle

> **Everyone contributes. Everyone learns. Everyone watches. Everyone may someday need the help of someone else.**
