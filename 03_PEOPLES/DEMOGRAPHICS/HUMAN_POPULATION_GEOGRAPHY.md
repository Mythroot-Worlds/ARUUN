# MYTHROOT — HUMAN POPULATION GEOGRAPHY LAYER (HPGL)
## Working Human Geography Framework v0.3

**Status:** UPDATED WORKING CANON / PRESERVATION + PEOPLES-PHASE UPDATE

> **Versioning rule:** This revision preserves v0.2 and restores/retains material identified in v0.1 that must not be lost. New material is additive unless explicitly marked otherwise.

## Purpose

The HPGL determines **where humans can plausibly live, how densely, how they move, and how isolated or connected populations are** before named peoples/cultures are created.

It is a geographic/ecological layer, not a list of cultures.

## Working demographic baseline

Global human population: **~3.5–4 million**.

Working distribution:
- Hearth: ~1.5 million
- Shattered: ~800,000
- Rift: ~1.0 million
- Lost: ~300,000

These continental totals are treated as **locked working demographic canon for the current Peoples phase**, while fine-grained settlement placement remains revisable.

## Residential scale

A typical residential community may contain roughly **80–150 people**, with smaller groups (~40–80) and larger seasonal aggregations (~200–500+) also possible.

A residential community is not necessarily a complete cultural population; multiple communities can share language, marriage networks, ecological knowledge, trade, and seasonal gathering sites.

This is deliberately different from assuming every human population is permanently settled.

## Mobility model

Human movement may be relatively sedentary around unusually productive resources; seasonally mobile; semi-nomadic; fully nomadic; coastal/marine oriented; river/lake oriented; herd-following; plant-season following; or mixed, changing by season.

Large-animal migrations and seasonal plant, fruit, seed, fish, or wetland abundance can drive human movement.

### Technology constraint

Aquatic mobility must follow the technological stage actually established for Aruunites.

Early water use can include swimming, floating logs/natural flotation, simple rafts, and later simple dugout or comparable craft when woodworking supports them.

**Ships, advanced seafaring, and open-ocean navigation are NOT assumed at this stage.**

## Population geography rule

Do **not** distribute humans evenly across a continent.

Population concentrates where several factors overlap:

**food productivity + reliable water + tolerable climate + manageable terrain + safe access + seasonal predictability**

Population becomes sparse where:

**resource scarcity + extreme climate + dangerous terrain + high megafauna pressure + difficult travel**

Population percentage is not the same thing as geographic density. A large population may be dispersed across a huge territory.

## Continental distribution

### HEARTH — HUMAN POPULATION GEOGRAPHY

**Working population:** ~1.5 million

Hearth has the strongest geographic connectivity of the four major landmasses. Major rivers, grasslands, woodlands, wetlands, and temperate/coastal environments provide broad corridors for population movement.

### SHATTERED

**Working population:** ~800,000

Fragmentation creates stronger geographic isolation. Populations cluster around productive fragments, river valleys, coastal shelves, and accessible mountain passes.

### RIFT

**Working population:** ~1.0 million

Rift geography concentrates people along rift floors, lakes, wetlands, river corridors, productive volcanic soils, and accessible shoulders while active terrain creates local barriers.

### LOST

**Working population:** ~300,000

Lost remains sparsely connected to the other continental systems. Its population geography is shaped primarily by its own ecological history and long isolation.

## Status rule

This is a working population-geography framework. It should guide later settlement, culture, language, and demographic work without pretending that unresolved fine-grained placements are locked.
