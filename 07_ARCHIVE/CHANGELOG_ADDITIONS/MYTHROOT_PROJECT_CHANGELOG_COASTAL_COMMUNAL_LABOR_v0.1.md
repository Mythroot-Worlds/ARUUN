# MYTHROOT WORLDS — CHANGELOG ADDITION
## Hearth / Coastal Peoples — Communal Labor and Food Preservation

### Locked decisions

1. Coastal communities generally operate as **generalist societies**.
2. Children rotate through major community activities rather than being raised into a single occupation.
3. Adult specialization can occur, but it is layered on top of broad competence.
4. Coastal labor is primarily organized into **large communal work waves** rather than permanent hunter/gatherer/craft groups.
5. When the community fishes, most able-bodied members participate.
6. Elders, very young children, injured people, caregivers, watchkeepers, and task-excluded people remain in necessary supporting roles.
7. After major work, the community commonly reconvenes for communal meals.
8. Communities then rotate collectively into other major tasks such as weaving, net repair, toolmaking, tanning, food processing, construction, and maintenance.
9. Communal labor creates deliberate redundancy so essential knowledge is not lost when individual specialists die or become unavailable.
10. Work itself is a major form of social life, teaching, mentorship, humor, and communal identity.
11. Coastal peoples develop deliberate small-scale salt collection/evaporation and use salt for preservation, especially fish.
12. Salt-based preservation increases food security and can support somewhat larger/stabler communities where ecology permits.
13. The Coastal peoples remain exposed to severe marine and weather hazards; preservation and versatility are resilience mechanisms, not guarantees of safety.
14. Coastal and River peoples remain culturally linked through an ancient aquatic network, with no requirement to establish which culture came first.

### Core cultural principle

> **No one carries the whole burden when everyone can carry part of it.**

### Six Hearth cultural foundations now established

- **Plains:** mobility, cooperation, need-driven specialization.
- **Mountains:** specialized knowledge, kin lineages, inter-village marriage networks.
- **River:** broad knowledge, aquatic connectivity, flow-oriented culture.
- **Wetlands:** adaptation to environmental change, local mobility, seasonal high-ground aggregation.
- **Desert:** oasis network, mobile Lifekeepers, water knowledge, service-oriented martial tradition.
- **Coast:** generalist competence, communal labor waves, salt preservation, shoreline exchange.

### Status

The six regional cultural groups now have sufficient **basic cultural foundations** to proceed to the next Peoples phase.

This does not mean they are fully detailed. Family, partnership, reproduction, kinship, leadership, belief, art, trade, conflict, and other deeper layers remain to be developed.
