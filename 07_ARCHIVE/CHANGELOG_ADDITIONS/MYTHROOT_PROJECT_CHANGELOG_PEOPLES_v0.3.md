### Great Plains decisions recorded
1. Plains people are highly to seasonally mobile, but not universally nomadic.
2. Movement follows prey, predators, plants, water, weather and seasonal productivity.
3. Repeated-use anchor communities support elders who cannot travel, very young children, injured/recovering people, storage, crafts, teaching, animal management and recurring social life.
4. Practical travel groups generally contain ~10–25 people, with ~10–15 a common small unit.
5. Work/travel groups are functional and fluid; they can split, merge and change membership.
6. Communities maintain continuous watch; night activity is especially hunter-heavy.
7. Peaceful outsiders are generally welcomed; hospitality is reciprocal survival infrastructure, not automatic trust.
8. Households, work/travel groups, communities and the wider Plains network are distinct layers of belonging.
9. Children learn through observation, play, useful participation and need-driven training.
10. Community need determines how strongly children are trained toward hunting, gathering, crafts, scouting, animal handling, etc.
11. Skills are redundantly distributed so critical knowledge does not depend on one individual.
12. Leadership is functional and situational; authority comes from demonstrated competence and trust in a specific responsibility.
13. Serious mistakes are distinguished from negligence/recklessness; accountability focuses on whether trust remains.
14. Environmental observation and information-sharing are core survival functions.
15. Daily life includes dispersal into work groups followed by communal eating, processing, repair, teaching, stories, humor and planning.
16. The working Plains principle is: **Everyone contributes. Everyone learns. Everyone watches. Everyone may someday need the help of someone else.**
