# MYTHROOT — OCEAN & MARINE CLIMATE REFERENCE
## Working Global Circulation Model v0.1

### Purpose

This is the working oceanographic layer connecting:

**latitude → winds → currents → heat/moisture → upwelling → marine productivity → coastal climate → terrestrial ecology**

This document is a design reference, not a claim of exact numerical climate modeling.

---

# 1. PHYSICAL BASIS

Surface ocean circulation is primarily driven by global wind systems, with the direction altered by planetary rotation (Coriolis) and constrained by continents/coastlines. Deep circulation is additionally influenced by temperature and salinity differences.

The major Northern Hemisphere subtropical circulation is clockwise; the major Southern Hemisphere subtropical circulation is counterclockwise.

Upwelling occurs when surface water is displaced away from an area and deeper water rises to replace it. Deep water is typically colder and nutrient-rich, so upwelling can strongly increase biological productivity.

# 2. WORKING LATITUDE FRAMEWORK

- Hearth: approximately 15°N–50°N
- Shattered: approximately 5°N–30°N
- Rift: approximately 15°S–20°N
- Lost: approximately 15°S–50°S

These remain working latitude bands rather than permanently locked coordinates.

---

# 3. NORTHERN SUBTROPICAL GYRE

A broad clockwise subtropical circulation occupies the northern ocean system.

### Western boundary current
A relatively narrow, warm, faster current moves toward higher latitudes along the western side of the ocean basin.

Effects:
- transports heat poleward;
- raises coastal sea temperatures;
- increases evaporation and atmospheric moisture;
- can create warmer/wetter coastal climates.

### Eastern boundary current
A broader, slower, generally cooler current returns toward lower latitudes along the eastern side.

Effects:
- cooler coastal waters;
- reduced evaporation relative to warm-current coasts;
- greater potential for coastal aridity/fog;
- favorable conditions for upwelling where winds and coastline orientation cooperate.

This creates a natural asymmetry between coasts at similar latitudes.

---

# 4. EQUATORIAL CIRCULATION

Near the equator, the normal subtropical gyres do not close into a simple single circulation because Coriolis influence approaches zero.

Trade-wind-driven surface flow generally moves warm surface water westward.

The equatorial region can therefore produce:
- strong east/west temperature contrasts;
- warm western tropical waters;
- return flows at depth;
- seasonal/current variability;
- high productivity where mixing/upwelling occurs.

Rift crosses the equatorial zone and therefore receives the strongest influence from this system.

---

# 5. SOUTHERN SUBTROPICAL GYRE

A broad counterclockwise subtropical circulation surrounds the southern oceanic system.

### Western boundary current
Warm water is transported poleward along a western ocean boundary.

### Eastern boundary current
Cooler water returns equatorward along an eastern ocean boundary.

This produces a Lost climate contrast similar in principle to the northern system, but reversed in circulation direction.

---

# 6. MAJOR WORKING CURRENT SYSTEMS

## Northern Warm Current
**Region:** western side of the northern ocean basin.

**Direction:** generally toward the northeast/poleward.

**Primary effects:**
- warms northern coastal waters;
- increases humidity;
- moderates winter temperatures;
- supports productive coastal seas.

## Northern Cool Return Current
**Region:** eastern side of the northern ocean basin.

**Direction:** generally southwest/equatorward.

**Primary effects:**
- cooler coastal waters;
- lower evaporation;
- increased coastal fog potential;
- candidate upwelling region.

## Equatorial Westward Current
**Region:** near-equatorial ocean.

**Direction:** predominantly westward.

**Primary effects:**
- transports warm surface water;
- reinforces western tropical warmth;
- interacts with Rift's equatorial coastlines.

## Southern Warm Current
**Region:** western side of the southern ocean basin.

**Direction:** generally southward/poleward.

**Primary effects:**
- warms Lost's relevant coast;
- increases coastal moisture;
- supports productive warm-temperate marine ecosystems.

## Southern Cool Return Current
**Region:** eastern side of the southern ocean basin.

**Direction:** generally northward/equatorward.

**Primary effects:**
- cools Lost's relevant coast;
- favors seasonal upwelling where winds align;
- can produce cooler/drier maritime conditions.

# 7. UPWELLING ZONES

Upwelling should be treated as one of the world's major ecological engines.

## Shattered — major eastern/coastal upwelling zone

A cool-current coast on one major Shattered fragment is designated as a significant seasonal upwelling region.

Expected chain:

**wind → surface water offshore → deep nutrient-rich water rises → phytoplankton → zooplankton → fish → marine predators → coastal fauna**

Effects:
- exceptionally productive waters;
- strong seasonal marine biomass;
- major fishing opportunities;
- seabird/coastal predator concentrations;
- cold coastal water;
- frequent fog potential;
- strong contrast with warmer neighboring waters.

This provides an important reason for Shattered to have extremely productive western/ocean-facing ecosystems despite surrounding dry regions.

## Lost — major southern/eastern coastal upwelling zone

A second major upwelling region occurs along a cool-current-facing Lost coast.

Expected chain:

**deep nutrient input → plankton bloom → fish biomass → large marine predators → nutrient transfer onto coast**

This supports Lost's enormous marine ecosystem and provides an ecological bridge between its coastal and terrestrial systems.

## Rift — localized upwelling

Rift does not need a continent-scale permanent upwelling belt.

Instead, its:
- irregular coastlines;
- rift lakes;
- enclosed seas;
- volcanic margins;
- seasonal winds;
- continental geometry

produce localized productive zones.

These should vary strongly by season.

---

# 8. DOWNWELLING / LOW-PRODUCTIVITY PERIODS

Where winds pile surface water against a coast, surface water can sink.

This produces downwelling.

In Mythroot this creates useful seasonal cycles:

**productive season → nutrient input → biological boom → depletion/consumption → downwelling/stratification → lower productivity**

Do not treat productive marine zones as permanently producing unlimited food.

Seasonality matters.

---

# 9. MARITIME INFLUENCE

"Maritime influence" means the ocean modifies nearby land climate.

Water heats and cools more slowly than land, so oceans can:
- reduce coastal temperature extremes;
- provide atmospheric moisture;
- delay seasonal heating/cooling;
- create fog;
- alter precipitation;
- transport heat poleward.

Therefore two places at the same latitude can have very different climates depending on:
- current temperature;
- wind direction;
- distance from ocean;
- mountain barriers;
- coastline orientation.

---

# 10. CONTINENTAL CONSEQUENCES

## Hearth
Expect strong contrast between:
- warmer/moister current-influenced coast;
- cooler/drier return-current coast;
- continental interior.

Interior climates become increasingly continental:
- greater seasonal temperature range;
- less direct ocean moisture;
- stronger basin aridity where subtropical circulation and mountains reinforce it.

## Shattered
Ocean influence is dominant.

Expect:
- strong regional differences between fragments;
- warm/cool current contrasts;
- seasonal marine productivity;
- foggy/coastal regions;
- dry regions immediately adjacent to productive waters;
- migration opportunities driven by coastal food pulses.

The Farwader migration system is especially compatible with this.

## Rift
Equatorial circulation plus active geography produces:
- humid equatorial zones;
- seasonal tropical zones;
- localized upwelling;
- major lake productivity;
- wet/dry contrasts;
- strong elevation effects.

## Lost
Lost combines:
- enormous continental interiors;
- warm-current coasts;
- cool-current coasts;
- subtropical dry belts;
- temperate southern regions;
- strong coastal productivity in designated upwelling zones.

Its marine ecosystems should be exceptionally important because of its enormous coastline and isolation.

---

# 11. MARINE PRODUCTIVITY BELTS

### High productivity
- major upwelling coasts;
- shallow continental shelves;
- estuaries;
- river mouths;
- seasonal nutrient fronts;
- productive inland seas.

### Moderate productivity
- temperate coastal waters;
- mixed shelves;
- seasonal current boundaries.

### Lower open-ocean productivity
- warm, strongly stratified subtropical gyre interiors.

These broad patterns give marine animals reasons to cluster, migrate, breed, or avoid particular waters.

# 12. HUMAN CONSEQUENCES

The current model creates obvious human geography later.

Potential high-value human environments:
- productive upwelling coasts;
- estuaries;
- river mouths;
- shallow inland seas;
- seasonal floodplains;
- predictable migration corridors.

Potential dangers:
- fog;
- storm exposure;
- dangerous marine predators;
- seasonal resource crashes;
- coastal flooding;
- current-driven navigation hazards;
- sudden productivity shifts.

This should influence later settlement and cultural development.
