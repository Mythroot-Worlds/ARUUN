# HEARTH — DESERT PEOPLES
## Cultural Base v0.1 — First Pass

**Status:** WORKING CANON / PEOPLES PHASE

### Core philosophy
The Desert peoples resemble the Mountain peoples in some social pressures, but their isolation comes from **distance and water scarcity** rather than terrain.

Working principle:
> **Survival belongs to the people who know where to go, when to go, and what not to reveal.**

They are self-reliant, internally cohesive, cautious with outsiders, and exceptionally knowledgeable about water and routes.

### Community structure
Communities are expected to be relatively small compared with abundant Plains populations. Exact population and settlement density remain open until desert ecology and water distribution are mapped in detail.

Communities organize around known water sources, seasonal water, shelter, food-producing areas, safe travel corridors, dangerous terrain, and resource caches/storage.

### Isolation
Desert isolation is logistical. A nearby community can be effectively distant when crossing the terrain requires scarce water and precise timing.

This encourages strong internal cooperation and deep local knowledge.

### Knowledge
The desert rewards specialized knowledge of water locations, seasonal springs, groundwater signs, storm patterns, safe routes, shelter, edible plants, animal movement, and dangerous terrain.

Knowledge may become concentrated in families or trusted groups, echoing the Mountain lineage model, but exact hereditary specialization remains open.

### Hospitality
A peaceful traveler may receive water, food, shelter, and immediate survival assistance. But assistance does not automatically include revealing hidden water sources, routes, caches, or strategically important information.

Working distinction:
> **You may be kept alive without being given access to everything we know.**

This is a direction, not final law.

### Adaptation
The desert rewards preparation for heat, cold, storms, water scarcity, long travel intervals, failed resource locations, and shifting predator/prey patterns.

Exact mobility, travel ranges, community sizes, marriage patterns, and specialization remain to be developed.

### Mountain relationship
Both cultures may share internal cohesion, specialized knowledge, preparedness, competence, and careful trust.
- Mountains: isolation through terrain.
- Desert: isolation through distance and water scarcity.

Their institutions and traditions should not be assumed identical.

### Still to develop
Family; partnerships; kinship; reproduction; lineage; leadership; water rights; hospitality law; trade; seasonal migration; settlement patterns; conflict; beliefs; arts; material culture.
