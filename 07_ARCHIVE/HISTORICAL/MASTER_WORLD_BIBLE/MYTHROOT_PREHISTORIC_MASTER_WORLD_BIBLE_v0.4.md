# Canon Status Rule

**LOCKED CANON** requires an explicit decision.

**PROVISIONAL** means we may currently build around an idea without permanently committing the world to it.

**OPEN** means it has not yet been decided.

**RETIRED** means an explored idea has been deliberately rejected.

Seed material, research, profile guidance, and model suggestions do not become canon by implication.

# Current Canon Snapshot

## LOCKED / ESTABLISHED

- The world is **very low magic**.
- Magic is **real** and can have genuine effects.
- Magic does **not** operate as a game system.
- There are **no spells, levels, mana pools, numerical effects, quantified healing, or other game-mechanical magic structures** in the world.
- Rituals, chants, songs, prayers, gestures, preparations, herbs, pastes, smoke, and similar practices may be genuine vehicles or components of magic.
- Magic can **restore**, but it does not simply **heal away** physical consequence.
- Magic can help, stabilize, encourage, or improve the chances of recovery, but it **cannot guarantee recovery**.
- Magic can influence an outcome; **it cannot dictate the outcome**.
- Magic does not radically transform the world into a high-magic setting.
- Humanity is **not the apex species**.
- Human survival depends heavily on **knowledge, cooperation, adaptation, and accumulated experience**.
- Large-animal hunting is dangerous even for highly skilled people.
- A successful hunt can still create injuries, deaths, lost labor, medical burdens, and demographic/social consequences.
- Injury matters. People survive, adapt, and may carry permanent consequences.
- A person who can no longer perform a previous role is not automatically useless; communities adapt roles around changed abilities.
- Ecology is intended to be a major source of culture, conflict, and story.
- Animals should not exist merely as decorative monsters or spectacle.
- The world is intended to support stories rather than function as an exhaustive encyclopedia.

## PROVISIONAL / SEED MATERIAL

The original **Prehistoric World Concept v1.0** establishes additional starting ideas, including:

- wildly separated prehistoric periods sharing one ecosystem;
- small and scattered human civilization;
- constrained technology;
- subtle/consequential magic and spirituality;
- ecology functioning as a narrative engine;
- meaningful human relationships with predators.

These remain **seed material unless explicitly locked during worldbuilding**.

# 1. World Identity

## 1.1 Working Classification

**Prehistoric / Anachronistic World**

The term “prehistoric” is intentionally loose.

The world is not being designed as a strict reconstruction of any single historical period on Earth.

The eventual setting may combine prehistoric conditions, anachronistic species, fantasy elements, and original ecological relationships.

## 1.2 Core Creative Pressure

> **Human civilization is an ecological participant rather than an ecological master.**

The world should not treat humans as the default dominant force.

The environment, animals, resources, climate, and geography should materially shape human life.

## 1.3 Intended World Experience

**OPEN — to be deliberately established during foundation development.**

The world should ultimately have a clearly intentional experience rather than relying on “prehistoric” as its emotional identity.

Possible directions include awe, danger, vulnerability, wonder, discovery, belonging, intimacy, brutality, mystery, resilience, or interdependence.

No single choice is locked yet.

---

# 2. Humanity

## 2.1 Human Position

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED**

Humanity is not the apex species.

Humans survive through:

- knowledge;
- cooperation;
- adaptation;
- preparation;
- accumulated experience;
- understanding of the environment.

A single person is vulnerable.

A community is considerably more capable.

## 2.2 Settlement Philosophy

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED**

Human settlements are not assumed to be truly safe.

People deliberately identify locations that are **dramatically safer than the available alternatives**.

Settlement decisions are based on accumulated observation of the environment, including:

- animal movement;
- migration patterns;
- predator territories;
- terrain;
- visibility;
- water;
- seasonal changes;
- natural barriers;
- escape routes;
- resource access.

A settlement may exist because dangerous animals rarely enter the area, because major migrations pass elsewhere, because the terrain makes approach difficult, or because the location provides several advantages simultaneously.

> **Humans do not make the world safe. They learn where it is safer to live.**

## 2.3 Environmental Knowledge as Infrastructure

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED**

Knowledge of a place is itself a form of human infrastructure.

A community's accumulated understanding of its surroundings may determine:

- where it settles;
- when it moves;
- when it hunts;
- where it gathers resources;
- when it avoids particular areas;
- how it prepares for seasonal changes;
- how it responds to animal movement.

This knowledge may be transmitted through:

- observation;
- teaching;
- apprenticeship;
- oral tradition;
- ritual;
- stories;
- repeated practice.

Loss of environmental knowledge can therefore threaten a community even when its physical structures remain intact.

## 2.4 Settlement Defenses

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED PRINCIPLE**

Human settlements do not need to be fortified against every major creature.

Defense may instead rely heavily on:

- geography;
- natural barriers;
- visibility;
- warning systems;
- controlled approaches;
- escape routes;
- knowledge of animal behavior;
- timing;
- settlement placement.

Structures may exist to shape movement, protect vulnerable people, contain resources, or create warning zones rather than to physically stop the largest animals.

## 2.5 Settlement Knowledge as Cultural Wealth

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED PRINCIPLE**

A community's most valuable asset may not be its buildings or material possessions.

It may be:

> **The knowledge that lets its people understand and survive the place they inhabit.**

Experienced people may therefore function as repositories of environmental knowledge.

Their value is not simply social or symbolic; their knowledge can have direct survival consequences.


## 2.6 Human Progress & Sustainable Scale

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED PRINCIPLE**

> **Humanity's progress is constrained less by what it can understand than by what it can safely sustain.**

Human intelligence does not automatically produce large-scale technological development.

Technological and civilizational advances require the ability to sustain:

- labor;
- infrastructure;
- resource extraction;
- specialization;
- surplus;
- concentration of people;
- long-term safety.

The world's ecology can therefore limit the practical scale at which human knowledge can be applied.

Humans may understand a process without possessing the environmental stability required to exploit it extensively.

## 2.2 Civilization Scale

**PROVISIONAL / SEED**

The initial concept describes human civilization as small and scattered.

The exact population, settlement pattern, geographic distribution, and degree of civilization remain undefined.

## 2.3 Human Intelligence

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED PRINCIPLE**

Limited technology does not imply limited intelligence.

Human societies may possess sophisticated:

- ecological knowledge;
- material knowledge;
- social knowledge;
- medicine;
- ritual traditions;
- navigation;
- craftsmanship;
- oral history;
- environmental memory.

The specific expression of that knowledge remains open.

# 2.7 Planetary Climate Philosophy

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED**

The world's climate is shaped by several interacting natural cycles, including:

- axial cycles;
- orbital cycles;
- oceanic circulation;
- atmospheric circulation;
- tidal cycles;
- geological change.

These systems interact to produce long-term environmental and climatic variation.

The world can therefore feel more regionally and temporally variable than Earth while remaining naturally coherent.

Climate does not reset on a schedule. Change is continuous and gradual, with larger events occurring as part of natural planetary processes rather than as arbitrary planetary rearrangements.

> **The planet changes continuously, but it does not behave arbitrarily.**

The precise orbital mechanics, axial behavior, moon configuration, ocean currents, and climate regions remain to be developed.

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED**

Ecology is not background scenery.

Major ecological events may become major human events.

Potential consequences include changes to:

- food;
- migration;
- settlement;
- trade;
- conflict;
- religion;
- population;
- seasonal traditions.

## 3.2 Fauna

**OPEN**

Specific animals have not yet been locked.

The world may contain species from widely separated prehistoric periods, but the actual roster and ecological relationships remain to be developed.

## 3.3 Animal Agency

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED PRINCIPLE**

Animals should have lives and behaviors independent of human characters.

Predators are not automatically evil.

Dangerous animals may be:

- feared;
- respected;
- avoided;
- studied;
- hunted when necessary;
- protected;
- considered sacred;
- recognized as individuals.

Specific human-animal relationships remain open.

## 3.4 Ecological Consequence

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED PRINCIPLE**

Major creatures and ecological features should produce consequences beyond themselves.

A major species should affect its environment and, where appropriate, human life.

# 4. Hunting

## 4.1 Hunting Philosophy

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED**

Large-animal hunting is dangerous even for highly skilled humans.

A successful hunt is not merely an action scene.

It may affect:

- food supply;
- injuries;
- deaths;
- lost labor;
- injured specialists;
- childcare;
- healing;
- tool production;
- preservation;
- future hunting capacity;
- community demographics.

## 4.2 Community Scale

**PROVISIONAL / SEED**

That number is an example, not yet locked canon.

## 4.3 Hunt Outcome

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED PRINCIPLE**

> **A successful hunt can still be a costly victory.**

The world should preserve meaningful consequences even when humans act skillfully.

# 5. Injury & Adaptation

## 5.1 Injury Philosophy

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED**

People survive injuries.

They heal.

They adapt.

They may carry scars or permanent consequences.

A person injured by a major creature may be unable to perform a previous role for weeks, months, or permanently.

## 5.2 Role Adaptation

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED PRINCIPLE**

A changed body does not automatically make a person useless.

Communities may adapt roles around changed abilities.

Potential roles include:

- tracker;
- teacher;
- toolmaker;
- healer's assistant;
- storyteller;
- child mentor;
- settlement planner;
- spiritual specialist;
- keeper of ecological knowledge.

These are examples, not a locked occupational list.

# 6. Magic & Spiritual Practice

## 6.1 Magic Level

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED**

The world is **very low magic**.

Magic is real, but it does not dominate ordinary life or transform the setting into conventional high fantasy.

## 6.2 Magic Is Not a Game System

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED**

Magic has:

- no spell lists;
- no levels;
- no mana;
- no numerical effects;
- no quantified healing;
- no progression mechanics.

Magic should be described as a phenomenon and lived practice, not as mechanics.

## 6.3 Ritual Practice

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED**

Rituals genuinely have effects.

Possible components include:

- herbs;
- roots;
- fungi;
- pastes;
- smoke;
- animal materials;
- minerals;
- ash;
- oils;
- chants;
- songs;
- prayers;
- gestures;
- markings;
- offerings;
- fasting;
- dreams;
- trance;
- traditional preparation.

These are examples of possible practices, not a finalized magical catalog.

## 6.4 Restoration, Not Healing

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED**

> **Magic can restore, but it does not simply heal away consequence.**

Magic may:

- stabilize;
- support recovery;
- reduce suffering;
- restore strength;
- encourage the body's recovery;
- preserve life;
- improve the chance of recovery.

Magic does not guarantee that the desired outcome occurs.

A wound does not become irrelevant merely because magic was used.

Permanent consequences remain possible.

## 6.5 Outcome Rule

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED**

> **Magic can influence an outcome; it cannot dictate the outcome.**

This is a foundational limitation.

Magic is therefore meaningful without becoming an automatic solution to danger, injury, scarcity, or death.

## 6.6 Magic and the Natural World

**LOCKED / ESTABLISHED PRINCIPLE**

Magic should coexist with the physical world rather than replace it.

The exact relationship between magical effects and natural processes remains open.

## 6.7 Spirituality

**PROVISIONAL / SEED**

The original concept allows spirituality, spirits, dream-walking, communication or interpretation of spirits, spiritual relationships with animals, and supernatural knowledge.

None of those specific elements are currently locked.

# 15. Canon Management

## LOCKED / ESTABLISHED

### HARD CANON / LOCKED
Explicitly established truth.

### FLEXIBLE / PROVISIONAL
An active idea or seed that may change before being locked.

### OPEN
Not established and available for future development.

### UNKNOWN
Known to exist or matter, but deliberately unexplained.

### IN-WORLD BELIEF
What inhabitants believe; it may or may not be objectively true.

### SEED MATERIAL
Material inherited from an early concept that has not yet been explicitly locked during active worldbuilding.

> **Later explicitly locked decisions supersede earlier conflicting seed material.**

# 16. Story-Building Philosophy

The World Bible exists to provide enough bones to tell stories.

It should not attempt to answer every possible question.

A strong addition should create consequences, relationships, tensions, opportunities, or story possibilities.

The goal is:

> **Story density, not information density.**

A developed region may contain:

- geography;
- ecology;
- peoples;
- resources;
- settlements;
- history;
- conflicts;
- beliefs;
- story opportunities.

A distant region may intentionally remain only a name, rumor, ecological description, or blank space.

Uneven depth is intentional when it serves the world's creative use.

# 18. Development Rule

Worldbuilding is not required to proceed linearly.

A fauna idea may require a landscape.

A landscape may require a climate.

A climate may require a migration pattern.

A migration pattern may create a human culture.

A culture may require a historical event.

That is normal.

> **We maintain an order of importance, not an order of creation.**

Creative development may move freely.

Canon management must remain disciplined.
