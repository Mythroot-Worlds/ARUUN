Explicitly established truth.

# CURRENT CANON SNAPSHOT

## LOCKED / ESTABLISHED

- The world is **very low magic**.
- Magic is real and can have genuine effects.
- Magic does **not** operate as a game system.
- There are no spells, levels, mana pools, numerical effects, quantified healing, or other game-mechanical magic structures.
- Rituals, chants, songs, prayers, gestures, preparations, herbs, pastes, smoke, and similar practices may be genuine vehicles or components of magic.
- Magic can restore, but it does not simply erase physical consequence.
- Magic can help, stabilize, encourage, or improve the chances of recovery, but it cannot guarantee recovery.
- Magic can influence an outcome; it cannot dictate the outcome.
- Magic does not radically transform the world into a high-magic setting.
- Humanity is **not the apex species**.
- Human survival depends heavily on knowledge, cooperation, adaptation, preparation, and accumulated experience.
- Large-animal hunting is dangerous even for highly skilled people.
- A successful hunt can still create injuries, deaths, lost labor, medical burdens, and demographic/social consequences.
- Injury matters. People survive, adapt, and may carry permanent consequences.
- A person who can no longer perform a previous role is not automatically useless; communities adapt roles around changed abilities.
- Ecology is a major source of culture, conflict, and story.
- Animals should not exist merely as decorative monsters or spectacle.
- The world is intended to support stories rather than function as an exhaustive encyclopedia.
- The world's biological identity should feel **prehistoric before it feels alien**.
- Science is the primary grounding framework for this world's ecology, climate, geology, and evolution, while creative decisions are still allowed where explicitly chosen.
- The world is intended to feel biologically coherent rather than like a catalogue of random monsters.

## PROVISIONAL / SEED MATERIAL

- Widely separated prehistoric periods/species may coexist.
- Human civilization is small and scattered.
- Technology is materially constrained but human knowledge may be sophisticated.
- Spirituality and subtle/consequential magic are part of the setting.
- Ecology functions as a civilization-level narrative engine.
- Humans have meaningful, complex relationships with predators.
- The exact mechanism producing the world's anachronistic ecology remains undefined.

## OPEN / UNDEFINED

- World name.
- Exact human species.
- Exact origin/mechanism of anachronistic coexistence.
- Exact human population.
- Detailed human cultures.
- Political structures.
- Languages.
- Formal history.
- Cosmology.
- Exact religion/belief systems.
- Exact technology ceiling.
- Final planetary astronomical parameters not explicitly locked below.

# 1. WORLD IDENTITY

## 1.1 Working Classification

**Prehistoric / Anachronistic Survival Fantasy**

The setting is not a strict reconstruction of one Earth period.

Its defining identity is:

> **Human civilization is an ecological participant rather than an ecological master.**

The world should make environmental knowledge, cooperation, geography, resources, injury, animal behavior, and accumulated cultural memory materially important.

## 1.2 Core Creative Promise

The world should produce:
- awe;
- danger;
- vulnerability;
- discovery;
- resilience;
- interdependence;
- and the feeling of living inside an ecosystem vastly older and more powerful than civilization.

The exact emotional emphasis remains open.

## 1.3 Core Narrative Question

> **How do you build a life in a world that was never built for you?**

# 2. PLANETARY / ASTRONOMICAL FOUNDATION

## 2.1 Moons

**SELECTED CONFIGURATION / WORKING CANON**

The planet has **two moons**.

### Great Moon
- Physically larger.
- Farther from the planet.
- Intended to appear broadly comparable in apparent size to the nearer moon.

### Near / Medium Moon
- Physically smaller.
- Closer to the planet.
- Intended to appear broadly comparable in apparent size to the Great Moon.

Their different masses and orbital distances produce distinct tidal cycles.

### Tectonic interaction

The moons do **not** simply push continents around.

Planetary interior dynamics and plate tectonics remain the fundamental drivers of continental movement.

Lunar tidal stresses may interact with:
- already-stressed faults;
- volcanic systems;
- plate boundaries;
- other geological thresholds.

This may influence the timing or frequency of some geological events without replacing plate tectonics.

## 2.2 Day / Night

A working planetary rotation period of approximately **32 Earth hours** has been used during development, but the exact value remains **PROVISIONAL** unless separately locked.

Night is not assumed to be a fixed equal division of light and darkness.

Actual illumination varies with:
- solar geometry;
- atmospheric conditions;
- terrain;
- moon position;
- moon phase;
- season.

A night may therefore be:
- bright;
- dim;
- or genuinely dark.

Moonlight can visibly illuminate the landscape during otherwise astronomical night.

## 2.3 Star / Orbit

A warm/amber **K-type star** remains the leading candidate, but is not independently locked in the current canon.

~26° axial tilt and moderate stable orbital eccentricity remain candidate parameters rather than hard canon.

## 2.4 Gravity

Approximately **1.25g** has been used as a working planetary assumption in the evolution model.

Treat this as a **working planetary parameter** until explicitly locked.

Its major design implication is that extreme unsupported body height is mechanically more costly, while large organisms remain possible when ecological benefits compensate.

# 3. TECTONIC / GEOGRAPHIC FOUNDATION

The tectonic model is a **research-informed working model**, not a fully locked scientific canon.

Its core architecture is retained because it successfully explains the required continental forms.

## 3.1 Required Continental Systems

The world contains:

1. **Hearth** — mostly intact great continent.
2. **Shattered** — great continental system broken into several enormous fragments.
3. **Rift** — great continental system splitting into two major halves that remain relatively close.
4. **Lost** — enormous continuous continent separated from the others by an immense ocean.

The three primary continental systems are grouped within one broad regional cluster.

Lost is extremely isolated.

## 3.2 Working Tectonic History

The working model is:

**Ancient continental assembly**
→ **supercontinental interval**
→ **Lost separation**
→ **main-cluster breakup**
→ **Shattered fragmentation**
→ **Rift partial split**
→ **long tectonic reorganization**
→ **present configuration**

The mechanisms used are real geological processes:
- divergent boundaries;
- subduction;
- transform motion;
- continental rifting;
- oblique convergence;
- accretion;
- erosion and uplift.

## 3.3 Lost Isolation

Lost has a working isolation timescale of roughly **700 million–1 billion years**.

This is a working evolutionary-history parameter, not a statement that every detail of its geological chronology has been finalized.

The purpose is to create enough time for:
- deep evolutionary divergence;
- adaptive radiation;
- ancient lineage persistence;
- convergent solutions;
- radically different ecological relationships.

## 3.4 Continental Identities

### HEARTH — Connected Abundance

Primary pressures:
- connected ecosystems;
- large continuous ranges;
- broad geographic distributions;
- long migrations;
- large populations;
- strong competition;
- relatively strong gene flow.

Expected geographic/ecological character:
- ancient stable interior;
- old mountain systems;
- broad interior basins and plains;
- major rivers;
- wetlands;
- extensive grasslands/woodlands;
- smaller peripheral islands.

### SHATTERED — Fragmentation and Specialization

Primary pressures:
- geographic isolation;
- regional divergence;
- founder effects/bottlenecks;
- altered body size;
- resource specialization;
- coastal/marine dependence;
- migration between fragments where possible.

Expected geographic/ecological character:
- several enormous continental fragments;
- deep marine basins;
- major straits;
- rotated/translated fragments;
- offset mountain systems;
- strong ocean influence;
- regional wet/dry contrasts.

Shattered is **not** a field of tiny islands.

A major signature concept is an amphibious migratory grazer capable of crossing dozens of miles of open water between fragments when seasonal food collapse makes migration worthwhile.

### RIFT — Transition and Instability

Primary pressures:
- highland/lowland specialization;
- freshwater dependence;
- wetland specialization;
- volcanic/disturbance adaptation;
- divergence across geographic barriers;
- organisms crossing sharply contrasting environments.

Expected geographic/ecological character:
- long active rift system;
- fault valleys;
- escarpments;
- rift lakes;
- inland seas;
- wetlands;
- volcanic fields;
- geothermal areas;
- dry basins;
- strong elevation gradients.

The two major halves remain close because the rift has not matured into a full ocean.

### LOST — Deep-Time Independence

Primary pressures:
- adaptive radiation;
- divergent body plans;
- ancient lineage persistence;
- convergent solutions;
- unrelated lineages filling familiar functions;
- highly specialized regional ecosystems.

Lost should be biologically unfamiliar because of **independent evolutionary history**, not because its physics are inherently alien.

# 4. CLIMATE FOUNDATION

Climate is intended to follow causal chains:

> **planetary conditions → geography → ocean circulation → climate → ecology → evolution → culture**

Major climate/environment categories currently used:
- warm wet;
- seasonal wet;
- dry forest;
- grassland;
- semi-arid;
- arid;
- highland;
- cool forest;
- wetland;
- riverine;
- lake;
- inland sea;
- coastal;
- marine;
- volcanic/disturbed;
- transitional.

Climate is interpreted with geography.

A wetland means saturated ground, standing/slow water, flooding cycles, characteristic vegetation, and associated nutrient processes—not simply "warm and wet."

## Four broad climate identities

**Hearth:** connected seasonal ecosystems, broad migrations, large predator/prey networks.

**Shattered:** isolation, local specialization, coastal influence, seasonal fragment-to-fragment pressures.

**Rift:** wet/dry transitions, elevation, freshwater, volcanic succession, abrupt environmental gradients.

**Lost:** climate zones that make physical sense, with evolutionary novelty arising from long isolation.

# 5. VEGETATION / FLORA FOUNDATION

## 5.1 Visual Identity

The world should feel prehistoric before it feels alien.

Vegetation generally favors:
- robustness;
- breadth;
- density;
- low-to-moderate growth;
- thick ground vegetation;
- substantial grasses/grass-like plants;
- shrubs;
- short/broad/rounded trees where forests occur.

"Lush" means biologically robust and dense, not necessarily tall.

Vegetation color varies regionally. Unusual colors may occur, but no single color is a planetary default.

## 5.2 Flora System

The flora system is organized by **function**, not by a demand to catalogue every species.

Core functions include:
- primary production;
- grazing resources;
- browse resources;
- fruit/seed resources;
- root/tuber resources;
- ground cover;
- wetland/aquatic engineering;
- nutrient cycling and soil building;
- pioneer/disturbance species;
- fire adaptation;
- water conservation;
- canopy/shade engineering;
- pollination support;
- seed dispersal;
- defensive/toxic plants;
- coastal/salt tolerance;
- desert specialization;
- volcanic/mineral specialization;
- ecological connectors.

Plant design follows:

**CONTINENT → CLIMATE → FUNCTION → EVOLUTIONARY POSSIBILITIES → PLANT**

## 5.3 Flora Library

**WORKING CANON**

30 canonical flora entries currently exist, including 13 signature entries.

The flora library is intentionally not exhaustive.

Its purpose is to establish:
- resource foundations;
- habitat structure;
- seasonal food;
- water/soil interactions;
- regional visual identity;
- relationships with canonical fauna.

## 5.4 Flora Ecology Principle

Plants should not exist as isolated decorative objects.

Ask:
- what consumes them?
- what pollinates them?
- what disperses them?
- what competes with them?
- what do they alter?
- what happens if they disappear?

Succession is also important:

**bare substrate → pioneer community → grasses/herbaceous growth → shrub/thicket → woodland/forest or another stable community**

The exact sequence varies by climate.

# 6. FAUNA FOUNDATION

## 6.1 Function-First Rule

Every creature begins with:

> **What ecological job does it perform?**

Examples:
- grazer;
- browser;
- mixed feeder;
- root/fruit consumer;
- prey species;
- omnivore;
- burrower;
- small predator;
- pursuit predator;
- ambush predator;
- pack/social predator;
- scavenger;
- ecosystem engineer;
- dangerous specialist;
- migratory connector;
- filter feeder;
- aquatic grazer;
- aquatic predator;
- marine predator;
- aerial consumer;
- aerial predator;
- scavenger;
- seed/fruit disperser.

## 6.2 Predictive Evolution Matrix

The matrix takes:

**CONTINENT → CLIMATE → FUNCTION → EVOLUTIONARY POSSIBILITIES**

It identifies favored, plausible, difficult, and poor-fit solutions.

It does not dictate the final organism.

Creative selection creates the organism.

## 6.3 Prehistoric Identity

The default biological vocabulary should strongly lean prehistoric-esque:
- big lizards;
- big snakes;
- heavy herbivores;
- armored animals;
- tusked/horned beasts;
- burrowers;
- amphibious predators;
- egg-layers;
- primitive-feeling aerial organisms;
- large arthropods where justified.

However, not everything should be reptilian or gigantic.

Fur, feathers, filaments, mammal-like forms, and familiar body plans are valid when evolutionary logic supports them.

### "T-Rex but not T-Rex" principle

A familiar silhouette is acceptable when convergence makes sense.

The question is not:
> "Has Earth already made something like this?"

The question is:
> "Did this world's ecological pressures plausibly produce something like this?"

## 6.4 Size Philosophy

The desired dangerous-animal benchmark is approximately:

> **Too dangerous for one person; possible for a coordinated hunting group; potentially deadly even to that group if they make mistakes.**

Large animals are encouraged.

Routine kaiju are not.

# 11. HUMANITY

## 11.1 Human Position

**LOCKED**

Humans are not the apex species.

Human advantages are:
- knowledge;
- cooperation;
- adaptation;
- preparation;
- accumulated experience;
- understanding of the environment.

A single person is vulnerable.

A community is considerably more capable.

## 11.2 Settlement Philosophy

**LOCKED**

Human settlements are not assumed to be absolutely safe.

People deliberately choose locations that are dramatically safer than alternatives based on:
- animal movement;
- migration patterns;
- predator territories;
- terrain;
- visibility;
- water;
- seasonal changes;
- natural barriers;
- escape routes;
- resource access.

> **Humans do not make the world safe. They learn where it is safer to live.**

## 11.3 Environmental Knowledge as Infrastructure

A community's knowledge of its place is itself infrastructure.

It determines:
- where people settle;
- when they move;
- when they hunt;
- when they gather;
- when they avoid an area;
- how they prepare for seasonal changes;
- how they respond to animal movement.

Knowledge can be transmitted through:
- observation;
- teaching;
- apprenticeship;
- oral tradition;
- ritual;
- stories;
- repeated practice.

Loss of environmental knowledge can therefore threaten a community even when its physical structures remain intact.

## 11.4 Human Civilization

Detailed human civilization remains **OPEN**.

The existing profile establishes that low technology does not mean low intelligence.

Human societies may possess sophisticated:
- ecological knowledge;
- material knowledge;
- medicine;
- navigation;
- craftsmanship;
- oral history;
- environmental memory;
- social systems.

Specific cultures, settlements, political systems, languages, and religions remain to be developed.

# 12. HUNTING / INJURY PHILOSOPHY

Large-animal hunting is a major social and economic event.

A successful hunt can still cause:
- injury;
- death;
- lost labor;
- medical burdens;
- reduced future hunting capacity;
- childcare/work redistribution;
- tool-production consequences;
- food-preservation pressures.

A person who becomes unable to perform a former role is not automatically useless.

Communities adapt roles around changed abilities.

Survival therefore includes **adaptation to consequences**, not merely avoiding death.

# 15. WORLD-BUILDING PHILOSOPHY

## Story density, not information density.

A developed region may contain:
- geography;
- ecology;
- peoples;
- resources;
- settlements;
- history;
- conflicts;
- beliefs;
- story opportunities.

A distant region may remain:
- a name;
- rumor;
- ecological description;
- or blank space.

Uneven depth is intentional.

## Broad before specific

The project intentionally works by:
1. broad category coverage;
2. establishing the system;
3. identifying relationships;
4. then returning for specificity.

This prevents overbuilding one region while neglecting the rest.

## Science as a tool

Science is used heavily where it improves coherence.

It is not a prison.

When a deliberate creative decision overrides strict realism, the decision should be identified rather than disguised as scientific fact.

# 17. NEXT DEVELOPMENT PHASE — HUMANITY AND CIVILIZATION

With the broad physical world, climate framework, flora, fauna, and ecological relationships sufficiently established for a first pass, the next major system is **humanity**.

However, the next step should NOT be "invent a bunch of tribes."

The human system should be built from the environment.

Recommended order:

## A. Human ecological baseline
- What can humans eat?
- What can they cultivate?
- What do they hunt?
- What animals are too dangerous to hunt?
- What materials are available?
- What injuries are common?
- What knowledge is essential?

## B. Human survival model
- settlement;
- mobility;
- seasonal movement;
- food storage;
- water access;
- shelter;
- fire;
- tools;
- clothing;
- medicine;
- child care;
- elder knowledge.

Define what people can reliably produce from:
- stone;
- wood;
- bone;
- fiber;

## F. History
History should be added where it creates present-day consequences.

Do not invent thousands of years of chronology just to fill pages.

# 20. REFERENCE MATRICES AND HOW TO USE THEM

The following documents remain **standalone working tools**. They are not replacements for the Master World Bible, and they do not need to be reproduced in full here.

Their purpose is to provide reusable frameworks for designing, expanding, or testing parts of the world while keeping new material consistent with established canon.

## Predictive Evolution Matrix

The **Predictive Evolution Matrix** is used to determine what kinds of biological solutions are plausible under a given combination of:

**Continent → Climate → Function**

It is primarily used for creature design.

### How to use it
1. Identify where the organism lives.
2. Identify the climate/environment.
3. Identify the ecological function the organism needs to perform.
4. Use the matrix to generate favored, plausible, difficult, and poor-fit evolutionary possibilities.
5. Select and refine a creature from those possibilities.
6. Check the result against the World Bible and existing flora/fauna so it does not contradict established ecology.

The matrix is a **design constraint and possibility generator**, not a species catalogue and not a command to choose the most scientifically extreme option.

## Creature Necessity Sheet

The **Creature Necessity Sheet** determines which ecological functions actually need canonical representation.

Its purpose is to prevent species bloat.

Use it to ask:
- What ecological function is missing?
- Does the world actually need another canonical species?
- Is an existing creature already covering this role?
- Would another creature add a genuinely different evolutionary solution or regional identity?

The goal is enough canonical fauna to make the world believable, not a complete planetary species list.

## Flora Function Matrix

The **Flora Function Matrix** identifies what plants need to **do** within an ecosystem.

Examples include:
- primary production;
- grazing resources;
- browse;
- fruit/seed production;
- root/tuber resources;
- ground cover;
- wetland engineering;
- soil building;
- disturbance succession;
- water conservation;
- canopy formation;
- coastal stabilization.

It is used before designing individual plants so that flora are built around ecological function rather than appearance alone.

## Flora Creation Matrix

The **Flora Creation Matrix** turns:

**Continent → Climate/Environment → Function**

into a set of plausible plant adaptations.

It considers factors such as:
- growth form;
- roots;
- water strategy;
- light strategy;
- reproduction;
- dispersal;
- defense;
- life history;
- ecological relationships;
- regional specialization.

As with the creature matrix, it defines the plausible design space rather than dictating a single answer.

## General Matrix Rule

All matrices are **supporting design systems**.

They should be used to:
- maintain consistency;
- expose useful possibilities;
- identify ecological gaps;
- prevent arbitrary invention;
- accelerate creation;
- and preserve the world's established scientific and ecological logic.

They should **not** override explicit World Bible canon.

When a deliberate creative decision conflicts with a matrix recommendation, the World Bible decision wins. The matrix can then be updated later if the project decides the new rule should become part of the reusable framework.

The matrices are therefore best understood as:

> **Tools for creating new canon — not canon themselves.**

The Master World Bible remains the central source for what is currently true about the world.
