## Prehistoric / Anachronistic World — v0.9

**Purpose:** Living world reference and canon ledger.

**Canon rule:** Explicit decisions become locked canon. Working models, research conclusions, and suggestions do not become canon merely because they appear in this document.

# 1. WORLD DESIGN PROFILE

## Core identity

A naturalistic, prehistoric-esque world that is fundamentally its own world rather than Earth transplanted into another timeline.

It is Earth-adjacent in recognizable biology and physical logic, but evolution, geography, climate, and history followed a different path.

The desired experience is:
- an ancient, old-world environment;
- primitive/early civilization;
- humanity living inside a powerful ecosystem;
- recognizable natural processes producing unfamiliar results;
- low-grade magic that does not turn the setting into conventional high fantasy.

## Design philosophy

For this world, science is a **design constraint and source of causality**, not an absolute creative master.

Where science can explain something naturally, prefer that explanation.

Where multiple scientifically plausible outcomes exist, choose the one that best serves the world.

Where a deliberate creative choice is more important, the choice may override strict realism.

The important distinction is:
- **Established premise** — what we decide the world has.
- **Derived consequence** — what follows from those premises.
- **Plausible option** — one scientifically defensible possibility.
- **Creative decision** — deliberately chosen for the setting.
- **Open/unknown** — not yet decided.

# 2. MAGIC & SPIRITUAL ECOLOGY

## Locked / established

- Magic is real but very low-grade.
- It is not a game system.
- There are no mana pools, levels, spell lists, numerical effects, or quantified healing.
- Rituals, chants, gestures, preparations, herbs, pastes, smoke, blood, and similar practices can have genuine effects.
- Magic can **restore**, but does not simply erase physical damage.
- Magic can help, stabilize, encourage, or improve the chance of recovery.
- Magic cannot guarantee an outcome.
- Magic can influence an outcome; it cannot dictate it.
- Material practices may have ordinary biological/environmental explanations, while a small spiritual presence also interacts with the natural world.
- The exact metaphysical mechanism remains open.

The setting's low-grade magic should remain compatible with a world whose dominant explanation for unusual phenomena is still natural/material.

# 3. PLANETARY FOUNDATION

Current working planetary parameters:

- ~1.25g gravity.
- Atmosphere broadly habitable to the world's human lineage.
- 32-hour day.
- ~26° axial tilt.
- ~214 Earth-day orbital period.
- ~0.08 orbital eccentricity.
- Warm K-type star.
- ~0.65 AU orbital distance.
- Roughly 70/30 land-water target.
- Two moons: one larger/farther, one smaller/closer, visually similar in apparent size.
- Two moons contribute secondary tidal/geological effects but do **not** directly push continents; internal planetary heat and mantle dynamics remain the fundamental tectonic driver.
- Sky may have a subtle greenish cast; exact atmospheric chemistry remains open.
- Vegetation and flames may differ in appearance from Earth where atmospheric chemistry and biology plausibly allow it.

# 4. HUMANITY

## Locked / established

- Humans are native to this world.
- They are human-like in broad body plan and social behavior but followed a distinct evolutionary path.
- They may be somewhat shorter/stockier than modern humans.
- Slightly greater bone density and physical resilience are plausible consequences of ~1.25g gravity.
- Human evolution should produce interesting but restrained differences, not arbitrary alien anatomy.
- Humanity is not the apex predator.
- Survival depends heavily on knowledge, cooperation, adaptation, and accumulated experience.
- Civilization is constrained less by what humans can understand than by what they can safely sustain.
- Human settlements are preferentially established where environments are **dramatically safer than surrounding alternatives**, not because any location is truly safe.
- Humans learn migration patterns, terrain, animal behavior, weather, resources, and other environmental information because knowledge equals survival.
- Large-animal hunting is dangerous even for skilled people.
- A successful hunt can still cause injury, death, lost labor, medical burden, and social consequences.

# 5. TECTONIC WORLD MODEL

**Status:** Research-informed working model. The architecture is established as the current worldbuilding foundation; exact vectors and cartographic boundaries remain refinable.

## Four major continental systems

1. **Hearth** — mostly intact great continent.
2. **Shattered** — enormous continental fragments separated by major seas.
3. **Rift** — one great continental system actively splitting into two major halves.
4. **Lost** — enormous continuous continent isolated by an immense ocean.

The three main continental systems are relatively close together. Lost is extremely isolated.

## Working seven-plate architecture

### Hearth Plate
Carries the mostly intact continent. Old, thick continental interior with active margins and small peripheral island systems.

### Shatter-West Plate
Carries major western/central fragments of Shattered. Long-lived rifting followed by transform/oblique movement produces large fragments and rotated geology.

### Shatter-East Plate
Carries eastern fragments. Its long transform/oblique boundary allows translation and rotation relative to Shatter-West.

### Rift Crown Plate
Carries one major half of Rift.

### Rift South Plate
Carries the other major half. The two Rift plates are diverging, but the rift has not matured into a full ocean.

### Oceanic Ring Plate
Major oceanic plate containing spreading ridges, subduction margins, and transform systems that organize the main continental cluster.

### Lost Plate
Carries Lost. It has remained geographically isolated for roughly 700 million–1 billion years as a working geological history while continuing its own tectonic evolution.

## Geological history

**Ancient continental assembly**
→ volcanic arcs, accretion, collisions, stabilization

**Supercontinental interval**
→ broad ancestral continental complex

**Lost separation**
→ major rift succeeds, new oceanic crust forms, Lost begins long isolation

**Main-cluster breakup**
→ Hearth, Shattered, and Rift systems emerge

**Shattering**
→ overlapping rifts + transform/oblique motion + rotation + accretion create enormous fragments

**Rift formation**
→ continental crust thins, faults and basins form, volcanism increases, but full ocean formation has not yet occurred

**Long reorganization**
→ spreading, subduction, transform motion, accretion, uplift, erosion

**Present**
→ four continental systems remain active geological participants.

# 6. GLOBAL CLIMATE MODEL

Climate is derived from planetary parameters plus geography rather than selected arbitrarily.

Causal chain:

**planetary parameters**
→ atmospheric circulation
→ ocean circulation
→ tectonic geography
→ elevation/mountains
→ prevailing winds
→ precipitation
→ temperature
→ climate
→ water systems
→ ecosystems

Broad expectations:
- meaningful seasons from ~26° axial tilt;
- shorter ecological year because of ~214-day orbit;
- ocean-driven heat redistribution;
- strong rain-shadow effects;
- stronger continentality in large interiors;
- a lush world without every region being wet;
- dense low vegetation, robust grasslands, wetlands, seasonal woodland, scrub, dry basins, and localized deserts.

**Connected abundance.**

## Mapmaker brief
Show one enormous connected landmass, substantial interior, active-margin mountain systems, broad rivers flowing from mountains, large basins, wet/dry mountain contrasts, grassland/woodland interiors, coastal wetlands/deltas, and small peripheral islands.

# 8. SHATTERED CONTINENT

## Geographic identity

A former continental system divided into several enormous continental fragments.

These are continental-scale islands, not a field of small islands.

Major features:
- one especially large western/central fragment;
- several enormous eastern/northern fragments;
- smaller peripheral islands;
- independent mountain systems;
- deep marine basins;
- major straits;
- isolated interior basins.

## Climate

Ocean influence is stronger than on Hearth.

### Largest fragment
Wet coasts, seasonal woodland, grasslands, dry forests, interior basins, localized semi-arid regions.

### Wetter fragments
Dense low forests, wetlands, productive rivers, foggy/stormy coasts.

### Drier eastern fragments
Dry woodland, grassland, scrub, seasonal rivers, dry basins, localized arid environments.

## Ecological identity
**Fragmentation and specialization.**

Shared ancestral life can develop regional variants because populations are separated by major water barriers.

## Established ecological seed
A future amphibious/marine-capable migratory species can migrate between major fragments.

Working behavior:
- wet season creates abundant food on a relatively dry eastern fragment;
- hot/dry season causes food and water decline;
- animals migrate westward toward a wetter fragment;
- migration may involve ~40 miles of open water;
- migration carries genuine mortality risk.

Appearance, name, anatomy, and other species details remain open.

## Mapmaker brief
Show several enormous continental fragments, one especially large western/central fragment, deep seas and straits, independent mountains, wet coastal zones, drier interiors, smaller islands, and geological relationships suggesting former connection without making the fragments clean puzzle pieces.

# 9. RIFT CONTINENT

## Geographic identity

One enormous continental system actively separating into two major halves.

The rift is irregular and immature rather than a clean oceanic split.

Major features:
- two enormous halves;
- long rift system;
- fault valleys;
- steep escarpments;
- rift lakes;
- inland seas;
- wetlands;
- volcanic fields/cones;
- highlands;
- dry basins;
- branching/variable rift geometry.

## Climate

### Highlands
Moisture capture produces:
- wet forests;
- cool uplands;
- dense vegetation;
- river headwaters.

### Rift floor
Lower and often warmer, with sections that are substantially drier:
- dry grasslands;
- seasonal woodland;
- warm lowlands;
- seasonal waterways;
- localized saline basins.

### Lakes/wetlands
- permanent freshwater systems;
- seasonal lakes;
- marshes;
- floodplains;
- dense riparian vegetation;
- aquatic plant communities.

### Volcanic regions
Fresh barren substrate → pioneer vegetation → developing soils → recovering woodland/grassland → mature ecosystems.

### Branches
Different branches can have dramatically different moisture regimes.

## Ecological identity
**Transition and instability.**

It has some of the strongest environmental gradients per unit distance.

## Mapmaker brief
Show two enormous halves, irregular central rift, large lakes/inland seas, wetlands, steep escarpments, highlands, volcanic zones, dry rift-floor basins, wet uplands, and sections where the rift is narrow versus wide.

# 9. OCEAN CIRCULATION & MARINE CLIMATE

The ocean system follows the same causal philosophy as the terrestrial climate model:

> **latitude → winds → currents → heat/moisture → upwelling → marine productivity → coastal climate → terrestrial ecology**

Surface currents are driven primarily by global wind systems, modified by planetary rotation (Coriolis) and continental geometry. Northern subtropical circulation is broadly clockwise; southern subtropical circulation is broadly counterclockwise.

## Working current structure

- A northern subtropical gyre influences Hearth and Shattered.
- Equatorial westward flow strongly influences Shattered and Rift.
- A southern subtropical gyre influences Lost.
- Warm western-boundary currents transport heat poleward.
- Cooler eastern-boundary currents return water equatorward and create candidate upwelling environments.

## Major upwelling zones

Two major working upwelling belts are established:

**Shattered:** a major cool-current-facing coastal zone with strong seasonal marine productivity.

**Lost:** a major cool-current-facing coastal zone with strong nutrient delivery and marine biomass.

Rift has localized rather than continent-scale upwelling associated with irregular coastlines, enclosed seas, seasonal winds, volcanic margins, and rift lakes.

Upwelling brings cold, nutrient-rich deep water toward the surface, supporting phytoplankton and therefore larger marine food webs.

## Maritime influence

"Maritime influence" means the ocean modifies nearby land temperature and moisture. Water heats and cools more slowly than land, so nearby coastal areas experience moderated temperatures and altered humidity/precipitation compared with continental interiors.

Warm currents tend to make nearby coasts warmer and potentially wetter; cool currents can create cooler maritime climates, fog, and—where winds drive offshore transport—upwelling.

This means identical latitudes can produce different climates depending on current direction, coastline geometry, wind regime, and mountain barriers.

## Continental consequences

**Hearth:** strong coast/interior contrasts and increasing continentality inland.

**Shattered:** strong ocean influence, current-driven regional contrasts, productive coastal waters, and seasonal marine-resource pulses.

**Rift:** equatorial circulation, localized marine productivity, major lake systems, and strong geography-driven climate gradients.

**Lost:** enormous coastal systems, warm/cool current contrasts, productive upwelling coasts, and strong marine-terrestrial nutrient exchange.

The exact current speeds, temperatures, salinities, and depths remain open. The current map is a physically grounded climate/ecology scaffold rather than a numerical ocean model.

For detailed current directions, upwelling locations, marine productivity, and coastal effects, use the standalone **Ocean & Marine Climate Reference**.

- scavenger;
- volcanic opportunist;
- highland climber/browser;
- wetland ambush predator;
- major rift-floor grazer;
- lake ambush predator;
- aerial predator;
- warm riverine ambush predator.

Riftlurker populations can vary by isolated lake, creating regional variants without requiring each lake population to become a separately documented species.

### Lost
Lost's fauna emphasize deep-time evolutionary independence, convergent ecological solutions, ancient lineage persistence, and unusual but functional body plans.

Representative canonical roles include:
- arid ambush predator with four grappling limbs;
- plateau herbivore;
- forest pack predator;
- extreme armored browser;
- wetland ecosystem engineer;
- massive filter feeder;
- glide-only aerial consumer;
- deep-water predator.

Lost should feel evolutionarily different, not physically alien.

## 21.3 Background Faunal Diversity

The canonical 34 are not the planet's total species count.

Background layers explicitly include:
- small burrowing rodent-analogues on all four continents;
- insect/arthropod analogues;
- juvenile-stage niche divergence in some canonical organisms;
- regional Riftlurker variants;
- small shorebird/wader analogues;
- fish analogues.

These background layers are intentionally implied rather than fully catalogued.

## 21.4 Fauna Design Principles Already Represented

- Function comes before body plan.
- Familiar silhouettes are acceptable through convergent evolution.
- Large animals should be ecologically consequential.
- Dangerous does not mean malicious.
- Organisms have tradeoffs.
- Shared lineages can diverge regionally.
- Lost can produce independent solutions to familiar ecological functions.
- Cross-environment organisms are valuable because they connect systems.
- The 30–40 target is a documentation target, not a species-count claim.
