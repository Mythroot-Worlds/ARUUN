# Mythroot Prehistoric World — Tectonic Model v0.1

## Status
Research-informed working model. Nothing here is canon until explicitly locked.

## Design target
The present world contains:
1. One mostly intact great continent with many small peripheral islands.
2. One great continental system broken into several enormous fragments.
3. One great continental system split into two major halves that remain relatively close.
4. One enormous, continuous Lost Continent separated by an immense ocean.
5. Three main continental systems relatively close together; the Lost Continent is extremely isolated.
6. Working Lost Continent isolation: roughly 700 million–1 billion years.

## Working seven-plate architecture

### 1. Hearth Plate
Carries the mostly intact continent. Old, thick continental interior; active margins around portions of its perimeter. Peripheral islands arise from volcanic arcs, small accreted fragments, and rift remnants.

### 2. Shatter Plate
Carries a major portion of the fragmented continent. Long-lived rifting and later deformation produce large continental fragments.

### 3. Shatter-East Plate
Carries additional fragments of the shattered system. A long transform/oblique boundary allows rotation and translation of fragments.

### 4. Rift Crown Plate
Carries one major half of the partially split continent. Its interior contains an active continental rift.

### 5. Rift South Plate
Carries the opposing half. It is diverging from Rift Crown, but the rift has not yet matured into a full ocean.

### 6. Oceanic Ring Plate
A major oceanic plate organizing much of the main cluster. It contains spreading ridges and subduction margins.

### 7. Lost Plate
Carries the Lost Continent. It has been geographically isolated from the main cluster for roughly 700 million–1 billion years, while continuing its own internal geological evolution.

**Important:** Continents do not map one-to-one onto plates. The shattered continent, especially, can occupy multiple plates.

## How the world got here

### Phase A — Ancient continental assembly
Continental crust accumulated through volcanic arcs, accretion, collisions, and stabilization. The result was a broad ancestral continental complex rather than a perfectly uniform continent.

### Phase B — Supercontinental interval
Major continental blocks assembled into a broad supercontinental system. This is our fictional analogue to a supercontinent cycle.

### Phase C — Lost Continent separation
A major rift developed along one side of the ancestral complex. New oceanic crust formed as the continental block separated. It gradually became the Lost Continent and moved away over immense time.

### Phase D — Main-cluster breakup
The remaining continental complex reorganized into three dominant systems:
- mostly intact Hearth continent;
- fragmented Shatter continent;
- partially rifted Crown/South continent.

### Phase E — Shattering
The Shatter region experienced several overlapping rifts plus later oblique/transform motion. Some rifts became ocean basins; others failed. Fragments rotated, translated, and in places accreted to neighboring margins. The result is several enormous continental islands rather than a field of tiny islands.

### Phase F — Partial split
The Crown/South system developed a major continental rift. Crust thinned, faults formed, volcanism increased, and basins developed. The rift did not yet become a mature ocean, so the two major halves remain close.

### Phase G — Long reorganization
Over hundreds of millions of years, spreading ridges created oceanic crust, subduction consumed older oceanic crust, volcanic arcs and terranes accreted to continents, transform faults offset systems, mountains rose at collisions, and erosion reshaped the resulting relief.

### Phase H — Present
The world now has the four required continental systems, while tectonic motion continues.

## Major boundaries to develop

### Hearth–Oceanic Ring
Likely subduction system.
Expected: trenches, volcanic arcs, major mountains, earthquakes, accretionary margins.

### Shatter–Shatter-East
Likely long transform/oblique boundary.
Expected: major faults, offset ranges, displaced coastlines, rotations, localized earthquakes.

### Rift Crown–Rift South
Divergent continental boundary.
Expected: rift valleys, volcanic provinces, large lakes, inland seas, geothermal zones, progressive crustal thinning.

### Lost Plate margins
The Lost Continent should not be geologically frozen. Give it at least one significant active margin and several older/passive margins, allowing its own mountain and volcanic systems to develop during isolation.

## Science basis
