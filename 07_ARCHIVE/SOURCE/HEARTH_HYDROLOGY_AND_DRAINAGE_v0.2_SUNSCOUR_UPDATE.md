# HEARTH — HYDROLOGY & DRAINAGE
## v0.2 Sunscour Oasis Update

**Status:** WORKING CANON — ADDITIVE REGIONAL DETAIL

This revision preserves the v0.1 continental drainage architecture and adds the first science-derived local water pattern for Sunscour Basin.

## Existing continental rule

Hearth follows:

**mountains/uplands → headwaters → tributaries → rivers → floodplains/wetlands/lakes → deltas/estuaries**

Sunscour remains an **endorheic basin**:

**mountain catchments → seasonal/perennial rivers → inland lake/salt basin → evaporation**

## Sunscour regional hydrology

The basin is now modeled as a groundwater-connected desert rather than uniformly dry terrain.

### Major perennial oasis systems
Five major oasis systems are established as working placements:

1. Northwest mountain-front / Stonehearth transition
2. Northeast Aegir foothill margin
3. Eastern fault-line spring corridor
4. Southern Southward Range foothill margin
5. Southwestern canyon/fan margin

### Secondary systems
Six to ten smaller spring/groundwater systems should occur around:
- alluvial-fan toes;
- fault intersections;
- canyon mouths;
- permeable/impermeable geological contacts;
- seasonal channel crossings;
- basin-margin depressions.

### Seasonal water
Numerous intermittent streams, temporary pools, seasonal lakes, runoff depressions, and post-storm wetlands remain distributed through the basin.

### Basin center
The lowest central areas are dominated by:
- saline lake;
- salt flats/playas;
- mudflats;
- seasonal lake expansion;
- mineral accumulation;
- selected freshwater spring mounds around groundwater discharge zones.

The central basin is not a freshwater oasis field.

## Scientific basis

Desert oases commonly form where groundwater reaches the surface through springs or seeps. Groundwater is frequently recharged in surrounding mountains and redirected by faults, rock permeability contrasts, and basin-fill deposits. USGS studies document these mechanisms in multiple arid basins.

## Human geography consequence

Sunscour's 105,000-person dry-interior allocation remains sparse overall but concentrated around the oasis network.

The expected settlement structure is:

**major oasis → semi-stationary community**

surrounded by:

**seasonal camps → travel corridors → hunting/gathering territories → extreme low-density desert**

This supports a mixed Desert culture of oasis communities and mobile travelers.

## Mapping rule

Do not place oases because a settlement needs one.

Place them where:
**mountain recharge + groundwater flow + basin geology + structural discharge + local terrain**
make them plausible.

People follow the resulting water network.
