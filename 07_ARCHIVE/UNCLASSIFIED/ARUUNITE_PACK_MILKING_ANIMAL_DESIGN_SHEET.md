# ARUUNITE PACK / MILKING ANIMAL DESIGN SHEET
## Fauna Creation Brief — Pre-Agricultural Domestication Candidate

**World:** Aruun  
**People:** Aruunites  
**Status:** OPEN DESIGN — NOT CANON  
**Purpose:** Design one plausible animal that could become useful as a pack/transport animal and/or milk-producing domestic animal. Prefer an existing canonical fauna lineage if suitable.

---

## 1. DESIGN GOAL

Create **one animal** that Aruunites could plausibly domesticate without making it an Earth horse, donkey, cow, goat, sheep, camel, or ox with a new name.

Answer:
- Why would Aruunites domesticate this animal instead of simply hunting it?
- Why does it tolerate humans well enough for domestication?
- What real problem does it solve?

---

## 2. SETTING CONSTRAINTS

### Gravity
Aruun is approximately **1.25g**. Verify skeleton, joints, feet, musculature, gait, center of gravity, and sustainable load accordingly.

### Technology
This is a pre-agricultural / early-domestication setting. Do not assume:
- metal tack;
- wheeled vehicles;
- advanced veterinary medicine;
- industrial feed;
- purpose-built barns;
- intensive agriculture.

Simple ropes, harnesses, fire, shelter, and accumulated animal knowledge are available where appropriate.

### Ecology
Prefer an existing canonical fauna species or a regional variant. Create a new lineage only if the Fauna Function Matrix justifies it.

The current canonical fauna library contains **34 exemplars**; domestication remains an open design problem rather than a predetermined cow/horse analogue. fileciteturn67file7

## 3. DOMESTICATION TYPE

Choose one primary role:

**A — PACK / LOAD ANIMAL**  
Carries food, water, hides, tools, gathered plants, camp equipment, harvested meat.

**B — MILK ANIMAL**  
Produces meaningful milk after reproduction.

**C — DUAL-PURPOSE**  
Useful for both transport and milk.

Prefer C only when biology supports it. Do not force dual-purpose behavior.

---

## 4. BEHAVIORAL REQUIREMENTS

Domestication is primarily a behavioral problem.

Look for:
- naturally social behavior;
- stable group hierarchy;
- tolerance of proximity;
- low panic response;
- predictable movement;
- social learning;
- attachment to familiar individuals;
- ability to habituate to humans;
- willingness to follow a leader;
- manageable defensive behavior.

Avoid animals that are strongly solitary, constantly panicked, impossibly territorial, extremely slow to reproduce, or impossible to feed.

---

## 5. PACK / LOAD REQUIREMENTS

If a pack candidate, determine:

### Size
- shoulder/hip height;
- body length;
- mass;
- useful sustainable load.

### Load
Estimate a **sustained practical load**, not a heroic maximum. Account for 1.25g, terrain, heat, distance, food/water requirements, and recovery.

The animal should carry useful loads without becoming a biological forklift.

### Terrain
State where it performs best:
- grassland;
- woodland;
- mountain;
- desert;
- wetland;
- coast;
- plateau;
- mixed terrain.

### Gait
Describe walking, climbing, endurance, speed, turning ability, and load-bearing gait.

---

## 6. MILK REQUIREMENTS

If it produces milk, determine:
- approximate daily milk volume;
- lactation duration;
- when humans can safely harvest;
- how much must remain for offspring;
- fat/protein character;
- whether it is rich or relatively thin;
- whether it is best fresh, fermented, curdled, dried, or otherwise processed.

Do **not** assume cow-like production.

---

## 7. REPRODUCTION

Determine:
- age of sexual maturity;
- gestation/incubation;
- offspring number;
- offspring size;
- time between births;
- parental care;
- whether young can travel soon after birth;
- whether humans can selectively control breeding.

Domestication is easier when young survive well, breeding intervals are manageable, and humans can select breeding animals.

---

## 8. FEEDING COST

Determine:
- primary diet;
- daily food requirement;
- water requirement;
- grazing/browsing ability;
- competition with humans for important food plants;
- whether humans must cultivate food to maintain it.

**Early domestication should not require agriculture to exist first.**

If it can live on wild grasses, browse, fallen fruit, roots, or other abundant resources, explain why.

---

## 9. WATER

Determine normal water requirement, ability to find water, dry-period tolerance, swimming ability if relevant, and travel limits between water sources.

---

## 10. HUMAN BENEFITS

List useful outputs:
- carrying;
- milk;
- meat;
- hide;
- fiber;
- bone;
- horn/antler;
- dung/fuel;
- traction;
- warning behavior;
- companionship/social value;
- assistance locating water/food;
- predator detection.

Multiple useful outputs are desirable, but do not invent benefits that contradict the biology.

---

## 11. ECOLOGICAL COST

Determine:
- grazing pressure;
- competition with wild herbivores;
- water demand;
- disease transmission;
- invasive potential;
- predator attraction;
- vegetation effects;
- waste/manure effects;
- consequences if domestic animals escape.

Domestication should have ecological consequences.

---

## 12. DISEASE / HUMAN HEALTH

Consider:
- zoonotic disease potential;
- parasites;
- safe proximity;
- milk-processing requirements;
- sanitation;
- predator attraction.

Keep the tradeoffs realistic without making the species a disease machine.

---

## 13. DOMESTICATION PATH

Explain how domestication begins.

A plausible pathway might be:

**repeated association → tolerance → human protection → selective killing/keeping → juvenile handling → managed breeding → recognizable domestic population**

Humans do not need to consciously decide to “domesticate” it at the beginning. Mutual benefit can accumulate gradually.

---

## 14. WHY HUMANS CHOOSE IT

Answer:

- Why this animal?
- Why now?
- Why here?
- What problem does it solve?
- What does it provide that hunting/gathering cannot easily provide?
- Why doesn't every Aruunite population immediately domesticate it?

Regional limits may come from climate, wild range, fodder, disease, terrain, or simply a different local domestication history.

---

## 15. GEOGRAPHIC ORIGIN

- Hearth grassland/woodland;
- Hearth highlands;
- Rift lake/grassland;
- Rift highlands;
- Shattered coastal/interior fragments;
- Lost plateau;
- Lost highlands;
- Lost interior.

**Do not make it globally distributed by default.**

Wild range should determine where domestication can begin.

## 18. CANONICAL FAUNA INTEGRATION

Before inventing a new species, ask:

2. Could an existing animal plausibly become the domestic candidate?

4. Would domestication alter existing predator/prey relationships?
5. Does the candidate interact with existing canonical plants?

# 20. INSECTOID / HONEY-PRODUCING ANIMAL

Create a second domestication candidate **within this same design brief**: an insectoid-type animal that can plausibly provide Aruunites with a honey-like stored sweetener.

This is **not required to be a bee analogue**.

The ecological function is the important part:

**nectar-producing flora → pollinating/nectar-collecting insectoid → stored food/sweetener → human harvesting or management**

The current flora framework already identifies **Goldbloom** as a nectar resource whose pollinator/nectar-collecting animal remains an open fauna-development task. fileciteturn61file0

### Required insectoid questions

Determine:

#### Ecological role
- primary nectar sources;
- pollination role;
- other food sources;
- predators;
- parasites;
- seasonal activity;
- importance to plant reproduction.

#### Social structure
Determine whether it is:
- solitary;
- communal;
- eusocial;
- colony-based;
- caste-based;
- or something evolutionarily different.

Do not assume a queen/worker/drone system merely because bees have one.

#### Nest / colony
Describe:
- nesting location;
- architecture;
- colony size;
- temperature/humidity regulation if relevant;
- food storage;
- reproductive structures;
- defense mechanisms.

#### Stored sweetener
Determine:
- what the animal actually stores;
- how nectar is chemically transformed;
- approximate composition;
- storage life;
- whether it is honey-like, syrup-like, wax-associated, or something entirely different;
- whether humans can safely consume it;
- whether fermentation or processing is useful.

Do not assume Earth honey chemistry if Aruun's ecology suggests another solution.

#### Production
Determine:
- seasonal production;
- approximate colony surplus;
- how much must remain for the colony;
- whether harvesting can occur without killing/destroying the colony;
- whether humans can encourage colonies to occupy suitable nesting sites.

The goal is **harvestable surplus**, not infinite free food.

#### Human relationship
Explain the likely progression:

**wild harvesting → tolerance/avoidance → protection of productive colonies → managed nesting sites → selective management**

Full domestication is optional.

Aruunites may instead develop a relationship closer to **managed wild colonies**.

#### Defensive behavior
Determine:
- sting/bite/spray/chemical defense or another mechanism;
- defensive threshold;
- colony defense radius;
- whether smoke, water, plant compounds, clothing, or other simple techniques can reduce danger.

Humans should have to earn the sweetener.

#### Flight and 1.25g
Explicitly evaluate:
- body size;
- wing structure;
- wing loading;
- flight endurance;
- maneuverability;
- thermal effects;
- whether the insectoid flies at all.

**1.25g must matter.**

Do not simply scale an Earth bee upward.

#### Human value
List:
- sweetener;
- pollination;
- wax/resin/fiber or other useful products if biologically justified;
- medicinal/chemical products if justified;
- cultural significance;
- ecological knowledge.

#### Ecological consequences
Determine:
- effects of human harvesting;
- effects on wild pollination;
- competition with other pollinators;
- colony displacement;
- invasive potential;
- predator relationships;
- consequences of large-scale management.

#### Geographic distribution
Identify where the wild insectoid occurs and where humans can realistically manage it.

Explain why some Aruunite populations may never develop honey harvesting.

#### Regional variation
Determine whether regional variants are warranted by:
- climate;
- nectar availability;
- predators;
- nesting habitat;
- seasonal cycles.

Do not create variants unless the ecology produces meaningful differences.

# 22. HONEY-SYSTEM QUALITY TEST

Before accepting the insectoid, ask:

> **Could the insectoid exist and thrive without humans?**

> **Does its stored food make sense as an evolved colony resource?**

> **Does Goldbloom benefit from its existence?**

> **Would humans realistically risk harvesting it?**

> **Can harvesting happen without destroying the colony?**

> **Does 1.25g meaningfully affect its anatomy or behavior?**

> **Is it more interesting than simply calling it an Aruunite bee?**

If the answer to the last question is no, redesign it.

The goal is an **ecologically necessary insectoid that happens to create a human-valuable food resource**, not an insect invented solely to give Aruunites honey.


## 23. QUALITY TEST

Before accepting the animal:

> **Would a prehistoric Aruunite look at this animal and think, “That could make my life easier enough to be worth the trouble”?**

Also:

> **Could this animal exist without humans?**

And:

> **Does it solve a real problem without solving every problem?**

If it does everything, redesign it.

The goal is **a useful animal with limits**, not a fantasy super-beast.
