## Critical Worldbuilding Principle

Hearth peoples are **prehistoric survival societies** in a world where animals and environmental hazards are substantially more dangerous than on Earth. Their social systems should be derived from those conditions rather than importing modern/historical human institutions by default.

Cooperation is not presented as moral perfection. It is practical survival. Theft, rape, murder, organized warfare, etc. are not normal social institutions in these communities; deliberately destroying the people you depend upon is usually self-defeating. Conflict and disagreements can still happen, but do not assume modern crime/war structures without a reason.

**Nature is the primary adversary.** Numbers, cooperation, shared knowledge, and ecological competence are major survival tools.

## River — Current Canon

River is the current culture being completed.

Established:

- River people live around major rivers/floodplains and use the entire surrounding ecosystem, not merely the water.
- The river is their primary resource/network and is the reason they are there, but it is also dangerous.
- They fish, hunt, harvest wild resources, and practice **wild farming/management** rather than conventional agriculture.
- They maintain a defined settlement territory in which they farm/manage wild resources, harvest, fish, etc.
- Productive survival knowledge is not normally kept secret; there is little point in hoarding information that helps everyone survive.
- River communities are generally semi-sedentary/repeated-use settlements consistent with the existing Hearth population/geography model.
- Existing population material places ~375,000 Hearth people in river valleys/floodplains (25% of Hearth's ~1.5M population).
- General Hearth residential baseline: roughly 80–150 people, with smaller groups and larger seasonal aggregations where appropriate.
- River households are roughly 10–20 people and may include extended kin.
- Bilateral kinship is established; children connect to both parents' families.
- River kin extends along the river corridor, including upstream/downstream relationships and broader reciprocal support.
- Partnership/marriage: individuals have meaningful choice; either partner can refuse; marriage follows river-corridor networks; couples may remain, move, or establish a new household.
- **Leadership:** each River village has a **village chief**. The previous chief selects their successor.
- There is **generally no larger River government** above the village level.
- Neighboring River settlements may know/interact with each other through the river network, but this is not continent-wide political/cultural integration.
- Long-distance travel is difficult and uncommon; walking is often the only practical option and is dangerous.
- **Dispute resolution:** the chief primarily **mediates** serious disputes. The chief hears/facilitates rather than simply imposing a ruling; the community works toward resolution.
- **Death practice:** River people use **floating graves** — small rafts carrying the dead, decorated with flowers or similar offerings, released onto the river. This should be developed into a distinctive but practical/culturally appropriate tradition without overcomplicating it.
- **River worldview — CANON:** the river is necessary, powerful, unpredictable, and dangerous rather than simply “safe.” River people understand themselves as part of the ecosystem rather than masters of it. Their core survival philosophy is based on knowing the water, knowing the land, and knowing their people. Survival knowledge is shared. Numbers and cooperation allow people to survive dangers that an individual could not. A useful cultural principle is: **“The river takes the lone.”** This expresses the idea that isolation makes a person vulnerable and that survival depends on remaining part of the community.

## Important River Clarification

When asking about River subsistence, do NOT ask whether they use the river versus the land as if those are mutually exclusive. They are river people because the river is their primary resource and connective system, but they necessarily use the floodplain and surrounding wilderness because this is a prehistoric world full of large predators and dangerous animals.

## Canonical Repository Naming Rule

A repository rule was added at:

`TOOLS/REPOSITORY/RULES/DOCUMENT_NAMING_STATUS_CODES.md`

Use:

`[SCOPE]_[SUBJECT]_[STATUS]-[ID].md`

Example:

`RIVER_FAMILY_C-0001.md`

Status codes:

- `C` = CANON
- `P` = PROVISIONAL
- `O` = OPEN
- `X` = CONFLICTED
- `D` = DEPRECATED

**One subject = one active canonical file.** Git history is the revision history. Do not create `v2`, `FINAL`, `UPDATED`, etc. for ordinary revisions.

If an existing file cannot safely be edited, do not silently create a competing duplicate.
