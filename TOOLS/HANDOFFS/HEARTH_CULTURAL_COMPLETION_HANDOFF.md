## Current Priority: River

River is the current culture being completed because its existing material was less fully assembled than the other Hearth peoples.

Already established:

- River people live around major river/floodplain systems.
- The river is their **primary resource**, but they use the entire surrounding ecosystem because this is a harsh prehistoric world.
- They fish, hunt, harvest wild resources, and practice **wild farming/management**, not conventional agriculture.
- Settlement territories are recognized areas used for fishing, harvesting, wild farming/management, hunting, etc.
- Productive knowledge is not normally kept secret; survival depends on shared knowledge and cooperation.
- River households are extended households, approximately 10–20 people.
- Bilateral kinship and broader river-corridor/“river kin” relationships are established.
- Partnership/marriage material is already established in the existing Hearth family documents.
- Normal community scale and seasonal aggregation patterns are established in the population/settlement material.
- **Each River village has a chief.**
- The **previous chief selects the successor**.
- There is **generally no larger River government** above individual villages.
- The chief's authority is primarily village-level.
- For serious disputes, the chief **mediates rather than simply imposing a ruling**; involved people are heard and the community works toward resolution.
- River settlements know and interact with neighboring River communities, but this does not imply continent-wide familiarity.
- Long-distance travel is difficult and uncommon; walking is often the only practical option and the environment is dangerous.
- River society is fundamentally cooperative because survival is difficult. Theft, rape, murder, and organized war are not normal cultural institutions. The world is already dangerous enough without people intentionally destroying the people they depend upon.
- River death practice: **floating graves** — small rafts carrying the dead, decorated with flowers/offerings and released onto the river. This should be developed into a distinctive but not overcomplicated cultural practice.

### River worldview — CANON

The user explicitly approved this as **canon**:

> River people do not consider the river safe; they consider it necessary. The river provides water, food, transportation, floodplain resources, plants, animals, and connection, but it also contains dangers. They understand themselves as part of the ecosystem rather than masters of it.
>
> Their practical worldview is built around knowing the water, knowing the land, and knowing their people. Cooperation is not treated as an abstract moral achievement; it is common survival sense. Numbers allow them to survive dangers that an individual cannot.
>
> Survival knowledge is shared rather than hoarded. Useful discoveries about food, hazards, seasons, safe routes, predators, or productive areas are taught to others because withholding survival knowledge endangers the community.
>
> A useful cultural expression is **“The river takes the lone.”** This captures the idea that individuals survive by remaining part of the group. The underlying principle is canon; the exact phrase may remain a cultural saying/working expression unless later revised.

## Important Worldbuilding Principle

Do not import human institutions automatically.

Aruun is a harsh prehistoric world. Social structures should emerge from survival conditions, ecology, resource availability, population density, travel difficulty, predators/prey, and mutual dependence.

The people are not magically morally superior; their environment heavily rewards cooperation and makes destructive antisocial behavior costly. Do not assume modern concepts of crime, bureaucracy, warfare, property, or political hierarchy unless the world gives a reason for them.

Also: not every geographic area needs its own named people. We want enough cultural anchors for readers/licensees to understand the world while deliberately leaving substantial space for future peoples, settlements, and creator-developed content.

## File / Repository Rules

### One subject = one active canonical file.

Do **not** create `v2`, `FINAL`, `UPDATED`, etc. files for ordinary revisions. Git history is the version history.

**Search before creating.** If the subject already exists at the same scope, update the existing canonical file.

If an existing file cannot safely be edited, **do not silently create a competing copy**.

Supporting documents must be clearly distinguishable from authoritative canon. Deprecated material must not compete with current canon.

The repository rule for this is:

`TOOLS/REPOSITORY/RULES/CANONICAL_DOCUMENT_MAINTENANCE.md`

### Naming/status convention

New/normalized documents use the status/ID convention described in:

`TOOLS/REPOSITORY/RULES/DOCUMENT_NAMING_STATUS_CODES.md`

Core status codes:

- `C` = CANON
- `P` = PROVISIONAL
- `O` = OPEN
- `X` = CONFLICTED
- `D` = DEPRECATED

Example:

`RIVER_FAMILY_C-0001.md`

The ID identifies the record; it is **not a version number**.

The document should also carry its scope/status metadata internally so CORE can identify it even outside its normal directory.

Existing files should not be duplicated solely to conform to the new naming convention. Normalize intentionally during edits or a dedicated cleanup pass.

## CORE / Knowledge-System Context

The larger project is developing a generalized AI memory/retention/recall system called **CORE**. ARUUN is being used as a demanding real-world knowledge-base test case.

The earlier blind-test work explored whether a system could distinguish:

- broad vs narrow scope;
- continent vs region;
- regional vs settlement information;
- family vs leadership;
- birth vs leadership;
- supporting documents vs authoritative regional documents;
- same-region vs different-region information;
- people-focused vs area-focused information;
- conflicting/variant information;
- document-of-context relationships.

This is why consistent file organization and explicit authority/status metadata matter.

CORE should ultimately be able to skim context, identify what it knows, identify what is missing, ask useful questions, infer only where justified, recognize uncertainty, loop through competing hypotheses, and reach conclusions without fabricating information.

For ARUUN work, however, **human canon decisions remain authoritative**.
